# Content Mapping Framework

Content mapping is the core discipline of the Content activity. It connects what you know about buyers (personas, journey, problems) to the content you create. Every piece of content should be traceable to a specific persona, at a specific journey stage, addressing a specific question or need.

## The Content Map Structure

The content map is a persona × journey stage matrix. For each cell, you define what content is needed and why.

### Matrix Dimensions

**Rows: Buyer Personas**
List personas in priority order. For most organizations, start with 2–4 key personas:
- The Economic Decision Maker (EDM) — controls budget, approves investment
- The Lead Evaluator — drives the research and comparison process
- The Technical Buyer — assesses integration, security, compliance
- The Champion / Internal Advocate — builds the internal case for change

Your organization may use different persona labels. The critical thing is that content addresses each persona's distinct concerns, language, and information needs.

**Columns: Buyer Journey Stages**
Map to your documented buyer experience. The standard five-stage model:

1. **Awareness** — Buyer recognizes a problem or opportunity
2. **Education** — Buyer investigates the problem space and possible approaches
3. **Selection** — Buyer evaluates specific vendors and solutions
4. **Purchase** — Buyer makes the final decision, negotiates, and completes procurement
5. **Loyalty** — Buyer validates the decision, expands usage, or advocates

## Stage-by-Stage Content Guidance

### Awareness Stage

**Buyer mindset:** "Something isn't working" or "There might be a better way"

**Content objective:** Help the buyer name and understand their problem. Establish your organization as a knowledgeable, trustworthy voice in the space. Do NOT pitch your product at this stage.

**Buyer questions to address:**
- Am I the only one facing this problem?
- How big is this problem, really?
- What's the cost of doing nothing?
- Who else is thinking about this?

**Recommended content types:**
- Thought leadership articles and blog posts
- Industry research and benchmark reports
- Infographics highlighting market trends or problem prevalence
- Podcasts and video interviews with industry experts
- Social media content that sparks problem recognition
- Educational webinars on the problem space

**Tone and approach:** Empathetic, educational, problem-focused. Lead with the buyer's world, not your product. Use the buyer's language, not internal terminology. Reference market data and third-party validation.

**Distribution channels:** Organic search (SEO), social media, industry publications, syndication partners, paid media for amplification.

**Key metrics:** Reach, impressions, new visitor traffic, content engagement (time on page, scroll depth), social shares, brand awareness lift.

### Education Stage

**Buyer mindset:** "What approaches exist to solve this problem?"

**Content objective:** Educate the buyer about solution categories and approaches. Position your approach as a credible option without hard-selling. Begin to differentiate your perspective.

**Buyer questions to address:**
- What are the different ways to solve this problem?
- What are the trade-offs between approaches?
- What should I look for in a solution?
- What have others in my situation done?

**Recommended content types:**
- Solution category guides ("The Definitive Guide to [approach]")
- Comparison frameworks (approach A vs. approach B — not vendor A vs. vendor B yet)
- Customer stories framed around the journey, not the product
- Interactive assessments or diagnostic tools
- Analyst reports and third-party evaluations
- Educational email sequences
- How-to guides and best practice resources

**Tone and approach:** Authoritative and balanced. Show you understand the full landscape, not just your corner of it. This builds credibility and trust. Introduce your distinctive competencies naturally — your unique perspective on the problem, not product features.

**Distribution channels:** Email nurture, content hubs, webinars, analyst relations, SEO for solution-category terms, retargeting.

**Key metrics:** Lead generation (form fills, subscriptions), content downloads, email engagement, return visits, time on site, lead scoring progression.

### Selection Stage

**Buyer mindset:** "Which specific solution and vendor is right for me?"

**Content objective:** Help the buyer evaluate your solution against alternatives. Demonstrate fit with their specific needs. Overcome objections. Build confidence in your ability to deliver.

**Buyer questions to address:**
- How does this product specifically solve my problem?
- How does it compare to alternatives I'm considering?
- What does implementation look like?
- What results have similar organizations achieved?
- What are the risks?

**Recommended content types:**
- Product demos and interactive tours
- Detailed case studies with quantified results
- Technical white papers and architecture documents
- ROI calculators and business case builders
- Competitive comparison sheets (fact-based, not disparaging)
- Reference customer connections
- Buyer's guides and evaluation checklists
- Free trials, sandboxes, or proof-of-concept programs

**Tone and approach:** Specific, evidence-based, and confident. This is where product capabilities meet buyer needs. Use the positioning framework: lead with the problem, connect to your solution, differentiate from alternatives, prove with evidence. Speak the persona's language — the technical buyer wants different details than the economic buyer.

**Distribution channels:** Sales handoff, product website, email nurture (bottom of funnel), retargeting, review sites, direct outreach.

**Key metrics:** SQLs generated, demo requests, trial signups, opportunity creation, content influence on pipeline, sales feedback on asset usefulness.

### Purchase Stage

**Buyer mindset:** "How do I justify, finalize, and execute this decision?"

**Content objective:** Remove friction from the final decision. Help the buyer build the internal business case. Address procurement, legal, and compliance requirements.

**Buyer questions to address:**
- How do I justify this investment to my leadership?
- What does the implementation timeline look like?
- What are the contractual terms and SLAs?
- What support and onboarding do we get?
- What are the risks of choosing this vendor?

**Recommended content types:**
- Business case templates and ROI documentation
- Implementation guides and success plans
- Security and compliance documentation
- Service level agreements and support tiers
- Executive summary documents for C-suite reviewers
- Customer references matched to the buyer's industry and size
- Procurement-friendly feature and pricing comparisons

**Tone and approach:** Reassuring, professional, and thorough. The buyer has largely decided in your favor — now they need ammunition to convince others and confidence that execution will go smoothly. Reduce perceived risk at every turn.

**Distribution channels:** Sales-delivered, customer success handoff, partner portals, secure document sharing.

**Key metrics:** Close rate, time to close, deal size, discount rates, content usage in closed-won deals.

### Loyalty Stage

**Buyer mindset:** "Am I getting the value I expected? Should I expand or recommend this?"

**Content objective:** Validate the buyer's decision. Accelerate time to value. Create advocates who expand their usage and refer others.

**Buyer questions to address:**
- Am I using this effectively?
- What results should I expect by now?
- What else can this product do for me?
- Who else in my organization or network would benefit?

**Recommended content types:**
- Onboarding guides and quick-start resources
- Best practice playbooks
- Customer community content and forums
- Product update announcements and roadmap previews
- User conferences and customer events
- Advocacy programs (referral incentives, review programs)
- Cross-sell and upsell education
- Customer success stories (created with advocates)

**Tone and approach:** Supportive, celebratory, and forward-looking. Reinforce the buyer's decision. Highlight early wins. Connect them with a community of peers. Make it easy and rewarding to become an advocate.

**Distribution channels:** Customer success programs, in-product messaging, customer email programs, community platforms, user events.

**Key metrics:** NPS, customer satisfaction, product adoption rates, expansion revenue, referrals generated, advocacy participation, case studies produced.

## Per-Persona Content Adaptation

The same journey stage requires different content for different personas. Here's how content varies:

### Economic Decision Maker
- **What they care about:** Business outcomes, ROI, risk, competitive advantage, strategic alignment
- **Content preferences:** Executive summaries, business cases, ROI analyses, peer benchmarks
- **Format preferences:** Concise — one-pagers, executive briefs, short videos
- **Language:** Business and financial terms, strategic vocabulary

### Lead Evaluator
- **What they care about:** Comprehensive evaluation, stakeholder alignment, selection justification
- **Content preferences:** Detailed guides, comparison matrices, evaluation criteria, case studies
- **Format preferences:** Thorough — guides, whitepapers, interactive assessments
- **Language:** Domain-specific, process-oriented

### Technical Buyer
- **What they care about:** Integration, architecture, security, performance, compliance
- **Content preferences:** Technical documentation, API references, security whitepapers, architecture diagrams
- **Format preferences:** Detailed and technical — specs, documentation, sandbox access
- **Language:** Technical terminology, precision, specifications

### Champion / Internal Advocate
- **What they care about:** Building the internal case, overcoming objections, looking credible to peers
- **Content preferences:** Internal presentation templates, quick wins documentation, stakeholder-specific talking points
- **Format preferences:** Shareable, presentable — slides, one-pagers, short videos
- **Language:** Persuasive, outcome-focused, easy to reuse

## Content Format Selection Guide

Choose formats based on the intersection of persona preference and journey stage:

| Format | Best For Stages | Best For Personas | Production Effort |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Blog posts | Awareness, Education | All | Low |
| Whitepapers / Ebooks | Education, Selection | Lead Evaluator, Technical | Medium-High |
| Case studies | Selection, Purchase | EDM, Lead Evaluator | Medium |
| Videos (short) | Awareness, Education | All | Medium |
| Videos (demo) | Selection | Lead Evaluator, Technical | Medium-High |
| Infographics | Awareness | All | Low-Medium |
| Interactive tools | Education, Selection | Lead Evaluator | High |
| Executive briefs | Selection, Purchase | EDM | Low-Medium |
| Technical docs | Selection | Technical Buyer | Medium-High |
| Email sequences | Education, Selection | All | Medium |
| Webinars | Education, Selection | Lead Evaluator, Champion | Medium |
| One-pagers | All stages | All | Low |
| ROI calculators | Purchase | EDM, Champion | High |
| Presentation templates | Purchase | Champion | Low-Medium |

## Content Reuse and Repurposing

Strong content strategy maximizes the value of every asset through deliberate repurposing:

- **Core to derivative:** Start with a comprehensive asset (whitepaper, research report) and derive smaller assets (blog posts, infographics, social snippets, email content)
- **Stage adaptation:** Adapt the same core message for different journey stages (awareness-level blog post → selection-level technical brief)
- **Persona adaptation:** Reframe the same case study for different persona perspectives (business outcomes version for EDM, technical implementation version for Technical Buyer)
- **Format adaptation:** Convert a webinar into a blog post, video clips, an infographic, and a slide deck
- **Channel adaptation:** Modify content for different distribution channels (long-form for website, summary for social, key points for email)

Plan repurposing at creation time, not after the fact. When briefing a content asset, identify 3–5 derivative pieces that can be produced from the same research and interviews.

## Maintaining the Content Map

The content map is a living document. Update it:
- When new content is published (add to the appropriate cell)
- When content is retired (remove from the matrix)
- When personas change (add or modify rows)
- When the buyer journey is updated (adjust columns)
- When audit results reveal quality issues (flag underperforming assets)
- When new competitive pressures emerge (check for gaps in Selection stage)

Review the complete content map quarterly with stakeholders from marketing, sales, product, and customer success.
