# Content Audit Methodology

A content audit answers three questions: What do we have? How well does it perform? Where are the gaps? This systematic inventory and assessment is the foundation for strategic content planning.

## Phase 1: Content Inventory

Catalog every existing content asset. Cast a wide net — content lives in more places than most organizations realize.

### Where to Look
- Website (blog posts, landing pages, resource library, product pages)
- Sales collateral (decks, one-pagers, brochures, data sheets)
- Email campaigns (nurture sequences, newsletters, outbound templates)
- Social media (posts, videos, infographics)
- Gated assets (whitepapers, ebooks, guides, reports)
- Video library (demos, webinars, tutorials, testimonials)
- Case studies and customer stories
- Internal documents that could be externalized (technical briefs, FAQs)
- Partner and channel materials
- Event presentations and speaking decks
- Press releases and media coverage
- Community content (forums, user groups, Q&A)

### Inventory Fields

For each asset, capture:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Asset ID** | Unique identifier for tracking |
| **Title** | Name of the content piece |
| **Format** | Blog post, whitepaper, video, infographic, etc. |
| **URL / Location** | Where the asset lives |
| **Creation Date** | When it was originally created |
| **Last Updated** | When it was most recently revised |
| **Owner** | Who is responsible for maintaining it |
| **Target Persona** | Which buyer persona it addresses (if identifiable) |
| **Journey Stage** | Which buying stage it serves (if identifiable) |
| **Positioning Pillar** | Which messaging theme it reinforces (if identifiable) |
| **Topic / Theme** | The subject matter |
| **Call to Action** | What the reader is asked to do next |
| **Gated?** | Whether the asset requires form submission |
| **Performance Data** | Available metrics (views, downloads, shares, conversions) |

### Inventory Tips
- Start with a spreadsheet — don't over-engineer the tool
- Assign the audit to someone who can be objective, not the content creator
- Include content that may be outdated or underperforming — the goal is completeness
- If the inventory is large (100+ assets), prioritize by recency and traffic first, then work backward

## Phase 2: Quality Assessment

Evaluate each asset (or a representative sample for large inventories) against these criteria.

### Assessment Criteria

Score each criterion 1–5 (1 = poor, 5 = excellent):

**Relevance** — Does this content address a real buyer problem? Is the problem still current and urgent? Would the target persona recognize their situation in this content?

**Accuracy** — Is the information factually correct? Are product claims current? Are statistics and data points up to date? Does it reflect the current competitive landscape?

**Alignment** — Does this content reinforce established positioning? Does it use buyer language (not internal jargon)? Is it consistent with other content in the library?

**Quality** — Is it well-written, well-designed, and professionally produced? Does it reflect the brand standards? Would you be proud to share it with a prospect?

**Effectiveness** — Based on available performance data, does this content achieve its intended purpose? Does it generate engagement, conversions, or sales feedback?

### Assessment Categories

Based on the scoring, classify each asset:

- **Keep** (avg. score 4–5) — Strong asset, maintain and promote
- **Refresh** (avg. score 3–3.9) — Solid foundation but needs updating
- **Rewrite** (avg. score 2–2.9) — Concept is valid but execution needs significant work
- **Retire** (avg. score 1–1.9) — Remove from circulation, does more harm than good
- **Repurpose** — Content that scores well on some criteria but serves the wrong persona or stage; can be adapted rather than replaced

## Phase 3: Gap Analysis

The gap analysis is the most strategically valuable output of the audit. It reveals where the content library fails to support the buyer journey.

### Building the Content Matrix

Create a grid with:
- **Rows** = Buyer personas (primary persona first, then secondary)
- **Columns** = Buyer journey stages (Awareness → Education → Selection → Purchase → Loyalty)

In each cell, list:
1. Existing assets that serve this persona × stage combination
2. Their quality assessment rating
3. Whether any asset is designated as the "primary" piece for this cell

### Identifying Gaps

A gap exists when:
- **Empty cell** — No content exists for a persona × stage combination
- **Low-quality cell** — Content exists but scored poorly in assessment
- **Format gap** — Content exists in one format but the persona prefers another (e.g., text-only content for a persona that prefers video)
- **Channel gap** — Content exists but isn't distributed where the persona looks for information
- **Freshness gap** — Content is more than 12–18 months old and hasn't been validated for accuracy
- **Depth gap** — Surface-level content where the persona needs detailed, technical information (or vice versa)

### Gap Prioritization

Not all gaps are equally urgent. Prioritize using these factors:

1. **Journey stage criticality** — Gaps in the Selection and Education stages typically have the most direct impact on pipeline and revenue
2. **Persona priority** — Gaps for primary buyer personas matter more than gaps for secondary personas
3. **Sales feedback** — If sales reports frequently needing content that doesn't exist, that gap is urgent
4. **Competitive pressure** — If competitors have strong content where you have none, the gap is visible to buyers
5. **Program timing** — Gaps that affect upcoming campaigns or launches need immediate attention

### Gap Analysis Summary

Produce a prioritized list of content gaps with:
- Persona and journey stage
- Description of what's needed
- Why it matters (the business impact of the gap)
- Estimated effort to fill (quick win, moderate project, major investment)
- Recommended format and channel
- Priority ranking (P1 / P2 / P3)

## Audit Frequency

Content audits are not one-time events. Recommended cadence:

- **Full inventory audit**: Annually
- **Quality spot-checks**: Quarterly (sample 20–25% of the library)
- **Gap analysis refresh**: Semi-annually or whenever positioning, personas, or the competitive landscape changes significantly
- **New asset classification**: Ongoing — every new asset should be tagged with persona, stage, and pillar at creation time

## Common Audit Mistakes

- **Skipping the inventory** — Jumping straight to gap analysis without knowing what exists leads to duplicate creation and wasted effort
- **Auditing in isolation** — The audit should involve sales, customer success, and product stakeholders, not just marketing
- **Ignoring the "dark library"** — Sales teams often create their own content outside the official library; include those assets
- **Assessment without data** — Where performance data exists, use it; gut-feel assessments alone are insufficient
- **Audit without action** — An audit that doesn't lead to a prioritized content plan is wasted effort; always connect findings to next steps
