# Input Validation Checklist

Before developing content, validate that the strategic foundation exists. Content built without these inputs risks being inside-out, unfocused, or misaligned with market reality.

## Priority 1: Must-Have Inputs

These are non-negotiable. Without them, content work is guesswork.

### Positioning Document
- [ ] Clear articulation of the market problem(s) the product solves
- [ ] Value proposition stated in buyer language (not product jargon)
- [ ] Messaging pillars — the 3–5 core themes that anchor all communication
- [ ] Differentiation statements vs. competitive alternatives
- [ ] Per-persona positioning variants (if available)

**If missing:** Help the user draft a lightweight positioning statement using this structure: "For [persona] who [problem], [product] is [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternative], we [differentiator]." This is a working approximation, not a substitute for full positioning work.

### Buyer Personas
- [ ] At least one primary buyer persona defined
- [ ] Persona includes: role, responsibilities, goals, challenges, information sources, and decision criteria
- [ ] Persona uses buyer language (validated through market interviews, not internal assumptions)
- [ ] Clear distinction between buyer personas and user personas

**If missing:** Help the user sketch a minimum viable persona: role title, top 3 problems, how they currently solve those problems, what information sources they trust, and who else influences their buying decision.

### Buyer Experience / Journey Map
- [ ] Documented stages the buyer goes through from problem recognition to purchase decision
- [ ] Key questions the buyer asks at each stage
- [ ] Barriers or friction points at each stage
- [ ] Information needs at each stage
- [ ] Channels and touchpoints the buyer uses at each stage

**If missing:** Use this simplified journey framework as a starting point:
1. **Awareness** — "I have a problem"
2. **Education** — "What approaches exist to solve this?"
3. **Selection** — "Which specific solution is right for me?"
4. **Purchase** — "How do I justify and complete this decision?"
5. **Loyalty** — "Am I getting the value I expected?"

Work with the user to flesh out buyer questions and information needs for at least stages 1–4.

## Priority 2: Valuable Inputs

These significantly improve content quality but can be developed in parallel.

### Use Scenarios
- [ ] Stories that illustrate market problems in context
- [ ] Day-in-the-life narratives showing how the problem manifests
- [ ] Before/after scenarios showing impact of solving the problem

**Value for content:** Use scenarios provide raw narrative material for case studies, blog posts, and scenario-based content. They ground content in real-world situations rather than abstract claims.

### Competitive Landscape
- [ ] Key competitors and alternative approaches identified
- [ ] Strengths and weaknesses assessed per competitor
- [ ] Differentiation strategy articulated — when to fight, when to position around

**Value for content:** Competitive intelligence informs comparison content, objection-handling assets, and "why us" materials. Essential for Selection-stage content.

### Marketing Plan
- [ ] Upcoming programs and campaigns with dates and objectives
- [ ] Target segments and their relative priority
- [ ] Budget and resource allocation for content production
- [ ] Key events, launches, or milestones on the calendar

**Value for content:** The marketing plan determines content priorities and timelines. Without it, content work lacks urgency and sequencing.

### Win/Loss Insights
- [ ] Patterns from recent wins — what resonated, what content was useful
- [ ] Patterns from recent losses — where messaging failed, what content was missing
- [ ] Buying process insights — how buyers actually made their decision

**Value for content:** Win/loss data is the most direct feedback on whether content is working. It reveals what buyers actually care about vs. what you think they care about.

## Validation Assessment

After reviewing available inputs, classify the user's readiness:

**Green — Ready to proceed:** At least positioning, one buyer persona, and a buyer journey map are available. Content work can proceed with confidence.

**Yellow — Proceed with caution:** One or two Priority 1 inputs are weak or incomplete. Create lightweight approximations and flag assumptions that need validation. Content produced will need revision as inputs mature.

**Red — Foundation first:** Multiple Priority 1 inputs are missing. Recommend the user invest in upstream work before committing significant content resources. Help them create minimum viable versions of the most critical inputs so they can begin preliminary content planning.

## Working with Incomplete Inputs

Even in Yellow or Red states, some content work is productive:

- **Audit existing content** — You can inventory and assess what exists regardless of input maturity
- **Map known gaps** — Even rough personas and journey stages reveal obvious holes
- **Create foundational pieces** — Problem-focused thought leadership content (awareness stage) requires less precision than selection-stage content
- **Interview stakeholders** — Sales, customer success, and product teams hold implicit knowledge about personas and buyer questions that can bootstrap content planning

The key principle: document your assumptions explicitly. When upstream inputs are incomplete, every content decision rests on assumptions that should be stated and tracked for future validation.
