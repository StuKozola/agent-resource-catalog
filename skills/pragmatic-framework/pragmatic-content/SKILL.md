# Content — Pragmatic Framework (2027)

## Where this fits in the 2027 Pragmatic Framework

- **Category:** Enablement — *Can the people around the product make it succeed?*
- **Focus-area question:** *What do buyers and users need to read, watch or hear to decide and succeed?*
- **Position:** Below the bar (product- and team-focused)
- **Other focus areas in this category:** Adoption, Assets, Channel Support

## What Content Covers

Content is the material that communicates value, guides decisions and supports use: articles, blog posts, videos, webinars, white papers, guides, how-to and training material, and more. Good content builds trust and helps people at **every stage of buying and adoption** — not only buyers choosing a product, but also users learning to get value from it after the sale.

Content is a collaboration. **Product marketers** supply insight into buyers, users and their problems; **content specialists** (writers, designers, video producers, technical writers) turn that insight into material that reaches the right people at the right time. Neither does it well alone.

### Relationship to Other Focus Areas

**Content draws from:**
- **Positioning** (`pragmatic-positioning`, Planning) — value propositions and messaging pillars
- **Buyer Personas** and **User Personas** (`pragmatic-buyer-personas`, `pragmatic-user-personas`, Planning) — the language, concerns and information needs of people who decide and people who use
- **Buyer Journey** (`pragmatic-buyer-journey`, Planning) — stages, questions and friction points that say when content is needed
- **Requirements** (`pragmatic-requirements`, Planning) — use scenarios that put problems in context
- **Market Problems** and **Competitive Landscape** (Market) — the problems to address and the alternatives to compare against
- **Marketing Plan** (`pragmatic-marketing-plan`, Go-to-Market) — programs and goals content must support

**Content feeds:**
- **Awareness** and **Engagement** (`pragmatic-awareness`, `pragmatic-engagement`, Go-to-Market) — programs that deliver content to buyers
- **Adoption** (`pragmatic-adoption`, Enablement) — onboarding, how-to and training content for users
- **Assets** (`pragmatic-assets`, Enablement) — content adapted into sales and support tools
- **Channel Support** (`pragmatic-channel-support`, Enablement) — content partners use to position, sell, deliver and support
- **Organizational Alignment** (`pragmatic-organizational-alignment`, Go-to-Market) — shared messaging across internal teams
- **Launch** (`pragmatic-launch`, Go-to-Market) — launch-ready content

**Content vs. Assets:** Content informs and educates audiences directly (articles, videos, guides). Assets are the tools teams use to sell and support (decks, demos, battle cards, ROI calculators, case studies). Content often becomes raw material for assets.

## Workflow

Adapt based on where the user is — some start from scratch, others need to audit and fill gaps.

### Step 1: Validate Strategic Inputs
> Load `references/input-validation.md`.

**Must-have:** positioning (or at least a value proposition and pillars), primary buyer persona, buyer journey map (even rough). **For adoption content:** user personas and a definition of meaningful use. **Valuable:** use scenarios, competitive landscape, marketing plan, win/loss insights. If inputs are missing, build lightweight working versions; content without them drifts inside-out.

### Step 2: Audit Existing Content
> Load `references/content-audit.md`.

Answer: what do we have, how well does it perform, and where are the gaps? Include customer-facing help, training and onboarding material, not only marketing content. Output a coverage matrix across personas and stages.

### Step 3: Map Content to Buying and Adoption Stages
> Load `references/content-mapping.md`.

For each persona and stage, ask: "What does this person need to know, believe, feel or be able to do at this stage to move forward?"

| Stage | Who | Typical need |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Buyers | Recognize and name the problem |
| Education | Buyers | Understand approaches |
| Selection | Buyers, evaluators, trial users | Compare options; see fit |
| Purchase | Buyers, approvers | Justify and complete the decision |
| Onboarding and Adoption | Users, admins | Set up and reach meaningful use |
| Loyalty and Expansion | Customers, users | Get more value; renew, expand, recommend |

For each cell define the key questions, content objective, formats, channels and success measures.

### Step 4: Develop Content Briefs
> Use `templates/content-brief.md`.

Tie each asset to its persona, stage, positioning pillar (or, for adoption content, the user task), the question it answers and the action it should drive.

### Step 5: Build the Calendar and Production Plan
> Use `templates/content-plan.md`.

Prioritize by gap severity, program and launch needs, adoption blockers (where users stall), reuse potential and production capacity. Name who supplies insight and who produces for each item.

### Step 6: Measure and Iterate
> Load `references/content-measurement.md`.

Measure at three levels: asset (engagement, consumption), journey (progression between buying stages; activation and continued use for adoption content) and business (influenced pipeline, revenue, support deflection, retention).

## Internal products

For internal platforms, content is mostly adoption content: docs, getting-started guides, migration guides, examples, recorded demos and changelogs. The "buyers" are the leads who approve moving to the platform; give them short decision content (why, cost of switching, support model) alongside the user material.

## Output Formats

- **Content Audit Report** — inventory, quality scores, gap analysis
- **Content Map / Matrix** — persona × stage grid of needs, existing content and gaps
- **Content Brief** — specification for a single piece
- **Content Calendar / Production Plan** — prioritized schedule with owners
- **Content Performance Dashboard** — measures and cadence

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Inside-out content** — features instead of buyer problems or user tasks.
2. **Buyer-only content** — nothing that helps users succeed after the sale, so adoption stalls.
3. **Persona-blind or stage-blind content** — every piece should trace to a persona and a stage.
4. **Content without positioning roots** — inconsistent messaging across assets.
5. **Volume over value** — a small, well-targeted library beats a large unfocused one.
6. **Create-and-forget** — no measurement, no updates as the product and market change.
7. **Sales and CS disconnect** — content marketing likes but sales and customer success never use.
8. **Specialists working without insight** — content teams guessing at buyer problems because product marketing wasn't involved.
9. **Channel confusion** — distributing through channels the persona doesn't use.

## Adaptive Handling

- **Strong upstream work:** go to Step 2 (audit) or Step 3 (mapping).
- **Starting from scratch:** begin with Step 1 and build lightweight inputs.
- **Single brief:** skip to Step 4, confirming persona and stage.
- **Underperforming content:** start with Step 2, then use mapping to find misalignment.
- **Sales-focused request:** emphasize Selection and Purchase; coordinate with Assets and Organizational Alignment.
- **Adoption-focused request:** emphasize Onboarding and Adoption; coordinate with Adoption and customer success.

## Using AI in this focus area

- Inventory and tag an existing content library by persona, stage and topic; flag outdated or duplicate pieces.
- Draft first versions and derivatives (summaries, social posts, email versions) from approved positioning, research and interview notes.
- Turn support tickets and product docs into draft how-to and FAQ content for users.
- Rules: check AI output against real market evidence — AI can't tell you what buyers actually ask, so ground content in interviews and data (NIHITO still applies). Keep sensitive customer, deal and company data out of tools not approved for it. The team, not the tool, owns what gets published. Watch for generic, unsourced claims and factual errors about the product; have a subject matter expert review technical and adoption content.
