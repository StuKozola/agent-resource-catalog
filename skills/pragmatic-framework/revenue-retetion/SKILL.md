# Revenue Retention — Pragmatic Framework Skill

## Purpose

Guide users through developing a comprehensive Revenue Retention plan aligned with the Pragmatic Institute Framework. Revenue Retention is officially defined as:

> **"Define the specific plans and budgets for ensuring customer loyalty, as well as selling additional products and services to existing customers."**

Revenue Retention sits in the **Programs** category of the Pragmatic Framework, alongside Marketing Plan, Revenue Growth, Launch, Awareness, Nurturing, Advocacy, and Measurement. It is the counterpart to Revenue Growth — while Revenue Growth focuses on acquiring new customers, Revenue Retention focuses on maximizing the value of existing customer relationships.

## Framework Context

Revenue Retention draws on and feeds into several adjacent Pragmatic Framework activities:

- **Buyer Personas & User Personas** → Understanding who your existing customers are and what problems they face is foundational to retention planning
- **Buyer Experience** → The post-purchase experience directly impacts renewal likelihood and expansion opportunities
- **Market Problems** → Ongoing discovery of evolving customer problems drives upsell/cross-sell relevance
- **Positioning** → Internal positioning documents inform retention messaging by persona and segment
- **Pricing** → Pricing models, tiers, and packaging create the structure for expansion revenue
- **Product Profitability** → Retention metrics feed into profitability analysis; profitability data informs retention investment decisions
- **Measurement** → Revenue Retention plans must define KPIs that tie back to corporate goals
- **Nurturing** → Nurturing programs support retention by moving existing customers toward additional purchases and deeper engagement
- **Advocacy** → Retained, satisfied customers become advocates who provide testimonials, case studies, and referrals
- **Sales Alignment** → Retention plans must align with how existing customers want to buy additional products
- **Win/Loss Analysis** → Understanding why customers leave (loss analysis on churned accounts) informs retention strategy
- **Competitive Landscape** → Competitive intelligence helps anticipate and counter competitive win-back campaigns targeting your customers

## Workflow

Follow these phases to build a Revenue Retention plan. Adapt depth and sequence to the user's context — a SaaS company with subscription revenue will emphasize NRR and churn differently than a manufacturing company with contract renewals or a B2C company with repeat purchases.

### Phase 1: Customer Base Assessment

Start by understanding the current state of the existing customer base. Ask the user about:

1. **Customer segments** — How is the existing customer base segmented? By size, industry, product line, contract type, or persona?
2. **Revenue composition** — What percentage of total revenue comes from existing customers vs. new customers? Is the business subscription/recurring, transactional, or a hybrid?
3. **Current retention metrics** — What does the user currently track? Gather whatever is available:
   - Customer retention rate (logo retention)
   - Net Revenue Retention (NRR)
   - Gross Revenue Retention (GRR)
   - Churn rate (voluntary and involuntary)
   - Customer Lifetime Value (CLV/LTV)
   - Expansion revenue (upsell, cross-sell, add-on)
4. **Churn analysis** — Why do customers leave? What are the top churn drivers? Has Win/Loss Analysis been conducted on lost customers?
5. **Existing retention programs** — What is currently being done? Loyalty programs, customer success teams, renewal processes, QBRs, health scores?

If the user lacks data on these items, help them define what to measure and how to start gathering it. Refer them to the **Measurement** activity and read `references/metrics-guide.md` for detailed metric definitions and formulas.

### Phase 2: Retention Strategy Development

Based on the customer base assessment, develop strategies across three pillars:

**Pillar 1: Protect — Reduce Churn**
- Identify at-risk customer signals (declining usage, support escalations, missed QBRs, payment failures)
- Design proactive intervention workflows for at-risk accounts
- Address involuntary churn (payment failures, expired cards) with dunning and retry processes
- Create customer health scoring methodology
- Establish renewal process and timeline (when to begin renewal conversations)
- Map churn triggers to the customer journey and design preventive touchpoints
- Conduct Win/Loss Analysis on recently churned accounts to understand patterns

**Pillar 2: Grow — Expansion Revenue**
- Identify upsell opportunities (upgrades, additional seats/licenses, premium tiers)
- Identify cross-sell opportunities (complementary products, add-ons, services)
- Design expansion playbooks aligned to customer maturity and usage patterns
- Leverage usage data and customer success insights to time expansion offers
- Create pricing/packaging strategies that create natural upgrade paths (connect to Pricing activity)
- Train customer-facing teams to identify and act on expansion signals
- Generate Customer Success Qualified Leads (CSQLs) for the sales team

**Pillar 3: Deepen — Customer Loyalty**
- Design loyalty and rewards programs appropriate to the business model
- Build customer community and peer engagement programs
- Create customer advisory boards (CABs) for strategic accounts
- Develop personalized communication cadences by segment
- Invest in customer education and enablement (training, certifications, content)
- Build feedback loops that demonstrate responsiveness to customer input
- Connect to the **Advocacy** activity to turn loyal customers into references and case studies
- Implement executive sponsor programs for key accounts

### Phase 3: Budget and Resource Planning

Help the user translate strategies into specific plans with budgets:

1. **Program inventory** — List all retention-focused programs and initiatives
2. **Resource requirements** — People, technology, content, and external services needed
3. **Budget allocation** — Assign budgets by program, by quarter, and by customer segment
4. **ROI modeling** — Calculate expected return: what does a 1% improvement in retention or NRR mean in dollar terms? Use the "Retention Economics" section in `references/metrics-guide.md`
5. **Technology needs** — CRM, customer success platforms, analytics, marketing automation for lifecycle campaigns

### Phase 4: Measurement Framework

Define how success will be tracked. Every Revenue Retention plan must include:

1. **Primary KPIs** — Select 3-5 headline metrics (e.g., NRR, GRR, churn rate, expansion revenue, CLV)
2. **Leading indicators** — Health scores, NPS/CSAT, product adoption, engagement metrics
3. **Reporting cadence** — Monthly, quarterly review cycles
4. **Targets and benchmarks** — Set specific numeric goals with industry context
5. **Feedback to strategy** — How measurement results will trigger strategy adjustments
6. **Alignment with corporate goals** — Per the Pragmatic Measurement activity, ensure retention KPIs connect to the organization's overall business objectives

Read `references/metrics-guide.md` for detailed definitions, formulas, benchmarks, and guidance on interpreting metric combinations.

## Output Formats

Depending on what the user needs, produce one or more of these deliverables:

### Revenue Retention Plan (Comprehensive)
A complete document covering all four phases. Read `references/plan-template.md` for the recommended structure.

### Retention Strategy Brief
A concise 1-2 page summary of the three strategic pillars with key initiatives, owners, and timelines. Useful for executive stakeholder communication.

### NRR/GRR Dashboard Specification
Metric definitions, data sources, calculation logic, and visualization recommendations for building a retention metrics dashboard.

### Expansion Revenue Playbook
Focused deliverable on upsell/cross-sell strategies, triggers, talk tracks, and measurement.

### Churn Reduction Action Plan
Focused deliverable on identifying and addressing churn drivers with specific interventions and timelines.

### Customer Health Score Model
Framework for building a composite customer health score including inputs, weighting, thresholds, and action triggers.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing Revenue Retention with Revenue Growth** — Retention is about existing customers; Growth is about new customers. They require different strategies, different teams, and often different messaging. In the Pragmatic Framework these are explicitly separate activities.
- **Masking churn with expansion** — A high NRR can hide dangerously high churn if expansion revenue from a few large accounts compensates. Always track GRR alongside NRR to see the true retention picture.
- **Treating all customers the same** — Retention strategies should be segmented by customer value, risk level, and persona. High-value strategic accounts need different treatment than long-tail self-serve customers.
- **Focusing only on "save" motions** — Retention starts well before a customer signals intent to leave. The best retention strategies are proactive, not reactive.
- **Ignoring the product** — No amount of retention marketing can compensate for a product that does not solve the customer's problems. Revenue Retention plans should include feedback loops to Product (connecting back to Market Problems and Requirements).
- **No budget for retention** — Many organizations heavily invest in acquisition but underinvest in retention. Research consistently shows retaining existing customers is 5-7x more cost-effective than acquiring new ones, and a 5% improvement in retention can boost profits 25-95%.
- **Disconnecting from Sales Alignment** — Expansion selling to existing customers still requires alignment between how you sell and how the customer wants to buy.
- **Measuring too late** — Tracking only lagging indicators like churn rate means you discover problems after customers have already left. Build leading indicators (health scores, engagement, NPS trends) into the measurement framework.
- **Forgetting involuntary churn** — Involuntary churn from payment failures can represent 20-40% of total churn and is often the easiest to fix with dunning processes and payment retry logic.

## Pragmatic Principles

Keep these Pragmatic Framework principles front-of-mind throughout:

- **Outside-in thinking** — Build retention plans based on what customers need and how they experience your product, not internal assumptions
- **NIHITO (Nothing Important Happens In The Office)** — Talk to existing customers regularly; don't rely solely on dashboards and usage data
- **Data-driven decisions** — Use metrics to prioritize retention investments and measure impact
- **Market-driven, not product-driven** — Retention messaging should focus on solving customer problems, not pushing product features
- **Your opinion, although interesting, is irrelevant** — Base retention strategies on actual customer feedback and behavior data, not internal guesses about why customers stay or leave
