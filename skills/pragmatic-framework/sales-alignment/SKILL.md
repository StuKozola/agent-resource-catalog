# Sales Alignment — Pragmatic Framework Enablement Skill

## Context

Sales Alignment is an Enablement activity in the Pragmatic Framework. Its official definition:

> **Use your market knowledge to help sales align their selling process to the way the market wants to buy.**

The core insight is that most organizations design their selling process around internal convenience — how *they* want to sell — rather than around how buyers actually research, evaluate, and purchase. Sales Alignment flips this by mapping the selling process to the buying process, then ensuring the right content, tools, and information reach sellers at each stage.

**Key responsibilities from the Pragmatic Framework:**
- Align the organization's selling process to the buying process
- Describe information needs for key personas at each step of the process
- Align sales support content to various stages of the sales process

**Required skills:**
- Ability to partner and integrate tools for sales team support
- Understanding of the buying process (from Buyer Experience work)
- Understanding of the sales process and what drives sales to become strong partners

Sales Alignment sits at the intersection of several upstream Pragmatic activities:
- **Buyer Personas** — who is buying and their decision-making roles
- **Buyer Experience** — the stages, barriers, and decision criteria in the buying journey
- **Positioning** — how the product solves market problems, by persona
- **Win/Loss Analysis** — patterns in why deals are won or lost
- **Use Scenarios** — stories that put market problems in context

It feeds directly into related Enablement activities:
- **Content** — creating buyer-focused content for each buying stage
- **Sales Tools** — specific tools for specific steps of the selling process
- **Channel Training** — teaching sales how to *sell* (not how to *use*) the product

## Workflow

Follow these six phases. Adapt based on user maturity — some organizations may have robust Buyer Experience documentation while others are starting from scratch.

### Phase 1: Gather Upstream Inputs

Before building a sales alignment map, collect what already exists. Ask the user about:

1. **Buyer Personas** — Which personas are involved in purchasing decisions? What are their roles (economic buyer, technical buyer, champion, end user)?
2. **Buyer Experience / Journey** — Has the buying process been documented? What stages do buyers go through? What barriers do they encounter?
3. **Positioning** — What positioning documents exist? Are they segmented by persona?
4. **Win/Loss Data** — What patterns exist in recent wins and losses? Where in the process do deals stall or fall apart?
5. **Current Sales Process** — What does the existing selling process look like? What CRM stages are defined? How do sellers actually work deals today?

If the user lacks upstream inputs, help them build lightweight versions. Read `references/buying-process-framework.md` for the standard buying stages to use as scaffolding.

**Key diagnostic question:** Ask the user to describe how their current sales stages map to what buyers are actually doing at each point. Misalignment here is the core problem Sales Alignment solves.

### Phase 2: Map the Buying Process

Using Buyer Experience data (or building it collaboratively), define the buying process stages the market uses. A typical B2B buying process follows these stages:

1. **Problem Recognition** — Buyer realizes status quo is insufficient
2. **Research & Education** — Buyer self-educates on the problem space
3. **Solution Exploration** — Buyer identifies categories of solutions
4. **Requirements Definition** — Buyer defines criteria and builds a shortlist
5. **Evaluation & Validation** — Buyer evaluates specific vendors/products
6. **Decision & Negotiation** — Buyer selects vendor and negotiates terms
7. **Implementation & Adoption** — Buyer onboards and realizes value

For each stage, document:
- **What the buyer is doing** — activities, research, conversations
- **What the buyer is thinking** — questions, concerns, decision criteria
- **Who is involved** — which personas are active at this stage
- **Barriers** — what slows or stops the buyer from advancing
- **Information needs** — what the buyer needs to move forward

Read `references/buying-process-framework.md` for detailed stage definitions and persona mapping guidance.

### Phase 3: Map the Selling Process

Document the organization's current selling process. Common B2B selling stages include:

1. **Prospecting / Lead Qualification** — Identifying and qualifying opportunities
2. **Discovery** — Understanding the prospect's situation, problems, and needs
3. **Solution Presentation** — Demonstrating how the product solves their problems
4. **Proposal / Business Case** — Presenting pricing, ROI, and implementation plans
5. **Negotiation / Close** — Handling objections, negotiating terms, securing commitment
6. **Onboarding / Handoff** — Transitioning from sales to customer success

For each stage, document:
- **Seller activities** — what reps actually do
- **Exit criteria** — what must be true before advancing to the next stage
- **Tools and content currently used** — what reps rely on today
- **Common failure points** — where deals stall or sellers struggle

### Phase 4: Align Selling to Buying

This is the core deliverable. Create a side-by-side alignment map that connects each buying stage to the corresponding selling stage. The goal is not a 1:1 mapping — buying stages and selling stages rarely align perfectly. Instead, identify:

1. **Where selling activities match buyer needs** — these are strengths to reinforce
2. **Where the selling process is ahead of the buyer** — sellers pushing for commitment before the buyer is ready (e.g., demoing before the buyer has defined requirements)
3. **Where the selling process lags behind the buyer** — sellers not providing what buyers need at the right time (e.g., no technical depth available when the buyer is in evaluation)
4. **Content and information gaps** — stages where the buyer needs information that doesn't exist or isn't accessible to the seller
5. **Persona mismatches** — stages where the selling team engages the wrong persona

Read `references/content-mapping-guide.md` for the content alignment matrix and gap analysis methodology.

For each aligned stage, specify:
- **Buyer stage** ↔ **Seller stage**
- **Key personas active**
- **Buyer's information needs**
- **Required seller actions** (aligned to buyer, not to internal process)
- **Content and tools needed** (mapped to both buyer needs and seller activities)
- **Success metrics** — how to know the buyer has moved forward

### Phase 5: Identify Gaps and Recommendations

Synthesize the alignment map into actionable findings:

**Content Gaps** — Where buyers need information that doesn't exist
- Map each gap to a buying stage, persona, and information need
- Prioritize by impact on deal velocity and win rate

**Process Gaps** — Where the selling process doesn't match buying behavior
- Identify stages where sellers push when they should listen
- Flag handoff problems between sales roles (e.g., SDR to AE to SE)
- Note where CRM stages don't reflect actual buyer progress

**Skill Gaps** — Where sellers lack capabilities the buyer process demands
- Discovery skills for early-stage buying
- Technical depth for evaluation-stage buying
- Executive communication for decision-stage buying

**Tool Gaps** — Where sellers lack the right tools for the right moment
- Battle cards, ROI calculators, reference stories, demo environments
- Map each tool need to a specific buying stage and persona

### Phase 6: Document the Sales Alignment Plan

Produce a comprehensive Sales Alignment document. Read `templates/sales-alignment-template.md` for the full document structure.

The document should include:
1. **Executive Summary** — Key findings, critical gaps, and top recommendations
2. **Buying Process Map** — The documented buying stages with persona involvement
3. **Selling Process Map** — Current selling stages with activities and tools
4. **Alignment Matrix** — The side-by-side map with gap annotations
5. **Content Map** — What content exists, what's needed, mapped by stage and persona
6. **Gap Analysis** — Prioritized list of content, process, skill, and tool gaps
7. **Recommendations** — Specific actions to close gaps, with owners and timelines
8. **Success Metrics** — KPIs to measure whether alignment is improving

## Formatting Guidelines

- Use tables for alignment matrices — buying stage in the left column, selling activities, content, and gaps in adjacent columns
- Use persona names consistently (match the user's existing persona naming conventions)
- When listing content by stage, indicate whether it's buyer-facing or seller-facing
- Flag the highest-priority gaps with clear severity indicators
- Include both "quick wins" (content or process changes achievable in 30 days) and "strategic investments" (longer-term process or tooling changes)

## Pragmatic Principles to Reinforce

- **Outside-in thinking**: The selling process must be designed from the buyer's perspective, not the seller's convenience
- **NIHITO (Nothing Important Happens In The Office)**: Sales alignment should be informed by real buyer interviews and win/loss data, not internal assumptions about how buyers buy
- **Problems, not features**: Content mapped to the selling process should focus on buyer problems, not product features — sellers should be equipped to talk about problems first
- **Personas drive everything**: Different personas need different information at different stages — a one-size-fits-all approach to sales enablement fails

## Common Pitfalls

Watch for and flag these issues:
- **Inside-out selling process** — CRM stages named for what the seller does ("Demo Scheduled") rather than what the buyer has achieved ("Requirements Confirmed")
- **Feature-dump content** — Sales decks and data sheets organized by product capability rather than by buyer problem
- **Missing the champion** — No content or tools designed to help internal champions sell on your behalf when the seller isn't in the room
- **Ignoring the "no decision" path** — Most lost deals are lost to "no decision" (status quo wins), not to competitors; alignment must address buyer inertia
- **Linear assumptions** — Modern B2B buying is non-linear; buyers loop back, skip stages, and engage multiple vendors simultaneously; the alignment map should account for this

## Adaptive Handling

- **If the user has no Buyer Experience documentation**: Help them build a lightweight buying process based on their win/loss patterns and sales team knowledge, then use that as the foundation
- **If the user has a mature CRM and sales process**: Focus on gap analysis between existing selling stages and buyer behavior rather than rebuilding from scratch
- **If the user is focused on a specific deal stage**: Zoom into that stage's alignment rather than building the full map; flag that the full alignment should be completed later
- **If the user wants to improve win rates**: Start with Win/Loss Analysis patterns, identify where in the buying process deals are lost, and align from those pain points outward
- **If the user is launching a new product**: Build the sales alignment map proactively before launch, using buyer personas and positioning to anticipate the buying process
