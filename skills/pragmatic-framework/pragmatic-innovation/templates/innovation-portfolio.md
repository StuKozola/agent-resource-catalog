# Innovation Portfolio Assessment

## Purpose

Most organizations pursue multiple innovation initiatives simultaneously. The Innovation Portfolio Assessment helps teams categorize, balance, and prioritize their innovation investments — ensuring the portfolio as a whole is aligned with market needs and organizational strategy.

This connects directly to the Product Portfolio and Product Profitability activities in the Pragmatic Framework. A well-managed innovation portfolio should feed into the broader product portfolio strategy.

## When to Use

- Annual or quarterly planning when deciding where to invest innovation resources
- When the team has more innovation ideas than capacity and needs to prioritize
- When leadership asks "are we investing in the right things?"
- When the innovation pipeline feels unbalanced (too many moonshots, or too incremental)

## How to Guide the User

### Step 1: Inventory Current Innovations

Help the user list all active and proposed innovation initiatives. For each one, capture:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Name** | Short descriptive name |
| **Market Problem** | Which validated market problem does this address? |
| **Target Persona/Segment** | Who benefits? |
| **Innovation Type** | Incremental, Adjacent, or Transformational |
| **Stage** | Concept, Validation, Development, or Launched |
| **Distinctive Competency Link** | Which PRIME competency does this leverage? |
| **Estimated Investment** | Rough resource/cost estimate (T-shirt size is fine) |
| **Expected Impact** | Revenue, retention, strategic positioning, etc. |
| **Risk Level** | Low, Medium, High |

### Step 2: Analyze Portfolio Balance

Once the inventory is complete, assess the portfolio along several dimensions:

**By Innovation Type**:
A common healthy ratio (adjust for your org's risk tolerance and market position):
- ~70% Incremental — sustaining and improving current products
- ~20% Adjacent — extending into new markets or approaches
- ~10% Transformational — breakthrough or disruptive innovations

If the portfolio is heavily skewed in one direction, discuss whether that's intentional or a sign of drift.

**By Market Problem Coverage**:
- Are the top 3-5 validated market problems each addressed by at least one innovation?
- Are there validated problems with no innovation investment?
- Are there innovations that don't map to any validated problem? (These are the highest risk — consider pausing or validating the problem.)

**By Distinctive Competency**:
- Which competencies are being leveraged most heavily? Least?
- Are any innovations attempting to build entirely new competencies? (Higher risk and longer timeline — make sure that's intentional.)

**By Stage**:
- Is there a healthy pipeline from concept → validation → development → launched?
- Are things stuck in "concept" without moving to validation? (Innovation theater risk)
- Are things jumping from concept straight to development without validation? (Market risk)

### Step 3: Identify Actions

Based on the analysis, help the user identify:

1. **Keep / Accelerate**: Innovations that are well-aligned with market problems, leverage distinctive competencies, and are making progress.

2. **Validate Before Continuing**: Innovations that seem promising but have unvalidated market assumptions. Define what validation is needed and by when.

3. **Pause / Kill**: Innovations that don't connect to validated market problems, don't leverage distinctive competencies, or are stalled without clear path forward. Killing innovations that aren't working is a sign of good portfolio management, not failure.

4. **Gap-Fill**: Validated market problems that have no innovation investment. These are potential new initiatives to add to the portfolio.

### Step 4: Set Review Cadence

Innovation portfolios should be reviewed regularly — quarterly at minimum. Help the user define:
- Who reviews the portfolio (product leadership, executive sponsors)
- What metrics they'll track (stage progression, market validation results, resource allocation)
- What triggers a re-assessment outside the regular cadence (major competitive move, market shift, key customer loss)

## Output Format

The final deliverable should be a concise portfolio summary that a leadership team could review in 20-30 minutes, containing:

1. Portfolio inventory table (from Step 1)
2. Balance analysis with visualizations where helpful (by type, by problem, by stage)
3. Recommended actions for each initiative (keep/accelerate, validate, pause/kill)
4. Identified gaps and proposed new initiatives
5. Review schedule and governance
