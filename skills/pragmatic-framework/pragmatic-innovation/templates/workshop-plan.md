# Innovation Workshop Plan

## Purpose

An Innovation Workshop is a structured team session for generating market-driven innovation ideas. The key difference from a generic brainstorm: every idea must be anchored to a validated market problem and evaluated against the organization's distinctive competencies.

This workshop format embodies the Pragmatic principle that innovation should solve real problems, not just produce exciting-sounding ideas.

## When to Use

- Kicking off innovation planning for a new quarter or fiscal year
- Exploring solution approaches to a specific high-priority market problem
- After completing customer discovery and wanting to translate findings into innovation ideas
- When the team needs to break out of incremental thinking and explore adjacent/transformational possibilities

## Pre-Workshop Preparation

These materials must be assembled before the workshop. If they don't exist, the workshop isn't ready — do the upstream work first.

**Required inputs** (print/share with all participants before the session):
1. Top 5-10 validated market problems with urgency and pervasiveness scores
2. Distinctive competencies summary (PRIME analysis output)
3. Competitive landscape overview (key competitors, their strengths/weaknesses, gaps)
4. Asset inventory highlights (notable tech, IP, skills, data assets)

**Participants** (8-12 people ideal):
- Product management (owns facilitation)
- Engineering / R&D (2-3 people with deep technical knowledge)
- Design (1-2 people)
- Customer-facing roles: Sales, Customer Success, or Support (1-2 people for market voice)
- Optional: Executive sponsor (for the first 30 min to set context, then leaves)

**Logistics**:
- Block 3-4 hours (half-day); innovation work suffers when rushed
- Room with whiteboard space or digital collaboration tool
- Printed copies of the market problems and distinctive competencies

## Workshop Agenda

### Part 1: Grounding (30 minutes)

**Objective**: Get everyone aligned on the market reality before generating ideas.

1. **Market Problem Review** (15 min) — Walk through the top validated market problems. For each, share: who has the problem, how acute it is, what evidence supports it, what customers said in their own words. The goal is empathy with the customer's pain, not abstract data.

2. **Distinctive Competencies Review** (10 min) — Remind the team what the org is uniquely good at. Emphasize that good innovation leverages these — it's not about what's theoretically possible but what *this organization* can deliver better than anyone else.

3. **Ground Rules** (5 min):
   - Every idea must connect to a market problem on the board
   - "Because it would be cool" is not sufficient justification — ask "for whom and why?"
   - Quantity first, evaluation later (during generation phases)
   - No idea ownership — once shared, ideas belong to the team
   - Silence phones and laptops during generation exercises

### Part 2: Problem Deep-Dives (45 minutes)

**Objective**: Deepen understanding of the top 2-3 problems before solutioning.

For each selected problem:
- **Customer story**: Read or tell a real customer story that illustrates this problem. Use actual quotes from interviews where possible. The UnitedHealthcare Sherpa example is instructive: going into sick patients' homes and seeing that insurance paperwork had taken over the kitchen revealed problems that no survey would have surfaced.
- **Current workarounds**: How are people solving this today? What's painful about the workaround?
- **Jobs-to-be-done lens**: What is the customer really trying to accomplish? What does "done well" look like from their perspective?

### Part 3: Idea Generation (60 minutes)

**Objective**: Generate a large quantity of ideas anchored to validated problems.

Run 2-3 rounds of structured ideation, varying the prompt to get different types of thinking:

**Round 1 — Direct Solution** (20 min):
"Given market problem X and our distinctive competencies, what could we build or do differently to solve this problem better than anyone else?"

**Round 2 — Constraint Removal** (20 min):
"If we had unlimited resources and no legacy constraints, how would we solve this problem? What would the ideal customer experience look like?" (Then: which elements could we realistically deliver?)

**Round 3 — Adjacent Transfer** (20 min):
"How has this type of problem been solved in other industries or domains? What can we borrow or adapt?" (Especially useful for adjacent and transformational innovation.)

For each round:
- 5 minutes silent individual brainstorming (sticky notes or digital equivalent)
- 10 minutes share and cluster (group similar ideas)
- 5 minutes rapid discussion (clarify, not evaluate)

### Part 4: Evaluation and Prioritization (45 minutes)

**Objective**: Narrow to the most promising ideas using Pragmatic criteria.

1. **Quick Filter** (10 min) — For each idea cluster, do a rapid check:
   - Does it solve a validated market problem? (If not, set aside)
   - Does it leverage a distinctive competency? (If not, flag as higher risk)
   - Is the market willing to pay for this? (If unclear, it needs validation)

2. **Scoring** (20 min) — For ideas that pass the quick filter, score on:
   - **Problem-Solution Fit**: How directly and completely does this solve the problem? (1-5)
   - **Competency Alignment**: How well does this leverage our unique strengths? (1-5)
   - **Market Impact**: How large is the addressable opportunity? (1-5)
   - **Feasibility**: Can we realistically deliver this? (1-5)
   - **Defensibility**: How hard would it be for competitors to replicate? (1-5)

3. **Select Top Ideas** (15 min) — Identify the top 3-5 ideas based on scores. Discuss as a group — scores are a starting point for discussion, not a final answer. Agree on which ideas merit an Innovation Brief.

### Part 5: Next Steps (15 minutes)

For each selected idea:
- Assign an owner to write the Innovation Brief (use the Innovation Brief template)
- Set a deadline for the brief (typically 1-2 weeks)
- Identify what additional validation is needed (customer concept testing, technical spike, etc.)
- Schedule a follow-up review session

## Post-Workshop

Within 48 hours:
- Distribute workshop notes and photos of all idea boards
- Confirm Innovation Brief owners and deadlines
- File the full idea inventory (including ideas not selected) for future reference — market conditions change and today's "not yet" might become tomorrow's priority

## Common Facilitation Pitfalls

- **Letting HiPPOs dominate**: The Highest Paid Person's Opinion shouldn't carry more weight. Enforce silent brainstorming and equal sharing time.
- **Jumping to solutions too fast**: If people start pitching solutions during Part 2, redirect to problem understanding.
- **Losing the market anchor**: If ideas drift away from validated problems, point back to the board. "That's interesting — which market problem does it address?"
- **Over-evaluating during generation**: Keep Parts 3 and 4 strictly separate. Evaluation during brainstorming kills creativity.
- **Ending without commitments**: Every workshop should produce assigned owners and deadlines, or the ideas will evaporate.
