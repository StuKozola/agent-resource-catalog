# Revenue Growth — Pragmatic Framework Skill

## Purpose

Revenue Growth is an activity in the **Programs** category of the Pragmatic Framework. Its official definition:

> **Define the specific plans and budgets for selling products and services to new customers.**

Revenue Growth is specifically about **new customer acquisition** — the plans, channels, budgets, targets, and metrics required to bring net-new buyers into the business. It is distinct from Revenue Retention, which covers loyalty, upsell, and cross-sell to existing customers.

## Framework Context

Revenue Growth sits in the Programs row of the Pragmatic Framework alongside:
- **Marketing Plan** — overarching strategies and tactics for awareness and leads
- **Revenue Retention** — plans and budgets for existing-customer revenue
- **Launch** — execution and measurement of strategic launches
- **Awareness** — programs to raise brand profile in target segments
- **Nurturing** — programs to move prospects through the funnel
- **Advocacy** — amplifying the voice of satisfied customers
- **Measurement** — measuring and tuning programs against corporate goals

Revenue Growth draws heavily on upstream inputs:
- **Market Definition** and **Buyer Personas** — who are the target segments and buyers
- **Positioning** — how the product solves market problems for each persona
- **Buyer Experience** — the documented buying journey for target segments
- **Pricing** — the pricing model, schedules, and guidelines
- **Distribution Strategy** — which channels align with buyer preferences
- **Business Plan** — the financial model and investment basis
- **Competitive Landscape** — strengths and weaknesses of alternatives

Revenue Growth feeds downstream into:
- **Sales Alignment** — aligning the sales process to how the market buys
- **Content** and **Sales Tools** — materials needed to execute acquisition programs
- **Channel Training** — training sales channels on how to sell
- **Measurement** — KPIs to track acquisition program effectiveness

## Key Principles

1. **Outside-in thinking**: Revenue Growth plans start with how buyers want to buy, not how the company wants to sell. Use buyer personas and the documented buyer experience to shape acquisition strategies.

2. **New customers only**: Revenue Growth is exclusively about net-new customer acquisition. Expansion, upsell, cross-sell, and retention belong to Revenue Retention. Keeping this distinction clean is critical for accurate planning and measurement.

3. **Plans AND budgets**: This is not aspirational goal-setting. Revenue Growth requires specific, funded plans with allocated budgets, channel investments, headcount decisions, and timeline commitments.

4. **Market-driven channels**: Channel selection should be informed by buyer persona preferences and the Distribution Strategy, not internal convenience.

5. **Measurable targets**: Every acquisition plan needs defined KPIs — new customer count, new-logo revenue, CAC, pipeline targets, conversion rates — tied to the overarching Business Plan financial model.

## Workflow

When a user wants help with Revenue Growth, follow this workflow. Adapt the depth and sequence based on what the user already has in place.

### Step 1: Assess Readiness — Gather Upstream Inputs

Before building a Revenue Growth plan, confirm what foundational inputs are available. Ask the user about:

- **Target segments and buyer personas**: Who are the ideal new customers? What are their characteristics, problems, and buying behaviors?
- **Positioning**: What is the product's positioning for each target segment? How does it solve their specific problems?
- **Buyer experience/journey**: What steps do these buyers take when evaluating and purchasing? What are the barriers?
- **Pricing model**: What is the pricing structure? Are there introductory offers, trials, or land-and-expand pricing for new customers?
- **Distribution channels**: What channels (direct sales, partners, self-serve, marketplace) are available and preferred by target buyers?
- **Competitive landscape**: Who are the main competitors for new-customer attention? What are their strengths?
- **Current revenue baseline**: What is the current new-customer revenue run rate? Historical acquisition trends?

If critical inputs are missing, note them and recommend the user address those first (referencing the appropriate Pragmatic Framework activity), but continue building what you can.

### Step 2: Set Revenue Growth Targets

Help the user define specific, quantified targets for new-customer acquisition:

- **New-customer revenue target** — total revenue expected from new customers in the planning period
- **New-logo count target** — number of net-new customers to acquire
- **Average deal size** — expected average contract value for new customers
- **Revenue by segment** — break targets down by market segment, geography, or product line
- **Timeline** — quarterly or monthly targets with ramp assumptions
- **Revenue mix** — what percentage of total company revenue should come from new customers vs. existing (industry benchmarks suggest strong growers target >10% of revenue growth from new customers)

Use the formula:
```
New-Customer Revenue Target = New-Logo Target × Average Deal Size
```

Cross-validate against the Business Plan financial model and the Market Definition (TAM/SAM/SOM).

### Step 3: Define Acquisition Channels and Strategy

For each target segment and buyer persona, define the acquisition channels and strategies:

**Channel Selection Criteria:**
- Where do target buyers discover and evaluate solutions? (from Buyer Experience)
- What channels does the Distribution Strategy specify?
- What is the expected CAC per channel?
- What is the channel's capacity and scalability?

**Common B2B Acquisition Channels:**
- Direct sales (outbound prospecting, hunting teams)
- Inbound marketing (content, SEO, thought leadership)
- Paid digital (search, display, social advertising)
- Partner/channel sales (resellers, referral partners, alliances)
- Product-led growth (free trial, freemium, self-serve)
- Events and conferences (trade shows, webinars, field events)
- Account-based marketing (ABM) for high-value targets
- Referral programs (customer referral incentives)

For each channel, document:
- Target segment(s) served
- Expected new-customer contribution (logos and revenue)
- Budget allocation
- Key tactics and campaigns
- Timeline and milestones
- Owner/team responsible

### Step 4: Build the Acquisition Budget

Help the user construct a detailed acquisition budget that covers:

**Budget Categories:**
- **Personnel**: Sales headcount (hunters/SDRs/BDRs), marketing staff dedicated to acquisition
- **Demand generation**: Paid media, content creation, SEO, events
- **Sales enablement**: Tools, CRM, prospecting platforms, sales intelligence
- **Partner/channel**: Channel development costs, partner incentives, co-marketing
- **Product-led**: Trial infrastructure, onboarding for new customers, freemium support
- **Programs**: ABM programs, referral incentives, promotional offers

**Budget Validation:**
- Calculate target CAC: `Total Acquisition Budget ÷ New-Logo Target`
- Validate CAC against Customer Lifetime Value (CLV): healthy ratio is ≥ 3:1 (CLV:CAC)
- Compare CAC to industry benchmarks
- Ensure budget supports the pipeline coverage needed (typically 3-5× pipeline to target ratio)

**Pipeline Math:**
```
Required Pipeline = Revenue Target ÷ Win Rate
Required Opportunities = Required Pipeline ÷ Average Deal Size
Required SQLs = Required Opportunities ÷ SQL-to-Opportunity Rate
Required MQLs = Required SQLs ÷ MQL-to-SQL Rate
Required Leads = Required MQLs ÷ Lead-to-MQL Rate
```

### Step 5: Define Metrics and Measurement

Establish the KPIs that will be tracked to measure Revenue Growth plan effectiveness:

**Primary KPIs:**
- New-customer revenue (actual vs. target)
- New-logo count (actual vs. target)
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- CAC payback period
- CLV:CAC ratio

**Pipeline and Funnel KPIs:**
- Pipeline generated (by channel, segment)
- Pipeline coverage ratio
- Conversion rates at each funnel stage
- Sales cycle length for new customers
- Win rate for new opportunities

**Channel-Level KPIs:**
- Cost per lead (CPL) by channel
- Lead-to-customer conversion rate by channel
- Revenue contribution by channel
- Channel ROI

**Leading Indicators:**
- Marketing-qualified leads (MQLs)
- Sales-qualified leads (SQLs)
- Demo/trial requests
- Inbound inquiry volume
- Website traffic from target segments

### Step 6: Build the Revenue Growth Plan Document

Assemble all elements into a structured Revenue Growth Plan. Load the template from `references/revenue-growth-plan-template.md` for the recommended structure.

### Step 7: Review and Validate

Guide the user through validation:
- Does the plan align with the Business Plan financial model?
- Are targets realistic given market size (SAM/SOM) and competitive dynamics?
- Is the budget sufficient to support the pipeline math?
- Are channel investments aligned with buyer preferences?
- Are there dependencies on other teams (sales, product, partnerships) that need alignment?
- Is there a quarterly review cadence to track and adjust?

## Output Formats

Depending on the user's needs, produce one or more of:

1. **Revenue Growth Plan** — comprehensive document covering targets, channels, budget, metrics, and timeline (use template)
2. **Acquisition Budget** — detailed budget breakdown by channel and category
3. **Pipeline Model** — funnel math showing required leads → MQLs → SQLs → opportunities → closed-won
4. **Channel Strategy Matrix** — segment × channel mapping with investment and expected contribution
5. **KPI Dashboard Specification** — defined metrics with targets, owners, and review cadence
6. **Executive Summary** — one-page overview of growth targets, key investments, and expected ROI

## Common Pitfalls

- **Mixing new and existing customer revenue**: Revenue Growth is strictly about new customers. Upsell and expansion belong in Revenue Retention. Combining them obscures acquisition performance.
- **Setting targets without funded plans**: Revenue targets without corresponding budgets and channel plans are just wishes. Every target needs a funded path to achievement.
- **Ignoring buyer experience**: Building acquisition plans around internal capabilities rather than how buyers actually discover and purchase leads to wasted spend.
- **Under-investing in pipeline**: Not building enough pipeline coverage (3-5× target) almost always results in missed revenue goals.
- **Channel concentration risk**: Over-relying on a single acquisition channel creates fragility. Diversify across at least 2-3 channels.
- **Not tracking CAC by channel**: Aggregate CAC masks channel-level inefficiencies. Track acquisition economics at the channel level.
- **Disconnecting from Sales Alignment**: Revenue Growth plans that aren't coordinated with how sales actually engages new prospects will underperform.
- **Annual-only planning**: New-customer acquisition plans need quarterly (or monthly) targets and reviews to catch and correct course quickly.
