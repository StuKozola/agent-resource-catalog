# Portfolio Artifact Templates

The Pragmatic Framework says to manage a portfolio like a product. That means the portfolio needs
the same strategic artifacts that any individual product would have. This reference provides
templates and guidance for each one.

## 1. Portfolio Business Plan

The portfolio business plan is the investment thesis for the entire product grouping. It answers:
"Why does this portfolio exist, and is it worth the investment?"

### Structure

**Executive Summary**
- Portfolio name and scope (what's included)
- Market opportunity size (TAM/SAM/SOM for the portfolio as a whole)
- Current portfolio revenue and growth trajectory
- Investment required and expected return
- Strategic rationale (why these products belong together)

**Market Context**
- Target market segments and their characteristics
- Key market problems the portfolio addresses
- Market trends affecting the portfolio (growth, disruption, regulation)
- Competitive landscape at the portfolio level (not just per-product)

**Portfolio Composition**
- List of products with strategic role (star, cash cow, growth bet, etc.)
- Revenue and margin contribution by product
- Lifecycle stage distribution
- Gap analysis — what's missing?

**Financial Model**
- Revenue forecast by product and in aggregate (3-year horizon)
- Cost structure and margin analysis
- Resource requirements (headcount, capital, marketing spend)
- Key assumptions and sensitivity analysis
- Break-even analysis for newer products within the portfolio

**Risk Assessment**
- Market risks (segment contraction, disruption, regulatory change)
- Competitive risks (new entrants, consolidation, pricing pressure)
- Execution risks (technology, talent, coordination complexity)
- Portfolio concentration risks (revenue dependence on one product or segment)

**Recommendations**
- Invest / maintain / divest decisions for each product
- Resource allocation priorities
- Key initiatives for the planning period
- Success metrics and review timeline

### Tips for Writing a Strong Portfolio Business Plan
- Ground every assertion in market data, not internal opinions (Pragmatic Rule: "Your opinion,
  although interesting, is irrelevant")
- The financial model should be honest about uncertainty — use scenarios (best case, expected,
  worst case) rather than single-point forecasts
- Include explicit criteria for when to revisit the plan (market trigger events, performance
  thresholds)
- Keep it to 10-15 pages; this is an executive decision document, not a research paper

## 2. Portfolio Positioning Document

Portfolio positioning describes how the entire portfolio solves market problems. It's the
umbrella story that individual product positioning fits under.

### The Pragmatic Positioning Format

**For** [target market segment]
**Who** [statement of the market problem or need]
**The** [portfolio name] **is a** [portfolio category]
**That** [statement of key benefit the portfolio provides]
**Unlike** [primary competitive alternative]
**Our portfolio** [statement of primary differentiation]

### Positioning Document Structure

**Target Segment Profile**
- Who is the target buyer? (Use buyer personas from the Pragmatic Framework)
- What is their role, and what market problems do they face?
- How do they currently solve these problems?

**Portfolio Value Proposition**
- The positioning statement (using the format above)
- Supporting proof points (3-5 specific capabilities or outcomes)
- How the portfolio delivers more value than individual products purchased separately

**Competitive Frame of Reference**
- What alternatives does the market consider? (Competing portfolios, point solutions,
  build-it-themselves, do nothing)
- How is the portfolio differentiated from each alternative?
- What is the portfolio's "unfair advantage"?

**Portfolio Story**
- The narrative arc: problem → impact → solution → outcome
- How individual products work together to solve the problem more completely than any single
  product could
- Customer evidence (quotes, case studies, metrics)

**Messaging Hierarchy**
- Portfolio-level message (the umbrella)
- Product-level messages (how each product supports the portfolio story)
- Segment-specific variations (if the portfolio serves multiple segments)

### Tips for Portfolio Positioning
- The portfolio positioning must be more than just "we have a lot of stuff." It should
  articulate a unique perspective on the market problem that only this combination of products
  can address.
- Test the positioning with the market, not just internally. The Pragmatic principle of
  outside-in thinking applies here.
- The positioning should make the buying decision simpler, not more complex. If buyers are
  confused by the portfolio structure, the positioning isn't working.

## 3. Portfolio Buying Process

Document how buyers evaluate and purchase across the portfolio. This is different from individual
product buying processes because portfolio purchases often involve cross-sell, upsell, and
bundling dynamics.

### What to Document

**Entry Points**
- How do buyers first encounter the portfolio? Which product is usually the "land" in a
  land-and-expand strategy?
- What triggers a buyer to consider the portfolio rather than a single product?

**Evaluation Process**
- Who is involved in portfolio-level purchase decisions? (Often more senior than individual
  product purchases)
- What criteria do buyers use to evaluate a portfolio vs. assembling point solutions?
- What information do buyers need at each stage?

**Cross-Sell and Upsell Paths**
- Map the typical journey: Product A → Product B → Product C
- What triggers expansion from one product to another?
- What barriers slow or stop expansion?
- What's the average time between initial purchase and portfolio expansion?

**Buying Barriers**
- Common objections to portfolio-level commitments
- Organizational silos that prevent coordinated purchasing
- Budget and procurement challenges (portfolio purchases may span multiple budgets)
- Technical integration concerns

**Enablement Needs**
- What does sales need to sell the portfolio effectively?
- What does the buyer need to justify the portfolio purchase internally?
- What proof points or references help buyers commit at the portfolio level?

## 4. Portfolio Market Requirements

Market requirements at the portfolio level capture needs that no single product team owns but
the portfolio must address. These are typically about integration, consistency, and cross-product
experience.

### Types of Portfolio Requirements

**Integration Requirements**
- Data sharing between products (common data model, APIs, single source of truth)
- Unified authentication and authorization (single sign-on across the portfolio)
- Consistent user experience patterns (navigation, terminology, visual design)
- Workflow continuity (starting a task in one product and completing it in another)

**Cross-Product Requirements**
- Unified reporting and analytics that span multiple products
- Shared customer identity (recognizing the same customer across products)
- Consistent pricing and packaging (bundles, suites, tiered offerings)
- Common onboarding experience for new customers adopting multiple products

**Market-Level Requirements**
- Capabilities that the market expects from a portfolio-level offering (e.g., a single contract,
  consolidated billing, unified support)
- Compliance and regulatory requirements that apply across the portfolio
- Scalability expectations when a customer adopts the full portfolio

### Prioritizing Portfolio Requirements

Use the same Pragmatic approach as individual product requirements: prioritize by urgency and
pervasiveness. A portfolio requirement is high priority if:
- Many customers cite it as a barrier to expanding their usage across the portfolio
- It directly affects the portfolio's competitive positioning
- It's a prerequisite for the buying process to work at the portfolio level
- It addresses a market problem that spans multiple products

## 5. Portfolio Marketing Plan

The portfolio marketing plan covers awareness, demand generation, and programs at the portfolio
level. It complements (not replaces) individual product marketing plans.

### Structure

**Goals and Metrics**
- Portfolio-level awareness metrics (brand recognition for the portfolio, not just individual
  products)
- Portfolio adoption metrics (percentage of customers using 2+ products, average products per
  customer)
- Cross-sell and upsell revenue targets
- Portfolio win rate (when competing against other portfolios or assembled point solutions)

**Target Audiences**
- Who needs to know about the portfolio? (Often more senior personas than individual product
  audiences)
- What's the message for each audience?
- What channels reach portfolio-level decision makers?

**Programs and Campaigns**
- Portfolio launch or re-launch events
- Cross-product content (case studies showing multi-product value, total cost of ownership
  analyses, integration guides)
- Customer expansion programs (driving adoption of additional products within existing accounts)
- Analyst and influencer relations at the portfolio level

**Budget and Resources**
- Marketing budget allocated specifically to portfolio-level programs (separate from individual
  product marketing)
- Content creation and event support needs
- Agency or partner support requirements

**Calendar and Milestones**
- Key dates aligned with product launches, events, and fiscal planning cycles
- Review cadence for portfolio marketing effectiveness

### Tips for Portfolio Marketing Plans
- Don't just aggregate individual product plans. The portfolio marketing plan should create value
  that individual plans can't — specifically, it should demonstrate why the whole is greater than
  the sum of its parts.
- Customer success stories that show multi-product adoption are the most powerful portfolio
  marketing asset. Invest in creating them.
- Measure portfolio-specific outcomes (cross-sell rate, multi-product adoption) not just
  individual product metrics rolled up.
