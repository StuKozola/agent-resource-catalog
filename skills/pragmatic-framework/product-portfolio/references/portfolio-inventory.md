# Portfolio Inventory Guide

A portfolio inventory is the foundation of all portfolio work. Without a clear picture of what
you have, every strategic decision is guesswork. This guide walks through how to build one.

## What to Capture for Each Product

For every product, service, or partner offering in the portfolio, document:

### Identity
- **Product name** — Official name as the market knows it
- **Description** — One sentence: what it does and for whom
- **Category** — Product, service, add-on, partner solution, platform, etc.
- **Owner** — Who is the product manager or responsible party?

### Market Alignment
- **Target segments** — Which market segments does this product serve?
- **Market problems solved** — List the specific problems (from Market Problems activity)
- **Buyer personas** — Who buys this product?
- **User personas** — Who uses this product?
- **Competitive alternatives** — What do customers use instead if they don't buy this?

### Financial Performance
- **Annual revenue** — Current year and trend (growing, flat, declining)
- **Gross margin** — Profitability after direct costs
- **Revenue concentration** — Is revenue spread across many customers or concentrated in a few?
- **Customer acquisition cost (CAC)** — How expensive is it to win new customers?
- **Lifetime value (LTV)** — What's the long-term value of a customer relationship?

### Lifecycle Position
- **Stage** — Introduction, Growth, Maturity, or Decline
- **Age** — How long has this product been in market?
- **Recent trajectory** — Is it gaining or losing momentum?
- **Key milestones** — Major releases, pivots, or market events in the last 12 months

### Strategic Indicators
- **Distinctive competency alignment** — Does this product leverage the organization's unique
  abilities?
- **Cross-sell / upsell relationships** — Which other products in the portfolio does this
  product connect to?
- **Platform dependencies** — Does this product depend on or enable other products?
- **Strategic importance** — Is this product critical to the portfolio story, or is it legacy?

## Building the Inventory

### If Starting from Scratch

Many organizations don't have a clean inventory. Products accumulate over time through organic
development, acquisitions, and partnerships. Building the inventory often reveals surprises —
products that nobody realized were still being sold, overlapping offerings, or orphaned solutions
with no clear owner.

The pragmatic approach:

1. **Start with what sales sells.** Pull a list of every SKU, product code, or offering that has
   generated revenue in the last 12 months.
2. **Add what's in development.** Include products that are being built but haven't launched yet.
3. **Add partner offerings.** If you resell, bundle, or co-market partner solutions, include them.
4. **Remove duplicates and aliases.** The same product often goes by different names in different
   departments.
5. **Fill in the fields above.** Start with the fields you can populate from existing data
   (financial performance is usually available from finance; market alignment often requires
   product manager interviews).

### If Updating an Existing Inventory

Review and refresh on a quarterly cadence. Focus on:

- Has anything been added or removed since last review?
- Have lifecycle stages changed? (A growth product may have entered maturity.)
- Are financial metrics current?
- Have market problems or competitive dynamics shifted?

## Organizing the Inventory

Once you have the raw data, organize it to reveal patterns:

### By Market Segment
Group products by the segments they serve. This reveals:
- Which segments are well-served vs. underserved
- Where you have overlapping products competing for the same buyers
- Which segments generate the most revenue relative to the number of products

### By Market Problem
Group products by the problems they solve. This reveals:
- Which problems have multiple solutions (potential rationalization)
- Which important problems have no solution (gaps)
- Whether the portfolio covers the full problem space for your target markets

### By Lifecycle Stage
Map products to lifecycle stages. A healthy portfolio has a distribution across stages:
- Too many products in decline = revenue erosion ahead
- Too many in introduction = high investment with uncertain returns
- Too many in maturity = stable but vulnerable to disruption
- A balanced mix = sustainable growth with managed risk

### By Financial Contribution
Rank products by revenue, margin, or growth rate. This reveals:
- Which products are carrying the portfolio financially
- Which products are consuming resources disproportionate to their contribution
- Where the growth is actually coming from

## Output Format

The inventory should be captured in a format that's easy to review and update. A spreadsheet
works well, with each product as a row and the fields above as columns. For leadership
presentations, create summary visualizations:

- **Portfolio map** — A 2x2 or 3x3 matrix plotting products by two strategic dimensions
  (e.g., market growth vs. competitive position)
- **Revenue waterfall** — Stacked view showing each product's contribution to total revenue
- **Lifecycle distribution** — Pie or bar chart showing how many products are in each stage
- **Segment coverage heat map** — Matrix of segments x problems showing which products cover
  which intersections
