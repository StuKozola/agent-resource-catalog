# Assets — Pragmatic Framework (2027)

## Where this fits in the 2027 Pragmatic Framework

- **Category:** Enablement — *Can the people around the product make it succeed?*
- **Focus-area question:** *Do teams have the tools to sell and support the product consistently?*
- **Position:** Below the bar (product- and team-focused)
- **Other focus areas in this category:** Adoption, Content, Channel Support
- **Formerly:** Sales Tools; also takes the case-study material from the retired Advocacy focus area

## What Assets Covers

Assets are the practical tools teams use to **sell, support and explain** the product: case studies, presentations, demos, ROI calculators, templates, messaging guides and playbooks, plus the battle cards, objection handlers and discovery sheets reps use in live conversations. Users include sales, but also presales, customer success, support, partners and executives.

Assets do two jobs: they give teams **consistent messaging**, and they put **the right information in front of people at the right time**. Building them takes product, marketing and sales working together, each contributing what they know — product knows what the product does and where it's going, marketing knows the market and the positioning, sales knows the selling process and what buyers actually ask.

**Assets vs. Content:** Content (`pragmatic-content`) informs buyers and users directly. Assets are the tools teams pick up during a specific moment — a discovery call, a competitive deal, a QBR, a support explanation. Every asset should be built for a specific moment, not as a general-purpose document; a tool that tries to serve every situation serves none well.

## Inputs from Other Focus Areas

- **Positioning** (`pragmatic-positioning`, Planning) — the foundation of every asset and the messaging guide
- **Buyer Personas** (`pragmatic-buyer-personas`, Planning) — problems, language and priorities each asset addresses
- **Buyer Journey** (`pragmatic-buyer-journey`, Planning) — which buying steps need tools
- **Competitive Landscape** and **Win/Loss Analysis** (Market) — competitor strengths, objections and why deals are won or lost
- **Market Problems** (`pragmatic-market-problems`, Market) — the validated problems that create urgency
- **Customer Satisfaction** (`pragmatic-customer-satisfaction`, Go-to-Market) — advocates willing to tell their story for case studies
- **Customer Value Realization** (`pragmatic-customer-value-realization`, Operations) — evidence of customer results for case studies and ROI tools

Assets feed **Engagement** (`pragmatic-engagement`), **Launch** (`pragmatic-launch`), **Organizational Alignment** (`pragmatic-organizational-alignment`) and **Channel Support** (`pragmatic-channel-support`, which adapts assets for partners). If inputs are incomplete, identify the gaps and decide whether to address them first or proceed with what's available.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Map the Moments That Need Tools

Identify where teams need support, aligned to how the market buys and uses the product:
- **Selling moments** — prospecting, discovery, demo, competitive handling, business case, proposal, negotiation, closing
- **Support and explanation moments** — onboarding kickoff, QBRs and renewals, expansion conversations, explaining a limitation or change, partner enablement, executive briefings

Ask where deals stall or are lost, and where customer-facing teams give inconsistent answers. Prioritize those moments. For an existing toolkit, start with `templates/asset-audit-template.md`.

### Step 2: Choose the Asset Type
> Load `references/tool-types.md`.

- **Battle cards**: how to handle a competitor during a call. The competitive analysis behind them comes from `pragmatic-competitive-landscape`, and Assets turns it into the finished card.
- **Objection handling guides** — responses to common pushback
- **ROI calculators / value tools** — quantify the financial case
- **Demo scripts** — modular, organized by problem, not one monolith
- **Prospecting scripts** and **discovery / pain sheets** — persona-specific problem maps and questions
- **Qualification checklists**, **reference and proof-point sheets**, **proposal templates**
- **Case studies** — problem → solution → results stories from real customers
- **Presentations / master decks** — modular decks built from positioning
- **Messaging guides** — approved language for every team
- **Playbooks** — curated sets of assets for a scenario (new segment, displacement, expansion)
- **Customer-facing templates** — onboarding plans, success plans, QBR decks, security answers

### Step 3: Align the Asset to Personas

For each asset decide which persona the user will face, what problems that persona cares about, their language, typical objections and which proof points (case studies, data, references) resonate. An asset for an economic buyer emphasizes different things than one for a technical evaluator or an administrator.

### Step 4: Draft the Asset

Use `references/tool-types.md` for structure and the templates:
- `templates/battle-card-template.md` — competitive battle cards
- `templates/objection-handler-template.md` — objection handling guides
- `templates/case-study-template.md` with `references/case-study-framework.md` — case studies (interview guide, litmus test, approvals)
- `templates/asset-audit-template.md` — auditing asset coverage

Design principles:
1. **One asset, one purpose** — a specific moment and scenario
2. **Modular and navigable** — users jump between sections during live conversations
3. **Skimmable under pressure** — bold key phrases, short blocks
4. **Persona-specific language** — the buyer's vocabulary, not internal jargon
5. **Evidence-backed** — specific proof, not vague claims
6. **Consistent** — every asset traces to the same positioning and messaging guide
7. **Maintained** — review cadence and owner set from the start

### Step 5: Write Usage Guidance

For each asset: when to use it (the trigger), how to navigate it, what to customize per customer, and what not to do (e.g., reading a battle card verbatim). This separates useful enablement from a document dump.

### Step 6: Plan the Asset Lifecycle

- **Review triggers** — competitor moves, launches, pricing changes, new objections from lost deals, new customer evidence
- **Cadence** — quarterly minimum; competitive assets may need monthly updates; case studies refreshed or retired after ~24 months
- **Feedback loop** — how users report what works, what's missing, what's stale
- **Ownership** — named owner per asset group (often product marketing), with product, marketing and sales contributing
- **Measurement** — usage, influence on win rates and cycle time, user satisfaction, consistency of messaging

## Case Studies

Case studies moved here from the retired Advocacy focus area. Customer Satisfaction finds and recruits the customers; Assets turns their stories into tools. Load `references/case-study-framework.md` for the problem → solution → results structure, the litmus test, interview guide, formats and approvals, and use `templates/case-study-template.md`. Use Customer Value Realization evidence to make results concrete, and produce derivatives (one-paragraph summaries, proof-point sheets, deck slides) for other assets.

## Internal products

For internal platforms, assets are what platform teams and champions use to explain and "sell" the platform internally: a short deck on why to adopt, a migration ROI or effort estimate, a messaging guide for champions, internal case studies from early-adopting teams, and FAQs that answer common objections.

## Pragmatic Principles

- **NIHITO** (Nothing Important Happens In The Office) — the best assets come from real buyer and customer conversations, not conference-room assumptions.
- **Outside-in** — reflect how the market buys and uses, not how the company sells.
- **Problems first** — connect every asset to validated market problems, not feature lists.
- **Sell vs. use** — selling assets focus on *why to buy*; how-to material belongs in Content and Adoption.

The Pragmatic Institute **Launch** course covers Assets alongside Launch, Measurement and Organizational Alignment.

## Using AI in this focus area

- Draft first versions of battle cards, objection handlers and messaging guides from approved positioning, competitive research and win/loss notes.
- Turn customer interview transcripts into case study drafts and pull candidate quotes (customer approval still required).
- Check a library of decks and collateral for messaging that has drifted from the current positioning.
- Rules: check AI output against real market evidence — AI can't verify competitor claims or customer results, so confirm them with sources and the customer (NIHITO still applies). Keep sensitive customer, deal and company data, including unapproved customer names and metrics, out of tools not approved for it. The team, not the tool, owns what gets published. Watch for confident but wrong competitive claims; they damage credibility in front of buyers.
