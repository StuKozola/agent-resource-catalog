# Channels — Pragmatic Framework (Support Category)

## Framework Context

**Official Definition:** "Provide needed market and solution information to support channel opportunities and activities."

**Category:** Support (Execution side of the framework)

**Key Distinction:** The Pragmatic Framework separates channel-related work across three activities:
- **Distribution Strategy** (Focus) — Deciding *which* channels align with market buying preferences
- **Channel Training** (Enablement) — Teaching channel partners *how to sell* the product
- **Channels** (Support) — Providing *ongoing market and solution information* to support channel activities

Channels is the operational, day-to-day support function. It ensures that partners, resellers, distributors, and other indirect sales channels have current, relevant market intelligence and solution information to pursue opportunities effectively.

**Roles & Responsibilities (from Pragmatic Institute):**
- Provide ongoing support for the sales channel
- Look for opportunities to make support more repeatable

**Required Skills:**
- Technical product expertise
- Troubleshooting capabilities
- Customer service

## Workflow: Six-Step Channel Support Process

### Step 1: Assess Channel Landscape
Map your current channel ecosystem — who are your partners, what types (resellers, VARs, distributors, SIs, OEMs, referral partners), and what are their information needs?

**Key questions to guide the user:**
- What channel types does the organization use?
- How many active partners exist across each type?
- What is the current state of channel support (ad hoc vs. systematic)?
- Where are the biggest information gaps partners report?

### Step 2: Identify Information Needs
Determine what market and solution information channels need to pursue and close opportunities. Load `references/information-needs.md` for the detailed needs framework.

**Three categories of channel information needs:**
1. **Market Intelligence** — Buyer trends, segment dynamics, competitive movements, industry context
2. **Solution Information** — Product capabilities, technical specs, integration details, roadmap highlights, pricing guidance
3. **Opportunity Support** — Deal-specific competitive positioning, custom use cases, ROI justification, reference customers

### Step 3: Design Delivery Mechanisms
Establish how information reaches channel partners reliably and consistently. Load `references/delivery-mechanisms.md` for detailed guidance on channel communication architecture.

**Core delivery channels:**
- Partner portal / knowledge base (self-service, always-on)
- Regular cadence communications (newsletters, webinars, update calls)
- On-demand support (partner desk, dedicated channel managers)
- Co-selling / deal support (opportunity-specific assistance)

### Step 4: Create Repeatable Processes
Build systematic, scalable approaches to channel support rather than ad hoc responses. This is a core Pragmatic principle for the Channels activity — "look for opportunities to make support more repeatable."

**Repeatable process areas:**
- New product / release information packages
- Competitive update distribution
- Market intelligence summaries
- Deal support request workflows
- Partner feedback collection loops

### Step 5: Build the Channel Support Plan
Assemble findings into a structured plan. Use `templates/channel-support-plan.md` for the document template.

**Plan components:**
- Channel ecosystem map
- Information needs by partner type
- Delivery mechanism matrix
- Content calendar and cadence
- Escalation and deal support processes
- Success metrics and feedback loops

### Step 6: Measure and Optimize
Establish metrics to evaluate channel support effectiveness and continuously improve. Load `references/metrics-optimization.md` for the measurement framework.

**Key metrics:**
- Partner satisfaction with information quality and timeliness
- Channel-sourced pipeline and revenue contribution
- Time-to-information for partner requests
- Content utilization rates in partner portal
- Deal support request volume and resolution time
- Partner retention and engagement rates

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Treating channels like direct sales** — Channel partners have different information needs, time constraints, and motivations than internal sales teams. They represent multiple vendors and have limited mindshare for any single product.
2. **One-size-fits-all support** — Different channel types (VARs vs. distributors vs. SIs) need different depth and types of information. Tier your support model.
3. **Reactive-only support** — Waiting for partners to ask creates delays and frustration. Proactive, cadenced information delivery is essential.
4. **Flooding the channel** — Partners are time-constrained. Curate and prioritize information rather than forwarding everything. Less, better information outperforms more, generic information.
5. **Ignoring the feedback loop** — Channel partners have direct market intelligence. Failing to capture and route their insights back to product and marketing is a missed NIHITO opportunity.
6. **Confusing Channels with Channel Training** — Training teaches partners how to sell. Channels provides the ongoing market and solution information that keeps them current and effective.
7. **No repeatability investment** — Ad hoc support doesn't scale. Every support interaction is an opportunity to create a reusable asset.
8. **Disconnected from upstream activities** — Channel support materials must reflect current positioning, competitive intelligence, and buyer persona insights. Stale information erodes partner trust.
