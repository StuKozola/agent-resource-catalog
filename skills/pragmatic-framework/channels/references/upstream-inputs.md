# Upstream Inputs for Channel Support

The Channels activity sits in the Support category — the execution layer of the Pragmatic Framework. Its effectiveness depends on the quality and currency of inputs from upstream strategic and planning activities.

## Required Inputs

### From Positioning (Planning)
- **Positioning documents by segment and persona** — Channel partners must communicate your value proposition consistently. Without clear positioning, partners improvise messaging, creating brand confusion.
- **Key differentiators** — What makes your solution distinct from alternatives. Partners need crisp, memorable differentiators they can articulate in conversations.
- **25-word positioning statement** — The concise message that anchors all communications.

**If missing or incomplete:** Work with the user to extract at minimum a problem statement, target persona, and primary differentiator. Flag that Positioning work should be prioritized.

### From Buyer Personas (Planning)
- **Buyer persona profiles** — Who the channel partner is selling to, including their role, problems, decision criteria, and buying process.
- **Persona-specific messaging** — How to frame the conversation differently for economic buyers vs. technical evaluators vs. end users.

**If missing or incomplete:** Guide the user to identify at minimum the primary buyer role, their top 2-3 problems, and their typical evaluation criteria.

### From Competitive Landscape (Market)
- **Competitive analysis** — Who/what the competition is, including direct competitors, alternative approaches, and the status quo.
- **Win/loss patterns** — Why deals are won or lost, especially through the channel.
- **Competitive battle cards** — Quick-reference comparison tools for sales conversations.

**If missing or incomplete:** At minimum, identify the top 3 competitors or alternatives that channel partners encounter most frequently. Document basic strengths/weaknesses and talking points.

### From Sales Tools (Enablement)
- **Existing sales collateral** — Presentations, datasheets, demos, ROI calculators, case studies created for direct sales.
- **Sales process documentation** — How the internal sales team sells, which can inform channel partner processes.

**If missing or incomplete:** Inventory whatever exists. Channel-specific versions may need to be created, but starting from direct sales materials is efficient.

### From Channel Training (Enablement)
- **Training curriculum and materials** — What partners have been taught about how to sell the product.
- **Certification requirements** — What knowledge levels are expected before partners engage customers.

**If missing or incomplete:** Document what training has occurred (even informal). Channel support can partially compensate for training gaps through better ongoing information delivery.

### From Distribution Strategy (Focus)
- **Channel strategy** — Which channel types are in use and why they were selected.
- **Partner tier structure** — If partners are tiered (platinum/gold/silver), what each tier receives.
- **Channel economics** — Margin structures, incentives, and partner value proposition.

**If missing or incomplete:** At minimum, document the current channel types in use and the rationale for each.

## Recommended Inputs

### From Market Problems (Market)
- **Validated market problems** — The urgent, pervasive problems the product solves. Partners need to understand problems, not just features.
- **Problem prevalence data** — How common these problems are in different segments, which helps partners qualify opportunities.

### From Win/Loss Analysis (Market)
- **Channel-specific win/loss data** — Patterns in deals won or lost through the channel, which may differ from direct sales patterns.
- **Buying process insights** — What steps buyers take when purchasing through a channel partner vs. direct.

### From Buyer Experience (Planning)
- **Buyer journey map** — The stages buyers go through, including where channel partners play a role.
- **Barriers and friction points** — Where buyers get stuck, especially in channel-assisted purchases.

### From Marketing Plan (Programs)
- **Upcoming campaigns and programs** — What marketing activities are planned that partners should know about or participate in.
- **Lead generation programs** — How leads are generated and routed to channel partners.

## Input Readiness Assessment

Guide the user through a quick assessment:

| Input | Status | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning documents | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | Critical |
| Buyer persona profiles | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | Critical |
| Competitive analysis | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | Critical |
| Sales tools inventory | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | High |
| Channel training materials | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | High |
| Distribution strategy doc | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | High |
| Market problems research | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | Recommended |
| Win/loss data | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | Recommended |
| Buyer journey map | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | Recommended |
| Marketing plan | ___ Available / Partial / Missing | Recommended |

**Triage guidance:**
- If 2+ Critical inputs are missing, recommend pausing Channels work to address them first. Channel support built on weak positioning or unknown personas will be ineffective.
- If Critical inputs are available but Recommended inputs are missing, proceed with Channels work but flag the gaps. The channel support plan should include actions to close these gaps over time.
- If most inputs are available, proceed with full confidence into the six-step workflow.
