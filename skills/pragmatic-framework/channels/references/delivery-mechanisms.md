# Channel Support Delivery Mechanisms

Effective channel support requires a multi-layered delivery architecture that balances proactive communication with responsive support. The goal is to make information easily accessible while minimizing the effort partners must invest to stay current.

## Delivery Architecture: Four Layers

### Layer 1: Self-Service (Always-On)

The foundation of scalable channel support. Partners can access information independently, on their own schedule.

**Partner Portal / Knowledge Base**
- Centralized repository for all channel-facing content
- Organized by topic (product, competitive, market, pricing) and by partner workflow (prospecting, qualifying, proposing, closing, implementing)
- Search-enabled with clear taxonomy and tagging
- Version-controlled — outdated materials are archived, not just supplemented
- Mobile-accessible — partners need information in the field, not just at a desk

**Content types for self-service:**
- Product datasheets and solution briefs
- Competitive battle cards and positioning guides
- Pricing calculators and configuration tools
- Case studies and reference materials
- Sales presentation templates (co-brandable)
- Technical documentation and integration guides
- FAQ documents organized by common partner questions
- Video content — product demos, positioning overviews, technical walkthroughs

**Best practices:**
- Audit content quarterly for accuracy and relevance. Stale content is worse than no content — it erodes trust.
- Track usage analytics to understand what partners access and what they ignore. Low-usage content may indicate a discoverability problem or a relevance problem.
- Provide a "what's new" section or feed so returning partners can quickly see what changed since their last visit.
- Include a feedback mechanism — a simple "Was this helpful?" or content request form.

### Layer 2: Proactive Cadence (Push Communications)

Regularly scheduled communications that push curated information to partners without requiring them to seek it out.

**Communication Cadences**

| Vehicle | Frequency | Content Focus | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partner newsletter | Monthly | Product updates, competitive alerts, success stories, upcoming events | Email with portal links |
| Partner webinar | Quarterly | Market trends, new capabilities, roadmap preview, Q&A | Live webinar with recording |
| Competitive alert | As-needed | Significant competitor moves requiring immediate awareness | Short email or portal alert |
| Release brief | Per release | New features, business impact, talk tracks, known issues | Structured brief document |
| Market intelligence digest | Quarterly | Segment trends, buyer behavior shifts, industry analysis | PDF or portal article |
| Partner success spotlight | Monthly | Partner win stories, best practices, deal patterns | Short-form email or video |

**Best practices:**
- Respect partner attention. Channel partners represent multiple vendors. They will ignore communications that are too frequent, too long, or not relevant.
- Lead with "so what" — every communication should answer "why does this matter to the partner and their customers?" before diving into details.
- Segment communications by partner type and tier when possible. A VAR selling to mid-market doesn't need the same updates as an SI targeting enterprise.
- Include clear calls to action — don't just inform, guide partners on what to do with the information.
- Track open rates, click-through rates, and webinar attendance. Declining engagement signals a content or cadence problem.

### Layer 3: Responsive Support (On-Demand)

Reactive support triggered by partner requests. This layer handles questions, issues, and deal-specific needs that self-service content doesn't cover.

**Support Channels**

| Channel | Best For | Response SLA Target |
|---|---|---|
| Partner help desk / email | General questions, content requests, process inquiries | 4-8 business hours |
| Dedicated channel manager | Strategic partners, complex situations, relationship management | Same business day |
| Technical pre-sales desk | Solution architecture, integration, sizing questions | 4-8 business hours |
| Deal support request form | Opportunity-specific competitive, pricing, or executive engagement | Priority-dependent (see below) |
| Partner Slack / Teams channel | Quick questions, community support, informal communication | Best-effort, community-supported |

**Deal Support Prioritization**

Not all deal support requests deserve equal urgency. Establish a prioritization framework:

| Priority | Criteria | Response Target | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 — Urgent | Large deal, near decision, partner at risk of losing | 4 hours | Competitive displacement in final evaluation |
| P2 — High | Qualified opportunity, active evaluation, specific blocker | 1 business day | Technical architecture question for POC |
| P3 — Standard | Early-stage opportunity, general support need | 2-3 business days | ROI framework for a prospect industry |
| P4 — Low | Informational, no active deal, future planning | 1 week | General competitive positioning question |

**Best practices:**
- Track every request — even informal ones. Request patterns reveal opportunities for proactive content (repeatability).
- Close the loop — always follow up on resolved requests. Did the information help? Did they win the deal?
- Route feedback to the appropriate upstream activity. If partners repeatedly ask about a competitor you haven't analyzed, that's a signal for Competitive Landscape.
- Maintain an internal knowledge base of past responses to enable consistency and speed.

### Layer 4: Strategic Engagement (High-Touch)

Deep, relationship-driven engagement reserved for top-tier partners and strategic situations.

**Engagement Types**

| Engagement | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Quarterly Business Review (QBR) | Quarterly | Review pipeline, performance, market insights, joint planning |
| Partner Advisory Council | Semi-annual | Gather strategic feedback, share roadmap, co-create go-to-market |
| Executive Briefing | Annual or event-driven | Align on strategic direction, deepen executive relationship |
| Joint Account Planning | As-needed | Collaborative planning for specific target accounts |
| Co-selling Sessions | Deal-driven | Vendor SMEs join partner-led customer meetings |

**Best practices:**
- QBRs should be bidirectional — not just a scorecard review. Use them as NIHITO opportunities to gather market intelligence from partners.
- Partner advisory councils are gold mines for understanding channel needs. Structure them to surface honest feedback, not just validate existing plans.
- Executive engagement should be earned and meaningful. Don't waste partner executive time on routine updates.

## Building the Delivery Matrix

Map each information need to the appropriate delivery mechanism(s):

| Information Need | Self-Service | Proactive | Responsive | Strategic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product updates | Portal content | Release briefs, newsletter | Help desk | QBR roadmap review |
| Competitive intel | Battle cards | Competitive alerts | Deal-specific positioning | Advisory council input |
| Market trends | Portal articles | Quarterly digest, webinar | — | QBR market briefing |
| Pricing guidance | Calculator, price list | Promotion announcements | Pricing exception requests | Joint account planning |
| Use cases / stories | Case study library | Success spotlights | Custom use case development | Joint case study creation |
| Technical specs | Documentation library | Release notes | Pre-sales desk | Joint solution design |
| Deal support | — | — | Deal support request | Co-selling sessions |

## Scalability Considerations

As the channel grows, support must scale without proportionally increasing headcount.

**Scaling strategies:**
- Invest in self-service quality — every question that self-service answers is a question the support team doesn't handle
- Template and templatize — create reusable templates for common deliverables (competitive positioning, ROI frameworks, proposal sections)
- Enable partner-to-partner learning — partner communities, peer forums, and best-practice sharing reduce vendor support burden
- Automate cadence communications — use marketing automation or partner portal tools to systematize push communications
- Create a channel support playbook — document processes so new channel team members ramp quickly and deliver consistent support
