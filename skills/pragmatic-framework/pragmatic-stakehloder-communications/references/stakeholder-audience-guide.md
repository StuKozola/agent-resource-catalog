## Executive Leadership

**Who they are**: C-suite, VP-level, and senior directors who are accountable for product P&L, company strategy, and resource allocation.

**What they need**:
- Strategic alignment between product direction and company goals
- Business impact: revenue, market share, competitive position
- Status vs. goals — are we on track?
- Risks that require executive decisions or escalation
- The "why" behind major priority changes

**What they do NOT need**:
- Feature-level detail
- Sprint metrics or development status
- Internal team dynamics

**Best formats**:
- Monthly executive briefing document (1–2 pages max)
- Quarterly business review (QBR) slide
- Brief Slack/email for significant out-of-cycle updates

**Communication triggers**:
- Monthly cadence update
- Missed or at-risk milestone
- Major priority change or scope reduction
- Significant competitive development
- Budget or resource request

**Pragmatic principle**: Executives make investment decisions. Frame communications in terms of market opportunity, risk, and business impact — not features.

## Development / Engineering

**Who they are**: Engineering managers, tech leads, developers, and architects who build the product.

**What they need**:
- Market context for what they're building — the problem it solves, for whom
- Priority rationale when requirements change
- Clarity on what is in vs. out of scope
- Advance notice of major scope changes
- Recognition that what they ship solves real market problems

**What they do NOT need**:
- Sales pipeline data
- Marketing campaign details
- Business P&L information

**Best formats**:
- Sprint planning context notes (brief market problem framing)
- Written requirements with persona and problem context
- Face-to-face or team meeting for major priority or scope changes

**Communication triggers**:
- Sprint or release planning
- Requirements update or change
- Scope change or reprioritization
- Market feedback that validates (or changes) direction

**Pragmatic principle**: Engineering teams that understand the market problem make better implementation decisions. Share NIHITO-grounded context — the voice of the market, not just a ticket.

## Sales

**Who they are**: Account executives, SDRs, sales managers, and sales engineers who sell the product to prospects and customers.

**What they need**:
- What is shipping and when (rough timing, not hard commitments)
- Which market problems each new capability solves
- Competitive context — how does this strengthen our position?
- Which personas and segments benefit most
- What to say (and not say) to customers asking about the roadmap

**What they do NOT need**:
- Feature implementation details
- Sprint schedules
- Internal roadmap debates

**Best formats**:
- Bi-weekly or monthly sales update email
- Roadmap overview (phase-driven, not date-committed)
- Competitive battle card updates as relevant
- Q&A session before major releases

**Communication triggers**:
- Upcoming release (4–6 weeks ahead)
- Competitive landscape change
- Roadmap reprioritization that affects customer conversations
- Launch readiness review

**Pragmatic principle**: Sales needs to have accurate, consistent conversations with customers. Communicate direction without making commitments that product can't keep.

## Product Marketing

**Who they are**: PMMs, marketing managers, and demand gen teams who develop positioning, messaging, campaigns, and launch plans.

**What they need**:
- Roadmap themes and feature details for campaign planning
- Target personas and segments for each release
- Market problems being solved (for messaging)
- Release timing with enough lead time to plan campaigns
- Positioning decisions and rationale

**What they do NOT need**:
- Sprint-level development status
- Internal engineering architecture discussions

**Best formats**:
- Monthly PM–PMM sync
- Launch readiness briefings (4–6 weeks before release)
- Positioning document updates
- Shared roadmap view with release themes

**Communication triggers**:
- Roadmap planning cycle
- Upcoming release (6–8 weeks ahead for major releases)
- New positioning or messaging decisions
- Launch plan kickoff

**Pragmatic principle**: PMMs translate product capabilities into market messages. Give them the market-problem context they need to write positioning that resonates, not just a feature list.

## Customer Success / Support

**Who they are**: CSMs, account managers, and support specialists who work directly with customers post-sale.

**What they need**:
- What is changing in the product and when
- Which customers will be affected (positively or negatively)
- How to explain changes to customers
- Known issues or limitations in upcoming releases
- Which market problems each change addresses

**What they do NOT need**:
- Strategic roadmap planning context
- Financial projections

**Best formats**:
- Release notes (internal, ahead of customer-facing release notes)
- Feature briefing for major changes
- Brief Slack/email for urgent customer-affecting changes

**Communication triggers**:
- Upcoming release (2–4 weeks ahead)
- Changes that affect existing customer workflows
- Known issues or limitations that customers may encounter
- Major deprecation or sunset of features

**Pragmatic principle**: CS and Support are on the front lines of customer trust. They need enough context to be honest and helpful — never let them be surprised by what ships.

## Product Operations

**Who they are**: Product ops managers, program managers, and delivery leads who coordinate process, workflow, and cross-functional dependencies.

**What they need**:
- Release schedule and milestone status
- Cross-functional dependencies
- Process changes affecting product workflows
- Metrics and measurement frameworks

**Best formats**:
- Shared release calendar
- Dependency matrix
- Operations sync meeting

**Communication triggers**:
- Release planning
- Dependency changes
- Process updates

## Key Customers / Design Partners

**Who they are**: Strategic accounts or design partners who have an ongoing relationship with the product team — often involved in research, beta programs, or advisory boards.

**What they need**:
- Strategic direction — what problems is the team focused on solving?
- Roadmap themes (always as plans, never commitments)
- Opportunity to provide input before decisions are finalized
- Early access to betas when relevant

**What they do NOT need**:
- Internal team details
- Specific delivery dates (avoid committing)
- Competitive strategy details

**Best formats**:
- Quarterly roadmap conversation (not a presentation — a dialogue)
- Advisory board participation
- Beta program invitations

**Communication triggers**:
- Quarterly cadence
- Major strategic direction update
- Beta or early access availability
- When their specific problems are being addressed

**Pragmatic principle**: Customer communication about the roadmap is a feedback mechanism, not just a status update. Come prepared to listen as much as to share.

## Channel Partners

**Who they are**: Resellers, ISVs, system integrators, and technology partners who sell or integrate with the product.

**What they need**:
- Roadmap direction that affects integrations or their sales motion
- Release timing for integration planning
- Enablement content for new capabilities
- Competitive context

**Best formats**:
- Partner newsletter or update
- Quarterly partner briefing
- Integration and enablement guides at launch

**Communication triggers**:
- Roadmap planning cycle
- API or integration changes
- Major release
- Partner conference or event

## Communication Frequency Reference

| Audience | Routine Cadence | Trigger-Based |
|---|---|---|
| Executives | Monthly briefing | Milestone risk, major change, budget ask |
| Engineering | Per sprint | Reprioritization, scope change |
| Sales | Bi-weekly or monthly | Upcoming release (4–6 wks), competitive change |
| Product Marketing | Monthly sync | Release (6–8 wks ahead), positioning update |
| Customer Success | Per release | Customer-impacting change |
| Key Customers | Quarterly | Beta access, major direction change |
| Channel Partners | Quarterly | Integration change, major release |
