# Programs Guide — Comprehensive Reference for All Eight Programs Activities

This reference provides detailed guidance for each of the eight activities in the Pragmatic Framework's Programs category. Each section includes the official definition, responsibilities and skills from the Pragmatic Roles & Responsibilities document, strategic context, practical guidance, common mistakes, and integration points.

## 1. Marketing Plan

### Official Definition
Articulate the strategies and tactics for generating awareness and leads for the upcoming fiscal period, including key programs and events with measurements and goals.

### Responsibilities (Pragmatic Framework)
- Articulate the strategies and programs that will generate awareness and leads for the upcoming fiscal period — including measurements and goals

### Required Skills
- Analytical, with the ability to gather data, synthesize the data and tell a story
- Strategic thinking
- Internal influencer
- Written and verbal communications
- Presentation capabilities

### Strategic Context

The Marketing Plan is the strategic umbrella for the entire Programs category. While each sibling activity focuses on a specific execution area, the Marketing Plan ties them together into a cohesive, fiscally bounded strategy with shared goals and measurements.

A Pragmatic marketing plan is fundamentally different from an inside-out plan because it starts with market knowledge — positioning, buyer personas, buyer experience, and competitive landscape — rather than product features or internal priorities.

### Core Components

**Strategic Foundation:**
- Target market segments and their relative priority
- Positioning pillars with supporting evidence
- Competitive differentiation strategy
- Key challenges and risks with strategic responses

**Program-to-Buyer-Journey Map:**
The central discipline is mapping programs to the buyer experience. Every program should answer three questions: (1) Which persona does it reach? (2) At what buyer journey stage does it engage them? (3) What message does it deliver, traced back to positioning?

Build a matrix with journey stages as columns and personas as rows. For each cell, note current programs, gaps, and priorities.

**Budget Allocation:**
Allocate by expected impact, not historical precedent. Categories to budget across: personnel, technology/tools, content production, paid media, events, agency/contractor fees, and contingency.

**Calendar and Milestones:**
Sequence programs across the fiscal period, accounting for product roadmap timing, seasonal patterns, industry events, and competitor activity windows.

**Measurement Framework:**
Define metrics at three tiers: Tier 1 business outcomes (revenue, pipeline, CAC, ROI), Tier 2 pipeline/conversion metrics (MQLs, SQLs, conversion rates, velocity), and Tier 3 activity metrics (impressions, clicks, downloads).

### Common Mistakes
- Building the plan around products instead of market problems
- No connection between programs and buyer journey stages
- Measuring activities (emails sent) instead of outcomes (pipeline created)
- Setting budgets based on last year's allocation instead of this year's opportunity
- Creating the plan in isolation without sales alignment

## 2. Revenue Growth

### Official Definition
Define the specific plans and budgets for selling products and services to new customers.

### Responsibilities (Pragmatic Framework)
- Define specific plans and budgets for selling products and services to new customers

### Required Skills
- Analytical (ability to gather data, synthesize the data and tell a story)
- Strategic thinking
- Deep understanding of the market and market problems
- Understanding of field sales and field marketing support

### Strategic Context

Revenue Growth focuses exclusively on new customer acquisition — the "land" part of a land-and-expand strategy. This is distinct from Revenue Retention, which focuses on existing customers. The division is intentional: the strategies, tactics, economics, and metrics for acquiring new customers are fundamentally different from retaining and expanding existing ones.

Revenue Growth requires a deep understanding of how prospects become customers. This means knowing the sales channels, the buying process, and the economics of acquisition. Pragmatic Institute emphasizes that revenue forecasts should be built bottom-up from market realities, not top-down from wishful thinking.

### The Acquisition Engine

**Funnel Math Model:**
Revenue Growth plans require a quantitative model that works backward from the revenue target:

- Revenue target ÷ Average deal size = Deals needed
- Deals needed ÷ Win rate = Opportunities needed
- Opportunities needed ÷ SQL-to-opportunity rate = SQLs needed
- SQLs needed ÷ MQL-to-SQL rate = MQLs needed
- MQLs needed ÷ Lead-to-MQL rate = Leads needed

This model reveals the volume of marketing activity required and helps identify which funnel stages are bottlenecks.

**Pipeline Coverage Ratio:**
Healthy pipeline coverage is typically 3x to 4x the revenue target. If pipeline coverage drops below 3x, new customer acquisition becomes at risk regardless of win rate.

**Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):**
CAC = Total Sales & Marketing Spend ÷ Number of New Customers Acquired. Track CAC by segment, channel, and program to identify the most efficient acquisition paths. A healthy CLV:CAC ratio is generally 3:1 or higher.

### Channel Strategy for New Customers

The acquisition strategy should align with the distribution strategy defined in the Focus category. Common channels include direct sales, inside sales, channel partners, self-service/PLG (product-led growth), and marketplace.

For each channel, define: target personas served, expected deal size, sales cycle length, required marketing support, and cost of acquisition.

### Revenue Forecasting Fundamentals

Pragmatic Institute emphasizes building credible revenue budgets based on market realities:

- Understand thoroughly how sales channels work and how prospects become customers
- Gather credible industry information and customer feedback to answer questions about spending, growth rates, and competitive dynamics
- Use both top-down (market growth rate applied to current revenue) and bottom-up (units × price × productivity × seasonality) approaches
- Substantiate assumptions with market facts and reliable trend information
- Acknowledge the tension between executive ambition and achievable targets

### Integration Points
- **Awareness** feeds the top of the Revenue Growth funnel
- **Nurturing** converts awareness into qualified pipeline
- **Launch** represents a concentrated Revenue Growth event for new products
- **Sales Alignment** (Enablement) ensures the selling process matches the buying process
- **Pricing** (Business) directly impacts deal economics and CAC

### Common Mistakes
- Setting targets top-down without a credible bottom-up model
- Not distinguishing new-customer economics from expansion economics
- Ignoring CAC payback period — acquiring customers who take too long to become profitable
- Over-investing in one funnel stage while starving others
- No pipeline coverage discipline — realizing too late that the pipeline is insufficient

## 3. Revenue Retention

### Official Definition
Define the specific plans and budgets for ensuring customer loyalty, as well as selling products and services to existing customers.

### Responsibilities (Pragmatic Framework)
- Define specific plans and budgets for ensuring customer loyalty and selling additional products and services to existing customers

### Required Skills
- Analytical, with the ability to gather data, synthesize the data and tell a story
- Strategic thinking
- Shows understanding of customer base and the problems they face
- Written and verbal communications

### Strategic Context

Revenue Retention addresses the post-acquisition lifecycle — keeping customers, deepening their engagement, and expanding their spend. In subscription and SaaS models, revenue retention is often more valuable than new customer acquisition because the cost to retain is far lower than the cost to acquire.

The Pragmatic approach to retention starts with understanding customer problems — the same outside-in thinking applied to prospects also applies to existing customers. What problems do they face now? How has their context changed since purchase? What unmet needs create expansion opportunities?

### Key Metrics

**Net Revenue Retention (NRR):**
NRR = (Starting MRR + Expansion - Contraction - Churn) ÷ Starting MRR. NRR above 100% means the existing customer base is growing even without new customers. Best-in-class SaaS companies target 110%–130%+ NRR.

**Gross Revenue Retention (GRR):**
GRR = (Starting MRR - Contraction - Churn) ÷ Starting MRR. GRR strips out expansion to show the baseline health of the customer base. GRR above 90% is generally healthy.

**Expansion Revenue:**
Revenue from upsell (higher tier/plan), cross-sell (additional products), and add-on purchases from existing customers.

**Churn Rate:**
Logo churn (percentage of customers lost) and revenue churn (percentage of revenue lost). Revenue churn is usually more informative because it weights by customer value.

### Retention Program Architecture

**Onboarding and Time-to-Value:**
The most critical retention window is the first 90 days. Programs that accelerate time-to-first-value dramatically reduce early churn. Include welcome sequences, getting-started guides, milestone celebrations, and proactive outreach at key adoption thresholds.

**Adoption and Engagement:**
Ongoing programs that deepen product usage: customer education, training programs, user community building, best-practice sharing, and regular product update communications.

**Health Monitoring:**
Establish customer health scores based on leading indicators: product usage frequency, feature breadth, support ticket trends, NPS/CSAT scores, engagement with communications, and contract status. Use health scores to trigger proactive interventions.

**Churn Prevention:**
Programs targeting at-risk customers identified by declining health scores: executive outreach, success planning sessions, training refreshers, product configuration reviews, and escalation protocols.

**Expansion Programs:**
Upsell and cross-sell programs based on customer usage patterns and unmet needs: usage-based upgrade triggers, complementary product recommendations, customer advisory boards for roadmap input, and expansion-specific campaigns.

**Renewal Programs:**
Structured renewal process beginning 90–120 days before contract end: value realization reviews, upcoming roadmap previews, multi-year incentives, and executive engagement.

### Customer Segmentation for Retention

Not all customers warrant the same retention investment. Segment by:
- **Revenue tier** — High-value customers get high-touch programs; lower-value customers get scaled programs
- **Health score** — At-risk customers need intervention; healthy customers need engagement
- **Growth potential** — Customers with expansion headroom get targeted growth programs
- **Strategic value** — Customers whose logos, industries, or use cases provide disproportionate marketing or reference value

### Integration Points
- **Advocacy** converts retained, satisfied customers into active advocates
- **Revenue Growth** depends on a strong retention base — high churn negates acquisition investment
- **Product Profitability** (Business) reflects the combined effect of growth and retention economics
- **Win/Loss Analysis** (Market) should include expansion wins/losses, not just new business

### Common Mistakes
- Treating all customers the same regardless of value or risk profile
- Measuring only lagging indicators (churn) instead of leading indicators (health scores)
- No structured onboarding program — assuming customers will figure it out themselves
- Conflating customer satisfaction (CSAT/NPS) with retention — satisfied customers still churn
- Ignoring contraction (downgrades) as a signal of coming churn

## 4. Launch

### Official Definition
Plan, execute and measure effectiveness of strategic launches.

### Responsibilities (Pragmatic Framework)
- Define specific launch goals and strategies
- Develop launch plans and supporting documentation
- Ensure cross-organizational readiness
- Track and communicate launch status
- Build and lead cross-functional team

### Required Skills
- Ability to inspire others to action with or without implicit authority
- Delegation ability
- Communication skills and ability to effectively pivot communication based on audience
- Time-management and project management
- Ability to work and empathize across internal departments

### Strategic Context

Launch is a concentrated, cross-functional effort to introduce something new to the market. The Pragmatic approach emphasizes that launches should be tiered based on market impact, not on internal excitement about features. A launch is a coordinated execution event that touches marketing, sales, product, support, and channels simultaneously.

### Launch Tier Classification

**Tier 1 — Major Launch:** New product, new market entry, significant repositioning, or transformational product release. Requires full cross-functional coordination, external PR, analyst briefings, sales training, event support, and dedicated measurement.

**Tier 2 — Minor Launch:** Significant feature release, new pricing/packaging, new integration, or new use case enablement. Requires marketing content, sales enablement, customer communication, and selective external promotion.

**Tier 3 — Maintenance Launch:** Bug fixes, minor enhancements, routine updates. Requires customer notification and documentation updates. Minimal marketing involvement.

### Launch Plan Components

- Launch goals tied to business outcomes (pipeline generated, adoption rate, win rate change)
- Target audience definition (which personas, which segments, existing vs. new customers)
- Messaging and positioning for this launch (derived from core positioning)
- Content and asset plan (what needs to be created, by when)
- Sales enablement plan (training, tools, competitive guidance)
- Channel and partner communication plan
- Customer communication plan (for existing customers)
- PR and analyst relations plan (for Tier 1)
- Internal communication plan
- Timeline with milestones and owners
- Measurement plan with specific KPIs

### Common Mistakes
- Treating every release as a Tier 1 launch (launch fatigue)
- Launching to the market before sales is enabled
- Measuring launch success by vanity metrics (press mentions) instead of business outcomes (pipeline)
- No post-launch review to capture lessons learned
- Not defining launch goals upfront — making it impossible to measure success

## 5. Awareness

### Official Definition
Develop programs to raise the profile and awareness of your brand within strategic market segments to bring more prospects into your funnel.

### Responsibilities (Pragmatic Framework)
- Create and deliver thoughtful information to influence key market constituents
- Influence key audiences via strong content
- Deliver presentations to internal and external audiences

### Required Skills
- Written and verbal communications
- Pitch artist capabilities
- Storytelling
- Ability to build street credibility
- Strong understanding of the industry, the market and market problems

### Strategic Context

Awareness is the top-of-funnel engine. Its purpose is not to generate immediate leads but to build the brand's visibility, credibility, and trust within target market segments. The Pragmatic approach to awareness is distinctly market-problem-centric: you build awareness by demonstrating deep understanding of market problems, not by talking about your product.

Pragmatic Institute teaches that thought leadership is the most powerful awareness tool. Today's buyers are a research generation — they review information, visit multiple sources, and build opinions long before they ever talk to a vendor. The brands that earn trust during this research phase have a structural advantage when buyers enter the evaluation stage.

### Awareness Program Types

**Thought Leadership:**
Original research, expert commentary, trend analysis, and perspectives on market problems that demonstrate your organization's depth of understanding. Thought leadership should focus on problems your market cares about, even if those problems extend beyond what your product solves directly. This builds credibility and positions the brand as a trusted advisor.

Formats: articles, research reports, whitepapers, podcasts, speaking engagements, contributed articles in industry publications, webinars, and video content.

**Brand Campaigns:**
Targeted campaigns designed to increase aided and unaided brand recall within specific market segments. These should be grounded in positioning — the brand promise should connect to the market problems you solve.

Channels: industry publications, digital advertising (targeted by persona/segment), social media in communities where target personas gather, sponsorships, and PR.

**Industry and Analyst Relations:**
Engagement with industry analysts, influencers, and media who shape market perceptions. Analyst briefings, contributed research, and participation in industry reports (Gartner, Forrester, IDC, etc.) influence buyers who consult these sources during the research phase.

**Event Presence:**
Speaking slots at industry conferences (preferred over booth sponsorships for thought leadership), hosted events, webinars, and community meetups. The goal is to be present and visible where target personas congregate.

**SEO and Content Marketing:**
Content optimized for problem-oriented keywords (not product keywords) that attracts buyers in the early research phase. This creates a sustainable awareness engine that compounds over time.

### The Awareness-to-Nurturing Handoff

Awareness programs should create pathways into the nurturing funnel. This does not mean gating all content behind lead forms — Pragmatic's evolved thinking, especially in product-led contexts, recognizes that ungated content spreads further and builds more trust. The handoff happens when a prospect self-selects into deeper engagement: subscribing, downloading, attending, requesting a demo, or starting a trial.

### Measuring Awareness

Awareness is notoriously difficult to measure, but it is not immeasurable. Focus on:
- **Reach within target segments** — Not total impressions, but impressions among people who match your buyer personas
- **Share of voice** — How visible is your brand relative to competitors in target channels
- **Brand recall surveys** — Periodic surveys measuring aided and unaided recall in target segments
- **Website traffic from target segments** — Traffic quality matters more than traffic volume
- **Content engagement** — Downloads, time on page, social shares, and backlinks to thought leadership content
- **Speaking and media placements** — Quantity and quality of earned speaking slots and media coverage

### Common Mistakes
- Talking about your product instead of market problems (inside-out messaging)
- Measuring total reach instead of reach within target segments
- No long-term content strategy — awareness requires sustained investment, not one-off campaigns
- Confusing awareness with demand generation — awareness builds the brand, nurturing generates demand
- Ignoring earned media and thought leadership in favor of paid media alone

## 6. Nurturing

### Official Definition
Develop programs to move prospects quickly and effectively through the funnel, with the objective of turning prospects into satisfied customers.

### Responsibilities (Pragmatic Framework)
- Develop a lead nurturing process to turn prospects into satisfied customers and upsell or cross-sell existing customers on new products and services

### Required Skills
- Understanding of business goals and market problems
- Analytics and measurement focus
- Digital marketing

### Strategic Context

Nurturing is the middle-of-funnel engine that converts awareness-generated interest into qualified pipeline. The Pragmatic approach emphasizes that nurturing should be aligned to the buyer experience — the buying process that buyers actually follow, not the selling process that the company prefers.

Nurturing is not email automation, though email is often a channel. True nurturing means providing the right content, to the right persona, at the right stage of their buying journey, through the right channel, at the right time. Every touchpoint should help the prospect make progress toward a decision.

### The Nurturing Framework

**Stage 1: Problem Recognition → Problem Prioritization**
The prospect knows they have a problem but hasn't committed to solving it. Nurturing at this stage focuses on problem amplification: helping the prospect understand the cost of inaction and the urgency of the problem.

Content: educational content, benchmark data, ROI calculators, peer comparison data, industry trend analysis.

**Stage 2: Solution Research → Solution Evaluation**
The prospect is actively researching solutions. Nurturing here focuses on helping them evaluate effectively, while positioning your offering favorably.

Content: comparison guides, solution architecture overviews, analyst reports, customer case studies, demo invitations, webinars with customer speakers.

**Stage 3: Vendor Selection → Purchase Decision**
The prospect is evaluating specific vendors. Nurturing supports the sales process with proof points, competitive differentiation, and buying justification materials.

Content: customer references, ROI case studies, security/compliance documentation, implementation guides, executive briefings.

### Lead Scoring and Qualification

**Demographic/Firmographic Fit:**
Does this lead match your buyer personas and target segments? Score based on title, role, company size, industry, and geography.

**Behavioral Engagement:**
What actions has the lead taken? Score based on content consumed (and its funnel stage), website visits, email engagement, event attendance, and product trial activity.

**MQL and SQL Definitions:**
MQL (Marketing Qualified Lead): A lead that has met both fit and engagement thresholds. SQL (Sales Qualified Lead): An MQL that sales has accepted and confirmed as a genuine opportunity.

The MQL→SQL handoff is a critical integration point between marketing and sales. Both teams must agree on definitions, SLAs (how quickly sales follows up), and feedback loops (sales reports back on lead quality).

### Product-Led Nurturing

Pragmatic Institute recognizes the evolution toward product-led go-to-market, where free trials and freemium products replace lead forms as the primary entry point. In this model, nurturing shifts from email-based content sequences to in-product behavioral triggers: onboarding guidance, feature discovery prompts, usage milestone celebrations, and upgrade prompts based on actual product usage.

Product-led nurturing is more effective because it is based on behavioral signals with higher buying intent than traditional marketing engagement signals.

### The Sales Handoff

The moment a nurtured lead becomes sales-ready is one of the most important transitions in the go-to-market process. Define:
- Trigger criteria (what combination of fit + engagement + behavior signals a handoff)
- Handoff process (how is the lead routed, with what context)
- SLA (how quickly must sales respond)
- Feedback loop (how does sales report back on lead quality)
- Recycling process (what happens to leads that sales rejects — they go back into nurturing, not into a black hole)

### Measuring Nurturing

- MQL volume and conversion rate (lead → MQL)
- SQL volume and conversion rate (MQL → SQL)
- Lead velocity rate (month-over-month growth in qualified leads)
- Funnel stage conversion rates at each stage
- Time in stage (how long leads spend at each funnel stage)
- Nurture engagement metrics (email open/click rates, content consumption, event attendance)
- Lead recycling rate (what percentage of leads rejected by sales re-enter nurturing)
- Cost per MQL and cost per SQL

### Common Mistakes
- Treating nurturing as "email blast automation" instead of buyer-journey-aligned engagement
- No lead scoring — sending unqualified leads to sales, destroying credibility
- No sales feedback loop — marketing never learns whether leads are good or bad
- Same nurturing path for all personas — a CTO and a procurement manager need different content
- Gating all content — reducing reach and trust-building for marginal data capture
- No lead recycling — leads that aren't sales-ready yet are abandoned instead of re-nurtured

## 7. Advocacy

### Official Definition
Identify customers who are willing to give testimonials, case studies and references and amplify their voice in the market.

### Responsibilities (Pragmatic Framework)
- Identify customers who are willing to participate in testimonials, case studies or reference accounts
- Capture and share stories of customer success
- Utilize advocates in a variety of programs including events and thought leadership

### Required Skills
- Analytical, with the ability to gather data, synthesize the data and tell a story
- Sales-oriented thinking
- Written and verbal communications

### Strategic Context

Advocacy is the activity that converts customer satisfaction into marketing leverage. Buyers trust other buyers more than they trust vendors. A structured advocacy program creates a systematic pipeline of customer stories, references, and testimonials that feed awareness, nurturing, and sales programs.

The Pragmatic approach emphasizes amplifying customer voices in the market — not crafting vendor-friendly narratives and attributing them to customers. Authentic customer stories, told in the customer's language and focused on the problems they solved, are far more credible than polished vendor case studies.

### Advocacy Program Components

**Customer Reference Program:**
A structured system for identifying, qualifying, and managing customers willing to speak with prospects. Key elements:

- Nomination process (how are potential references identified — NPS scores, success milestones, CSM nominations, usage metrics)
- Qualification criteria (what makes a good reference — measurable results, articulate spokesperson, relevant industry/use case)
- Consent and permissions (formal opt-in with clear expectations about participation scope, frequency, and information shared)
- Segmented database (organized by industry, use case, persona, company size, and participation type)
- Matching process (how reference requests from sales are matched to appropriate advocates)
- Usage tracking (prevent advocate fatigue by tracking how often each reference is contacted)
- Recognition and reciprocity (thank-you notes, gifts, early access, advisory board invitations, conference invitations)

**Case Studies and Customer Stories:**
Written, video, or audio content that documents customer success. Pragmatic's approach focuses case studies on the market problem, not the product:

Structure: (1) The customer's situation and the problem they faced — described in terms any buyer in that segment would recognize. (2) Why they chose to act and what alternatives they considered. (3) How they solved the problem (featuring your product). (4) Measurable outcomes and business impact.

**Testimonials:**
Shorter-form endorsements used in marketing materials, website, and sales collateral. Effective testimonials are specific (not vague praise), attributed (real name, title, company), and focused on outcomes.

**Online Reviews:**
Programs encouraging satisfied customers to leave reviews on relevant platforms (G2, TrustRadius, Gartner Peer Insights, etc.). Review programs should be opt-in and should never incentivize specific ratings.

**Customer Speakers and Events:**
Recruiting customers to co-present at industry conferences, webinars, and company events. Customer speakers are far more credible than vendor speakers for prospect audiences.

**Customer Advisory Boards:**
Formalized groups of customers who provide input on product roadmap, strategy, and go-to-market. Advisory boards serve dual purposes: genuine product input and deepened customer relationships that often produce advocacy.

### Building the Case Study — Pragmatic Best Practices

Pragmatic Institute's "Case Study Litmus Test" evaluates whether a case study is effective:

- Does it describe a real, recognizable market problem?
- Is the customer clearly identified (not anonymous)?
- Are outcomes quantified with specific metrics?
- Would a prospect in the same segment see themselves in this story?
- Does it focus on the problem and outcome, not on product features?

### Activating Advocates Across Programs

Advocacy assets should be deployed systematically, not stored in a library:
- **Awareness** — Customer speakers at events, customer-authored thought leadership, social proof on website
- **Nurturing** — Case studies aligned to buyer journey stages, customer webinars, peer-to-peer connections
- **Revenue Growth** — References for sales teams, competitive displacement stories, ROI proof points
- **Revenue Retention** — Customer success stories shared with at-risk or expansion-opportunity accounts
- **Launch** — Customer beta stories and early-adopter testimonials

### Preventing Advocate Fatigue

The most common failure mode of advocacy programs is over-relying on a small number of advocates. Mitigation strategies:
- Track usage frequency per advocate and set maximum contact thresholds
- Continuously recruit new advocates — advocacy sourcing should be an always-on process
- Vary the ask — rotate between different participation types (quick testimonial vs. full case study vs. reference call)
- Provide meaningful recognition and reciprocity commensurate with the ask

### Measuring Advocacy

- Number of active advocates (willing and available)
- Advocate coverage by segment, industry, and use case
- Stories produced (case studies, testimonials, video stories)
- Referral-sourced pipeline (pipeline that originated from a customer referral)
- Reference utilization rate (percentage of sales deals that use a reference)
- Win rate impact (win rate on deals with reference vs. without)
- Advocate satisfaction and retention (are advocates staying in the program)
- Review volume and rating trends on third-party platforms

### Common Mistakes
- Only asking for advocacy when sales urgently needs a reference (reactive, not programmatic)
- Over-relying on a few "hero" advocates until they burn out
- Writing case studies about product features instead of market problems and business outcomes
- No formal consent or expectation-setting — surprising customers with requests
- No tracking system — losing track of who has been contacted and how often
- Not reciprocating — expecting advocacy without providing value back to the advocate

## 8. Measurement

### Official Definition
Measure and tune product marketing programs to ensure alignment with corporate goals.

### Responsibilities (Pragmatic Framework)
- Measure and tune product efforts to ensure alignment with corporate goals

### Required Skills
- Strategic thinking
- Math acumen
- Data management
- Written and verbal communications
- Excellent presentation abilities
- Analytical, with the ability to gather data, synthesize the data and tell a story

### Strategic Context

Measurement is the closed-loop feedback mechanism for the entire Programs category. The emphasis on "tune" is critical — measurement without adjustment is just reporting. The Pragmatic approach demands that every metric connect to a corporate goal, and that the measurement process include defined cadences for reviewing data and making program adjustments.

### The Metrics Hierarchy

**Tier 1 — Business Outcomes (Lagging Indicators):**
Revenue from marketing-sourced and marketing-influenced pipeline, CAC, marketing ROI, pipeline contribution, win rate on marketing-sourced leads, and NRR.

**Tier 2 — Pipeline and Conversion (Leading Indicators):**
MQL/SQL volume and conversion rates, pipeline coverage ratio, lead velocity rate, average sales cycle length, and funnel stage conversion rates.

**Tier 3 — Activity Metrics (Operational):**
Content production volume, email engagement rates, event attendance, website traffic, and social media metrics. Activity metrics are useful for operational management but should never be used as primary success measures.

### KPI Selection Criteria

Every KPI must be:
1. **Aligned** — Traces directly to a corporate or product goal
2. **Measurable** — You can actually collect the data
3. **Actionable** — The team can influence the metric through their programs
4. **Timely** — Data is available frequently enough to inform decisions
5. **Understandable** — Stakeholders can interpret it without extensive explanation

### Common Measurement Mistakes (from Pragmatic Institute guidance)

1. **Too many KPIs** — Tracking everything means optimizing nothing. Limit to 5–7 KPIs per program.
2. **Vanity metrics** — Impressions, page views, and email opens feel good but don't connect to business outcomes.
3. **Borrowed KPIs** — Adopting metrics from other companies without understanding if they fit your business model.
4. **No connection to financials** — Marketing metrics that don't ultimately connect to revenue, profit, or customer economics are incomplete.
5. **Measuring without tuning** — Collecting data but never changing programs based on what the data reveals.

### The Tune Process

Measurement without action is just reporting. The tune process defines:
- **Review cadence** — Weekly tactical reviews, monthly strategic reviews, quarterly portfolio reviews
- **Decision protocol** — At each review, what questions are asked, what thresholds trigger action, and who has authority to adjust
- **Adjustment types** — Budget reallocation, program modification, program termination, new program creation
- **Documentation** — Record what was changed, why, and expected impact for continuous learning

### Attribution Models

- **First-touch** — Credits the first marketing touchpoint. Useful for understanding what drives initial awareness.
- **Last-touch** — Credits the last touchpoint before conversion. Useful for understanding what closes deals.
- **Multi-touch** — Distributes credit across all touchpoints. More accurate but harder to implement.
- **Self-reported** — Asks buyers directly how they heard about you. Often reveals influence that digital attribution misses.

No single model is correct. Pragmatic recommends using multiple models and triangulating to understand marketing's true impact.

### Reporting to Stakeholders

Present marketing measurement in business language:
- Lead with business outcomes, not marketing activities
- Show the connection between programs and revenue/pipeline
- Acknowledge what's working and what isn't — credibility comes from honesty, not from cherry-picking good numbers
- Include the "so what" — what you're going to do differently based on what you learned
- Keep reports concise — executives don't need 50-slide decks

## Cross-Activity Integration Map

The eight Programs activities don't operate in isolation. Here's how they work together:

**Awareness → Nurturing → Revenue Growth:**
Awareness fills the top of the funnel. Nurturing moves prospects through the funnel. Revenue Growth converts nurtured prospects into paying customers. This is the primary demand generation flow.

**Revenue Growth → Revenue Retention → Advocacy:**
New customers become retained customers become advocates. Advocacy then feeds back into Awareness and Nurturing, creating a virtuous flywheel.

**Launch → (All Activities):**
Launches are concentrated events that temporarily intensify multiple activities simultaneously — awareness, nurturing, sales enablement, and customer communication.

**Measurement → (All Activities):**
Measurement monitors performance across all activities and triggers tuning adjustments. Without Measurement, the Programs category operates on assumptions rather than evidence.

**Marketing Plan → (All Activities):**
The Marketing Plan is the strategic umbrella that sequences, budgets, and coordinates all other program activities across the fiscal period.
