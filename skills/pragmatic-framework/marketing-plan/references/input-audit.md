# Input Audit — Checking Strategic Readiness for the Marketing Plan

Before building a marketing plan, verify which strategic inputs from the Pragmatic Framework are available and how complete they are. A marketing plan built on incomplete inputs will produce scattered tactics instead of a coherent market-driven strategy.

## The Input Checklist

### Tier 1: Critical (Plan Cannot Succeed Without These)

**Positioning**
- Do positioning statements exist for each target segment and persona?
- Are they formulated as "For [persona] who [problem], our [product] provides [benefit] unlike [alternative]"?
- Do they describe the product by its ability to solve market problems (not by features)?
- Are they current — reflecting the latest market understanding?
- If missing: Help the user create at minimum a working positioning hypothesis. Even a rough positioning statement is better than none. Reference the Positioning skill if available.

**Buyer Personas**
- Are buyer persona documents defined for the key decision-makers and influencers in the buying process?
- Do personas include: role, responsibilities, goals, challenges, buying behavior, information sources, and evaluation criteria?
- Are personas based on market data (NIHITO interviews, win/loss analysis) or are they assumptions?
- If missing: Help the user sketch 1–3 lightweight persona profiles based on whatever market knowledge exists. Flag that these should be validated through market research.

### Tier 2: Important (Significantly Improves Plan Quality)

**Buyer Experience (Journey Map)**
- Is the buying process documented — the stages buyers go through from problem recognition to vendor selection?
- Are barriers and friction points identified at each stage?
- If missing: Use a generic B2B buying process as a starting framework (Problem Recognition → Research → Evaluation → Selection → Purchase → Implementation) and customize based on user knowledge.

**Competitive Landscape**
- Are key competitors and alternatives identified?
- Are competitive strengths and weaknesses assessed?
- Is there a competitive strategy (where to fight, where to concede)?
- If missing: Ask the user to identify top 3–5 competitors and briefly characterize each. Even informal competitive knowledge improves program targeting.

**Market Definition**
- Are target market segments defined using problem-based segmentation?
- Is the addressable market sized (TAM/SAM/SOM)?
- Are segments prioritized for investment?
- If missing: Ask the user which market segments they are actively pursuing and what makes each attractive.

### Tier 3: Helpful (Provides Additional Context)

**Business Plan / Financial Model**
- Are revenue targets defined for the fiscal period?
- Is there a product profitability analysis?
- If missing: Ask for at minimum the revenue targets or growth objectives the marketing plan should support.

**Product Roadmap**
- Are upcoming releases, features, or milestones scheduled?
- Are there planned launches that need marketing support?
- If missing: Ask about any product launches or major milestones in the fiscal period.

**Win/Loss Analysis**
- Are recent win/loss patterns documented?
- Are there insights on why deals are won or lost?
- If missing: Ask the user informally what they hear from sales about why they win and lose.

## Working with Incomplete Inputs

It's rare for all inputs to be complete and current. The pragmatic approach is to work with what you have while flagging gaps.

**When Tier 1 inputs are missing:** Don't skip them — create lightweight versions. A rough positioning statement and basic persona profiles take 30 minutes and transform the plan's quality.

**When Tier 2 inputs are missing:** Use reasonable assumptions based on the user's domain knowledge and flag the assumptions explicitly in the plan document. Mark sections that depend on unvalidated assumptions so they can be revisited.

**When Tier 3 inputs are missing:** Proceed without them but note where additional context would improve specific programs. The plan can be updated as these inputs become available.

## Red Flags

Watch for these signals that the plan is being built on weak foundations:
- The user can't articulate who the target buyer is beyond a job title
- Positioning focuses on features and technology rather than market problems
- The competitive landscape is described as "we don't really have competitors"
- Market segments are defined by product line rather than by customer problems
- Revenue targets are aspirational without a model connecting them to pipeline and conversion rates
