# Program Mapping — Connecting Programs to the Buyer Experience

The core discipline of a Pragmatic marketing plan is mapping programs to the buyer experience. This is what separates a market-driven plan from a random collection of marketing activities.

## The Mapping Principle

Every program in the marketing plan should answer three questions:
1. **Which persona** does this program reach?
2. **At what stage** of the buyer's journey does this program engage them?
3. **What message** does this program deliver (traced back to positioning)?

If a program can't answer all three questions, it's either poorly defined or shouldn't be in the plan.

## Buyer Journey Stages

While every market has its own buying process, most B2B buying journeys follow a recognizable pattern. Use the buyer experience map if one exists; otherwise, start with this generic framework and customize.

### Stage 1: Problem Recognition (Awareness)

**Buyer mindset:** "I have a problem but I may not know how serious it is or that solutions exist."

**Marketing objective:** Help buyers recognize and articulate their problem. Establish your company as a knowledgeable voice in the space.

**Program types:**
- Thought leadership content (articles, research reports, podcasts, speaking engagements)
- Brand awareness campaigns targeting industry publications and communities
- SEO/SEM for problem-oriented keywords (not product keywords)
- Social media presence in communities where target personas gather
- Analyst relations and industry influencer engagement
- PR and earned media focused on market trends
- Event sponsorships and speaking opportunities at industry conferences

**Messaging focus:** Problem amplification — "Here's why this problem matters and what it costs you." Avoid talking about your product at this stage. Establish credibility by demonstrating deep understanding of the buyer's world.

**Key metrics:** Brand awareness (aided and unaided), share of voice, website traffic from target segments, content reach and engagement, social following growth, analyst mentions.

### Stage 2: Education and Exploration

**Buyer mindset:** "I know I have a problem and I'm exploring what kinds of solutions exist. I'm building my short list."

**Marketing objective:** Educate buyers about solution approaches. Position your category and product as the right way to solve the problem.

**Program types:**
- Educational webinars and virtual events
- Whitepapers and solution guides
- Comparison and buyer's guides (category-level, not vendor-level)
- Customer stories and use cases (focused on the problem journey, not product features)
- Analyst reports and evaluations
- Email nurturing sequences for known contacts
- Retargeting campaigns for website visitors
- Product-focused SEO/SEM

**Messaging focus:** Solution education — "Here's the best way to solve this problem, and here's what to look for in a solution." Begin introducing your product's approach but lead with the problem and the solution category, not features.

**Key metrics:** Content downloads, webinar registrations and attendance, email engagement rates, website engagement (time on site, pages per session), marketing qualified leads (MQLs), demo requests.

### Stage 3: Evaluation and Selection

**Buyer mindset:** "I'm evaluating specific vendors and trying to make a decision. I need to build consensus among stakeholders."

**Marketing objective:** Help buyers evaluate your product favorably. Equip internal champions with tools to sell internally. Overcome competitive objections.

**Program types:**
- Product demos (live and self-serve)
- Free trials and proof-of-concept programs
- ROI calculators and business case tools
- Competitive comparison content (battle cards for sales, public comparison pages)
- Customer reference programs
- Technical deep-dive content for technical evaluators
- Executive briefings for economic buyers
- Sales enablement (tools, content, training mapped to the buying process)

**Messaging focus:** Differentiation and proof — "Here's why we're the best choice for your specific situation, and here's the evidence." Tailor messaging to each persona's evaluation criteria.

**Key metrics:** Sales qualified leads (SQLs), opportunities created, pipeline value, demo-to-opportunity conversion, win rate, competitive win rate, sales cycle length.

### Stage 4: Purchase and Onboarding

**Buyer mindset:** "I've decided but I need to complete procurement and get started successfully."

**Marketing objective:** Remove friction from the purchase process. Ensure successful onboarding to set up long-term retention.

**Program types:**
- Procurement support content (security documentation, compliance materials)
- Onboarding programs and getting-started guides
- Implementation support and training programs
- Welcome campaigns for new customers
- Early success milestone celebrations

**Messaging focus:** Confidence and momentum — "You made the right choice. Here's how to get value fast."

**Key metrics:** Time to close, onboarding completion rate, time to first value, early NPS scores.

### Stage 5: Loyalty and Expansion

**Buyer mindset:** "I'm using the product. I might expand usage, renew, or tell others about it."

**Marketing objective:** Drive adoption, expand usage, and create advocates. Grow revenue from the existing customer base.

**Program types:**
- Customer education and training (ongoing)
- User community building
- Customer advisory boards
- Upsell and cross-sell campaigns
- Advocacy programs (references, testimonials, case studies, user-generated content)
- Customer events and user conferences
- Customer newsletters and product update communications
- Loyalty and reward programs
- Renewal campaigns

**Messaging focus:** Value realization and expansion — "Here's how to get even more value. Here's what's coming next."

**Key metrics:** Net revenue retention (NRR), expansion revenue, customer satisfaction (CSAT/NPS), advocacy participation, renewal rate, churn rate.

## Building the Program Map

### Step 1: Create the Journey-Persona Matrix

Build a matrix with buyer journey stages as columns and target personas as rows. For each cell, note:
- Current state (what programs exist today, if any?)
- Gaps (where are buyers underserved?)
- Priority (high/medium/low based on business impact)

### Step 2: Identify Program Gaps

Look for patterns:
- **Overweight in one stage:** Many companies have lots of awareness programs but weak evaluation-stage support. Others have strong sales tools but poor top-of-funnel.
- **Missing personas:** Some personas (especially technical evaluators or end users) may have no targeted programs at all.
- **No retention/expansion programs:** New-customer acquisition gets all the attention; existing customers are ignored.

### Step 3: Prioritize Programs

Not every gap needs a program this fiscal period. Prioritize based on:
- Business impact (which programs have the highest potential to move key metrics?)
- Buyer need (where are buyers most underserved in the current experience?)
- Competitive pressure (where are competitors outperforming you in the buyer experience?)
- Resource requirements (what can you realistically execute with available budget and team?)
- Dependencies (what needs to be in place before a program can succeed?)

### Step 4: Sequence Programs

Programs have dependencies and optimal timing:
- Positioning must be complete before content programs can execute
- Awareness programs should lead evaluation-stage programs by enough time for buyers to progress
- Launch programs need to align with product roadmap milestones
- Seasonal patterns (budget cycles, conference seasons, buying windows) affect timing

## Channel Strategy

For each program, determine the optimal mix of channels based on where target personas actually spend their time and consume information. Avoid defaulting to familiar channels — use buyer persona data to guide channel selection.

### Channel Considerations
- **Where do target personas discover new information?** (Industry publications, social platforms, events, peer networks, analyst firms)
- **What content formats do they prefer?** (Long-form research, short videos, interactive tools, podcasts, in-person presentations)
- **What channels support the journey stage?** (Awareness favors broad-reach channels; evaluation favors targeted, high-trust channels)
- **What channels can you measure effectively?** (Attribution capability varies dramatically by channel)
- **What's the cost efficiency by channel?** (CPL, CPA, and pipeline contribution per channel)

## Integration Points

The marketing plan doesn't exist in isolation. Key integration points with other Pragmatic Framework activities:

- **Launch** — Major product launches are specific programs within the plan, with their own detailed launch plans
- **Sales Alignment** — Programs that equip sales should be co-designed with sales leadership
- **Content** — Content strategy flows from the program map; content needs are identified per persona per journey stage
- **Measurement** — The plan's metrics framework feeds the Measurement activity for ongoing tracking and optimization
- **Stakeholder Communications** — Internal stakeholders need visibility into the plan and its progress
