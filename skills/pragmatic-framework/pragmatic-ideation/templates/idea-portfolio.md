# Idea Portfolio Assessment

## Purpose

Most organizations explore many ideas at once, from small improvements to new bets. This assessment helps teams see all their concepts and experiments in one place, balance them, and decide which to keep testing, which to move into delivery and which to drop, so that the set as a whole matches market needs and strategy.

It connects to Product Portfolio (Focus, `pragmatic-product-portfolio`, with its horizon view of core today, emerging next and new bets) and Business Outcomes (Business, `pragmatic-business-outcomes`). Concepts that earn evidence feed the broader product portfolio and roadmap.

## When to Use

- Annual or quarterly planning when deciding where to invest ideation and experiment capacity
- When the team has more ideas than capacity and needs to prioritize
- When leadership asks "are we investing in the right things?"
- When the idea pipeline feels unbalanced (too many moonshots, or too incremental)

## How to Guide the User

### Step 1: Inventory Current Ideas and Experiments

Help the user list all active and proposed concepts and experiments. For each one, capture:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Name** | Short descriptive name |
| **Market Problem** | Which validated market problem does this address? |
| **Target Persona/Segment** | Who benefits? |
| **Type** | Incremental, Adjacent, or Transformational |
| **Stage** | Concept, Testing, Delivery, Launched, or Dropped |
| **Evidence So Far** | Experiments run and whether they met their success thresholds |
| **Distinctive Competency Link** | Which PRIME competency does this leverage? |
| **Estimated Investment** | Rough resource/cost estimate (T-shirt size is fine) |
| **Expected Impact** | Revenue, retention, strategic positioning, etc. |
| **Risk Level** | Low, Medium, High |

### Step 2: Analyze Portfolio Balance

Once the inventory is complete, assess the portfolio along several dimensions:

**By Type**:
A common healthy ratio (adjust for your org's risk tolerance and market position):
- ~70% Incremental — sustaining and improving current products
- ~20% Adjacent — extending into new markets or approaches
- ~10% Transformational — breakthrough or disruptive bets

If the portfolio is heavily skewed in one direction, discuss whether that's intentional or a sign of drift.

**By Market Problem Coverage**:
- Are the top 3-5 validated market problems each addressed by at least one concept?
- Are there validated problems with no concept being explored?
- Are there concepts that don't map to any validated problem? (These are the highest risk — consider pausing or validating the problem.)

**By Distinctive Competency**:
- Which competencies are being leveraged most heavily? Least?
- Are any concepts attempting to build entirely new competencies? (Higher risk and longer timeline — make sure that's intentional.)

**By Stage**:
- Is there a healthy pipeline from concept → testing → delivery → launched?
- Are things stuck in "concept" without a test? (Ideation theater risk)
- Are things jumping from concept straight to delivery without user evidence? (Market risk)
- Is anything being dropped? A portfolio where nothing is ever dropped isn't really testing.

### Step 3: Identify Actions

Based on the analysis, help the user identify:

1. **Keep / Move to Delivery**: Concepts tied to validated market problems that have earned evidence from user tests. Hand them to Requirements and the roadmap.

2. **Test Before Continuing**: Concepts that seem promising but whose riskiest assumption hasn't been tested. Define the next experiment and by when.

3. **Drop**: Concepts that don't connect to validated market problems, missed their success thresholds, or are stalled. Dropping ideas that didn't earn evidence is good portfolio management, not failure. Record what was learned.

4. **Gap-Fill**: Validated market problems with no concept being explored. These are potential new initiatives to add to the portfolio.

### Step 4: Set Review Cadence

Idea portfolios should be reviewed regularly — quarterly at minimum. Help the user define:
- Who reviews the portfolio (product leadership, executive sponsors)
- What metrics they'll track (stage progression, experiment results, ideas dropped, resource allocation)
- What triggers a re-assessment outside the regular cadence (major competitive move, market shift, key customer loss)

## Output Format

The final deliverable should be a concise portfolio summary that a leadership team could review in 20-30 minutes, containing:

1. Portfolio inventory table (from Step 1)
2. Balance analysis with visualizations where helpful (by type, by problem, by stage)
3. Recommended actions for each concept (keep/move to delivery, test, drop)
4. Identified gaps and proposed new initiatives
5. Review schedule and governance
