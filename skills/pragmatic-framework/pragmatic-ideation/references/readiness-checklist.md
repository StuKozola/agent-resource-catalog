# Ideation Readiness Checklist

Before a team starts ideation, it needs solid inputs from other focus areas. Every idea must be grounded in a validated market problem, and the team needs to know who the users are so it can test with them. Use this checklist to assess readiness and identify gaps.

## 1. Market Problems (Required)

Ask the user these questions and assess their answers:

- [ ] **Do you have a written list of validated market problems?**
  - These should come from customer interviews, not internal brainstorming
  - Each problem should specify who has the problem (which persona/segment)
  - "Validated" means multiple customers independently confirmed the problem exists

- [ ] **Are the problems ranked by urgency and pervasiveness?**
  - Urgency: How much pain does this cause right now? Are people actively seeking solutions?
  - Pervasiveness: How many people/organizations in the target segment share this problem?
  - Willingness to pay: Would the market pay to have this solved?

- [ ] **Were problems discovered through an outside-in approach?**
  - Interviews with current customers, recent evaluators, and untapped potential customers
  - Not just from internal opinions, sales requests, or support tickets alone

**If gaps exist**: Help the user plan customer discovery interviews. They need at least 10-15 interviews across their target segments to have useful problem data. Remind them: "Your opinion, although interesting, is irrelevant" (Pragmatic Rule #6). The answer is not in the building.

## 2. User Personas (Required)

- [ ] **Do you know who will use the solution, and what they need to get done?** (`pragmatic-user-personas`)
- [ ] **Can you recruit 5-8 real users matching the persona for quick tests?**
- [ ] **Do you understand their current workflow and workarounds for the problem?**

**If gaps exist**: Sketch provisional personas from existing research and line up users to test with before generating ideas. Without users to test with, ideation becomes opinion.

## 3. Distinctive Competencies (Recommended)

- [ ] **Has the organization articulated its distinctive competencies using the PRIME method?**
  - **Purpose**: What is the organization's purpose? What problem is it built to solve?
  - **Reputation**: What is the org known for in the market? What do customers expect from this brand?
  - **Innovation**: What has the org innovated before? What is its track record?
  - **Methods**: What proprietary methods, processes, or approaches does the org use?
  - **Expertise**: What deep domain expertise does the org uniquely possess?

- [ ] **Are competencies *distinctive*, not just core?**
  - Core competencies = things you do well
  - Distinctive competencies = things you do well that competitors cannot easily replicate
  - The test: Can a competitor copy this within 12-18 months? If yes, it's core, not distinctive.

- [ ] **Are competencies validated externally, not just self-assessed?**
  - Win/loss data, customer feedback, and analyst reports should confirm what you think makes you special

**If gaps exist**: Run a PRIME workshop with the product team and key stakeholders. Cross-reference internal beliefs against win/loss data and customer interviews.

## 4. Competitive Landscape (Recommended)

- [ ] **Do you know who your direct and indirect competitors are?**
- [ ] **Do you understand their strengths and weaknesses?**
- [ ] **Have you identified gaps in the competitive landscape — problems nobody is solving well?**
- [ ] **Do you have a strategy for winning against key competitors?**

**If gaps exist**: This doesn't have to be exhaustive to start ideation, but having at least a basic competitive map prevents you from exploring a space where someone else already has an unassailable position.

## 5. Existing Assets (Recommended; also an input to Product Strategy)

- [ ] **Have you inventoried your technical assets?** (technology, platforms, infrastructure)
- [ ] **Have you inventoried your human assets?** (skills, domain expertise, relationships)
- [ ] **Have you inventoried your legal/IP assets?** (patents, trademarks, proprietary data)
- [ ] **Have you identified assets that could be leveraged in new ways?**

**If gaps exist**: A quick asset inventory exercise (even 30 minutes with the right people) can unlock ideas that would otherwise be invisible.

## 6. Win/Loss Analysis (Helpful)

- [ ] **Do you understand why recent evaluators bought or didn't buy?**
- [ ] **Have you identified patterns in the buying process?**
- [ ] **Are there consistent unmet needs that emerge from win/loss data?**

**If gaps exist**: Even informal win/loss conversations with 5-10 recent deals can surface valuable signals for where to explore.

## 7. Ability to Test (Required)

- [ ] **Can the team build cheap prototypes or run quick experiments?** (design and engineering time, tools, test environment)
- [ ] **Is there agreement on who decides keep, change or drop, and on what evidence?**

**If gaps exist**: Agree on a decision owner and a lightweight test budget before the workshop.

## Readiness Scoring

Count how many of the Required items are checked:

- **All Required items checked**: Ready to start ideation. Move to choosing a deliverable.
- **Most Required items checked with minor gaps**: Proceed, but flag the gaps and plan to fill them in parallel.
- **Major gaps in Required items**: Do not start ideation yet. Help the user plan upstream work (customer interviews, persona research, recruiting test users, etc.) and come back when the foundation is stronger.

The point is not bureaucratic gatekeeping — it's ensuring that ideation effort is directed at real opportunities rather than assumptions. Teams that skip this step tend to produce clever solutions to problems nobody actually has.
