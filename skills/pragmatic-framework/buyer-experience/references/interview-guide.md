# Buyer Experience Interview Guide

Use this question bank when conducting interviews with recent evaluators to understand the buying process. These questions are designed for product managers or product marketers conducting buyer experience research — not sales teams (who may inadvertently bias responses or trigger objection-handling instincts).

## Interview Setup

**Ideal timing:** 2–4 weeks after the deal closes (win or loss). Sooner risks the buyer's memory blending with post-purchase experience; later risks fading recall of the evaluation process.

**Target duration:** 30–45 minutes. Start by asking for 15 minutes and extend if the conversation is flowing.

**Who to interview:** The primary evaluator or champion — the person who drove the evaluation process. Supplement with interviews of other buying committee members (economic buyer, technical buyer) for a multi-perspective view.

**Recording:** Always ask permission. Recorded interviews can be coded and analyzed more rigorously.

**Framing the conversation:** Make it clear this is about understanding the buying *process*, not about trying to re-open a deal or upsell. Something like:

> "We're trying to understand how companies like yours go through the process of evaluating and selecting [product category]. Your experience is really valuable to us — whether you ended up choosing us or not. There are no wrong answers, and anything you share will help us improve."

---

## Opening Questions

These warm up the conversation and establish context.

1. Can you give me a quick overview of your role and how you were involved in this evaluation?
2. What was going on in your organization that led you to start looking at [product category]? What was the trigger?
3. Had you been thinking about this for a while, or was there a specific event that kicked things off?

---

## Journey and Process Questions

These map the stages the buyer went through.

4. Walk me through the process from when you first started looking to when a final decision was made. What were the major steps?
5. How long did the overall process take? Were there any points where it stalled or slowed down? What caused that?
6. Who else was involved in the process? What were their roles and what did they care about most?
7. Was there a formal process (like an RFP) or was it more informal?
8. At what point did you first reach out to vendors directly? What prompted that?
9. How many solutions or vendors did you consider? How did you narrow the list?

---

## Information and Research Questions

These reveal where and how buyers educate themselves.

10. When you were first researching options, where did you go for information?
11. Did you talk to peers or colleagues at other companies? How influential were those conversations?
12. Did you use any analyst reports, review sites (like G2 or Gartner), or industry publications? Which ones?
13. What kind of content was most useful to you during the process? What was least useful?
14. Was there information you wanted but couldn't find? What gaps frustrated you?
15. How did you feel about the information available on vendor websites? Was it helpful or was it mostly marketing speak?

---

## Evaluation and Comparison Questions

These uncover decision criteria and competitive dynamics.

16. What were the most important factors you were evaluating vendors on? Can you rank them?
17. Were there any "must-haves" — things that would immediately disqualify a vendor?
18. How did you compare vendors? Did you use a formal scoring system or was it more intuitive?
19. Did you do demos or trials? How useful were they? What made a demo good or bad?
20. Did you check references? How did you find them — through the vendor or independently?
21. Were there any surprises during the evaluation — things you learned that changed your thinking?

---

## Barriers and Friction Questions

These identify the obstacles that slow or derail the buying process.

22. What was the hardest part of this whole process?
23. Were there any points where you almost gave up or decided not to buy anything at all?
24. Did you encounter any barriers from within your own organization — like getting budget approval, aligning stakeholders, or navigating procurement?
25. Were there things any of the vendors did that made the process harder than it needed to be?
26. Was pricing transparent enough? Did you have a clear understanding of total cost before making a decision?
27. Were there any security, compliance, or legal hurdles? How did vendors help (or not help) with those?

---

## Decision Questions

These focus on the final selection.

28. What ultimately tipped the decision in favor of [chosen vendor]?
29. Was the decision unanimous, or were there dissenters? What were their concerns?
30. If you chose us: Was there anything about our process that almost lost us the deal?
31. If you chose a competitor: What did they do better? Was there anything we could have done differently?
32. If you made no decision: What prevented you from moving forward? What would need to change for you to re-engage?

---

## Reflection Questions

These capture the buyer's overall assessment and generate forward-looking insight.

33. If you had to do this evaluation again, what would you do differently?
34. What advice would you give to someone in your role who's about to start this kind of evaluation?
35. Is there anything I haven't asked about that was important to your experience?

---

## Questions Specific to Buying Roles

Tailor these based on who you're interviewing.

### For Economic Buyers (CFO, VP, Budget Owner)
- How did you evaluate the financial case for this purchase?
- What ROI or payback period did you need to see?
- How did this compete with other budget priorities?
- What would have made the business case easier to build?

### For Technical Buyers (IT, Security, Architecture)
- What technical requirements were most important?
- How did you assess integration with your existing systems?
- What security or compliance standards did vendors need to meet?
- Did you run a technical proof of concept? How did that go?

### For Functional/End-User Buyers
- How did you evaluate whether the product would actually work for your day-to-day needs?
- Did you get hands-on time with the product? Was that helpful?
- What were your biggest concerns about switching from your current approach?
- How important was ease of use relative to other factors?

### For Champions/Evaluators
- How did you go about building support internally?
- What objections did you hear from colleagues, and how did you address them?
- What materials or information would have helped you sell this internally?
- Did the vendor help you make the internal case? How?

---

## Post-Interview Analysis

After each interview, capture:
- **Journey stages observed** — Map this buyer's process to your stage framework
- **Barriers encountered** — List each friction point with stage and severity
- **Decision criteria** — Rank factors this buyer mentioned, noting any persona-specific patterns
- **Quotes** — Record verbatim quotes that vividly illustrate key points (these are invaluable for internal presentations)
- **Surprises** — Anything that contradicts your current assumptions
- **Recommendations** — Immediate ideas for improvements based on this conversation

After 5+ interviews, look for patterns:
- Which stages take the longest?
- Where do buyers most commonly stall?
- What barriers repeat across interviews?
- Are there segments or personas with distinctly different journeys?
- What content or touchpoints are consistently praised or criticized?
