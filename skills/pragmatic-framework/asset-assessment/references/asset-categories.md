# Asset Categories — Detailed Reference

This reference provides detailed descriptions and discovery questions for each asset category. Use these prompts when guiding a user through their asset inventory to ensure comprehensive coverage.

---

## 1. Technology Assets

Technology assets include any platforms, tools, systems, modules, or infrastructure the organization has built, licensed, or maintained.

### What to look for:
- Core platform components and architectures
- APIs (internal and external-facing)
- SDKs and developer tools
- Internal tools and utilities built for operational efficiency
- Infrastructure (cloud accounts, CI/CD pipelines, monitoring systems)
- Sandbox or prototype technology that never shipped
- Legacy systems that still hold valuable logic or data
- Licensed third-party technology with favorable terms

### Discovery questions:
- What platforms or core technology does the product run on?
- Are there reusable modules or microservices that multiple products share?
- What internal tools has engineering built that could be productized or reused?
- Is there technology in a sandbox or prototype stage that could be leveraged?
- What third-party platforms or tools do you have licenses for?
- Are there legacy systems with components that could be extracted and modernized?
- What infrastructure capabilities (cloud, DevOps, monitoring) are in place?
- Do you have any data pipelines or ETL processes that could serve new use cases?

## 2. Intellectual Property

Intellectual property assets protect your competitive advantage and can be licensed, sold, or leveraged for new products.

### What to look for:
- Patents (granted and pending)
- Trade secrets and proprietary processes
- Proprietary algorithms or models
- Copyrights on software, content, or creative works
- Trademarks and brand registrations
- Registered designs

### Discovery questions:
- What patents has the organization been granted? Are any pending?
- Are there proprietary algorithms, formulas, or processes that give you a competitive edge?
- What trade secrets does the organization protect?
- Do you have copyrighted content, software, or creative works?
- What trademarks or brand assets are registered?
- Are there IP assets from acquisitions that haven't been fully leveraged?
- Could any internal processes or methodologies be patented?

## 3. Skills & Expertise

People are assets. The specialized knowledge, certifications, and domain expertise within your team are often the hardest assets for competitors to replicate.

### What to look for:
- Deep domain expertise (industry-specific knowledge)
- Specialized technical skills (ML/AI, security, embedded systems, etc.)
- Process expertise (Agile, Six Sigma, regulatory compliance)
- Industry certifications held by team members
- Regulatory and compliance knowledge
- Language and cultural expertise for international markets
- Research capabilities

### Discovery questions:
- What specialized domain knowledge does your team have that competitors lack?
- Are there certified professionals on the team (e.g., PMP, CISSP, AWS certified)?
- Do team members have deep regulatory or compliance expertise?
- What process methodologies has the team mastered?
- Are there research capabilities or relationships with academic institutions?
- Do you have expertise in specific geographic markets or languages?
- What institutional knowledge would be hardest to replace if key people left?

---

## 4. Products & Components

Current products, retired products, and reusable components all represent investments that can be leveraged.

### What to look for:
- Currently shipping products and their components
- Products that have been withdrawn from market but retain usable technology
- Shared libraries, frameworks, or modules
- Product features that could be unbundled or repositioned
- Open-source projects the organization maintains or contributes to
- Reference architectures and design patterns

### Discovery questions:
- What products are currently in market? What are their core components?
- Have any products been retired that contain reusable technology?
- Are there shared libraries or components used across multiple products?
- Could any existing product features be unbundled and sold separately?
- Does the organization maintain or contribute to open-source projects?
- Are there proven reference architectures or design patterns documented?
- What product components could serve an adjacent market with minimal modification?

---

## 5. Services

Service capabilities represent expertise that can be packaged, scaled, or used to support new product offerings.

### What to look for:
- Professional services and consulting capabilities
- Support infrastructure and processes
- Training and education programs
- Implementation and onboarding methodologies
- Managed services offerings
- Customer success playbooks

### Discovery questions:
- What professional services does the organization offer?
- Is there a documented implementation or onboarding methodology?
- What training or education programs exist (internal or customer-facing)?
- How is customer support structured? Could the infrastructure support new products?
- Are there repeatable consulting frameworks or playbooks?
- Could any internal service capability be productized?
- What managed services are offered, and what infrastructure supports them?

## 6. Marketing & Content Assets

Marketing assets include all collateral, messaging, positioning, and content that communicates value to the market.

### What to look for:
- Brand assets (logos, style guides, brand voice documentation)
- Positioning and messaging documents
- Case studies and customer success stories
- Sales presentations, demos, and proof-of-concept materials
- Whitepapers, ebooks, and thought leadership content
- Website content and SEO rankings
- Email templates and nurture sequences
- Event materials (booth designs, presentation decks)
- Video content (product demos, webinars, tutorials)
- Social media content and following
- Analyst reports and third-party endorsements

### Discovery questions:
- Is there a current brand style guide? When was it last updated?
- What positioning and messaging documents exist?
- How many case studies or customer success stories are available?
- What sales tools and presentations does the sales team use?
- Is there a content library (blog posts, whitepapers, videos)?
- What email templates and automated sequences are in place?
- Are there event or conference materials that could be repurposed?
- What is the organization's social media presence and reach?
- Have industry analysts covered your products? Are there endorsements?
- Are there personas or buyer journey documents already created?

---

## 7. Data & Analytics Assets

Data is one of the most valuable and often underestimated asset categories.

### What to look for:
- Customer data and behavioral analytics
- Market research studies and reports
- Product usage data and telemetry
- Performance benchmarks and historical metrics
- Competitive intelligence databases
- Industry datasets or proprietary data sources
- Machine learning models and training data
- A/B test results and experiment logs

### Discovery questions:
- What customer data does the organization collect and maintain?
- Are there market research studies or reports that could inform new products?
- What product usage data is available, and how is it analyzed?
- Do you track performance benchmarks over time?
- Is there a competitive intelligence process or database?
- Do you have proprietary datasets that would be valuable to others?
- Are there trained ML models or accumulated training data?
- What historical experiment or A/B test data exists?

---

## 8. Relationship Assets

Relationships — with customers, partners, analysts, and channels — are strategic assets that can be leveraged for new ventures.

### What to look for:
- Strategic partner relationships
- Channel partner networks
- Customer advisory boards or beta communities
- Industry analyst relationships
- Academic or research partnerships
- Government or regulatory relationships
- Vendor relationships with favorable terms
- Community or ecosystem participation

### Discovery questions:
- What strategic partnerships are in place? Could they extend to new products?
- Is there a channel partner network? How effective is it?
- Do you have a customer advisory board or active beta community?
- What relationships exist with industry analysts?
- Are there academic or research institution partnerships?
- Do you have established relationships with regulators or government agencies?
- What vendor relationships have favorable terms that could benefit new initiatives?
- Is the organization active in industry communities or standards bodies?
