# The 2027 Pragmatic Framework — Quick Reference

Pragmatic Institute refreshed the Pragmatic Framework for 2027. It still has **37 focus areas in 7 categories**, and its purpose is the same: help teams build and market products the market wants to buy. The refresh updated terminology and added a full **Operations** category for the work that happens after launch.

## How the Framework is arranged

- **Left to right: strategy → execution.** Teams don't move through it once. They go back to focus areas as they gather evidence, make decisions and learn from results.
- **Above the bar: the market.** Problems, segments, buyers and business models.
- **Below the bar: the product and the teams around it.** Roadmaps, requirements, enablement and operations.
- **The loop.** What teams learn after launch, especially in Customer Value Realization, becomes the next round of Market Problems.
- **No AI box.** AI is part of how teams do product work, not a category of its own. It can help across the Framework, for example with organizing research, exploring alternatives, drafting requirements or analyzing results. Teams still verify what it produces, protect sensitive information and stay accountable for their decisions.

## Boundaries the Framework draws explicitly

- **Measurement vs. Business Outcomes vs. Customer Value Realization.** Measurement sets the indicators, leading and lagging. Business Outcomes tracks results for *your organization*. Customer Value Realization asks whether *customers* got the outcome they wanted.
- **Customer Satisfaction vs. Customer Value Realization.** Satisfaction is the customer's own judgment of their experience and relationship with you. Value realization is whether they achieved the outcome they bought for. Willingness to recommend, references and reviews are signals of satisfaction, but they don't define it.
- **Positioning vs. messaging.** Positioning is what you want to communicate; messaging is how you'll communicate it.
- **Buyer Personas vs. User Personas.** Buyers make or influence the purchase decision. Users work with the product, and their success decides whether it succeeds.

| Channels + Channel Training | Channel Support |

| Events | Part of Awareness and Engagement |

| Programs and Support categories | Go-to-Market and Operations categories |
| *(new)* | Product Strategy, Customer Satisfaction, Adoption, Scaling, Exceptions, Escalations, Customer Value Realization |
