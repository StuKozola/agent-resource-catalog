# Launch Tier Classification

Not every release deserves a launch, and not every launch requires the same level of investment. Launch tiers provide a rational way to allocate resources based on expected impact.

## The Two Dimensions

Score each dimension on a scale of 1 (highest) to 3 (lowest):

### Business Impact
How much will this launch affect your organization?

**Score 1 (High):**
- Significant revenue potential (new revenue streams, major growth opportunity)
- Strategic direction change (new market entry, new business model)
- Major competitive repositioning
- Executive-level visibility and commitment
- Affects multiple business units or product lines

**Score 2 (Medium):**
- Meaningful revenue contribution (extension of existing revenue)
- Important but not transformative strategic value
- Strengthens existing competitive position
- Department-level visibility
- Affects single product line

**Score 3 (Low):**
- Minimal direct revenue impact
- Maintenance or hygiene value (keeps product current)
- No competitive repositioning
- Team-level visibility
- Localized effect

### Market Impact
How much will your target market care?

**Score 1 (High):**
- Solves a new or significantly underserved market problem
- Changes the competitive landscape for buyers
- Opens new segments or personas
- Creates category awareness or new buying criteria
- Industry analysts and media will take notice

**Score 2 (Medium):**
- Improves solution to known market problems
- Makes product more competitive but doesn't redefine category
- Deepens value for existing segments
- Selective analyst and media interest

**Score 3 (Low):**
- Incremental improvement to existing capabilities
- Market expects this as standard maintenance
- No change in competitive dynamics
- No media or analyst interest expected

## Tier Assignment

| Business Impact | Market Impact | Tier |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 1 | **Tier 1** |
| 1 | 2 | **Tier 1** |
| 2 | 1 | **Tier 1** |
| 1 | 3 | **Tier 1** |
| 3 | 1 | **Tier 1** |
| 2 | 2 | **Tier 2** |
| 2 | 3 | **Tier 2** |
| 3 | 2 | **Tier 2** |
| 3 | 3 | **Tier 3** |

**Key principle:** If either dimension is high (score 1), the launch warrants Tier 1 treatment. High impact on either axis means the organization must mobilize fully.

## Resource Allocation by Tier

### Tier 1 — Full Market Event
- **Launch team:** Dedicated, cross-functional team with named launch manager
- **Launch plan:** Comprehensive plan document with all sections
- **Readiness assessment:** Full organizational assessment across all functional areas
- **Sales enablement:** Complete package (training, tools, battle cards, demo scripts, reference stories)
- **Marketing programs:** Major campaign with multi-channel execution (PR, AR, digital, events, content)
- **Internal communications:** All-hands announcement, executive briefings, company-wide alignment
- **Budget:** Dedicated launch budget with executive approval
- **Timeline:** 8-16 weeks of active launch planning before launch date
- **Post-launch:** Formal measurement and reporting against goals, lessons learned

### Tier 2 — Targeted Market Activity
- **Launch team:** Part-time cross-functional team coordinated by product marketing
- **Launch plan:** Focused plan document (abbreviated sections)
- **Readiness assessment:** Targeted assessment of sales, support, and marketing
- **Sales enablement:** Key tools (updated positioning, competitive updates, FAQ)
- **Marketing programs:** Targeted campaign (email, blog, webinar, social)
- **Internal communications:** Sales team briefing, stakeholder updates
- **Budget:** Allocation from existing marketing plan budget
- **Timeline:** 4-8 weeks of planning before launch date
- **Post-launch:** Metrics tracking, brief review

### Tier 3 — Release Communication
- **Launch team:** Product marketing with engineering support
- **Launch plan:** Release notes and communication plan only
- **Readiness assessment:** Support team awareness check
- **Sales enablement:** Update to existing materials if needed
- **Marketing programs:** Release notes, in-app notification, email to affected customers
- **Internal communications:** Team notification
- **Budget:** Minimal or none
- **Timeline:** 1-2 weeks
- **Post-launch:** Monitor support tickets and adoption metrics

## Context-Dependent Exceptions

Tier classification can shift based on circumstances. Always apply judgment:

- **Customer retention crisis:** An infrastructure improvement (normally Tier 3) could warrant Tier 1 treatment if it directly addresses a retention problem threatening significant revenue.
- **Competitive response:** A feature match (normally Tier 2) could warrant Tier 1 treatment if a competitor is actively winning deals on this capability.
- **Regulatory compliance:** A compliance update (normally Tier 3) could warrant Tier 2 treatment if it opens a new market segment or removes a buying barrier.

Document the rationale for any tier exceptions so the decision is transparent and defensible.
