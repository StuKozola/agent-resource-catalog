# Strategic Input Audit for Launch Planning

Before building a launch plan, verify that upstream Pragmatic Framework artifacts exist and are current. Launch is an execution activity — it depends on strategic inputs from Market, Planning, Focus, and Business categories.

## Priority 1 — Must Have Before Launch Planning

These inputs are essential. Without them, the launch plan will be built on assumptions rather than market evidence.

### Positioning
- [ ] Positioning document exists for each target segment
- [ ] Positioning document exists for each buyer persona
- [ ] Positioning articulates market problems solved (not just features)
- [ ] Positioning includes competitive differentiation
- [ ] Positioning has been validated with market feedback

**If missing:** Create a working positioning brief using the problem/solution/differentiation format. Flag as a gap that must be closed before launch execution.

### Buyer Personas
- [ ] Buyer persona documents exist for each archetype involved in the purchase
- [ ] Personas include roles, responsibilities, and titles
- [ ] Personas include problems faced and priorities
- [ ] Personas include buying criteria and decision-making process
- [ ] Personas include preferred information sources and channels

**If missing:** Build lightweight persona profiles from sales team interviews and CRM data. Validate with 3-5 actual buyer conversations (NIHITO).

### Buyer Experience
- [ ] Buying process is mapped from awareness through purchase
- [ ] Barriers at each stage are identified
- [ ] Information needs at each stage are documented
- [ ] Current tools and content aligned to each stage are inventoried
- [ ] Gaps in buying process support are identified

**If missing:** Map the buying process using Win/Loss data and sales team input. Even a rough map is better than no map — it directly informs sales enablement planning.

### Pricing
- [ ] Pricing model is defined (subscription, perpetual, usage-based, etc.)
- [ ] Price schedules and tiers are established
- [ ] Pricing guidelines and discount authority are documented
- [ ] Pricing is competitive and defensible
- [ ] Pricing changes (if any) are communicated and approved

**If missing:** Pricing must be resolved before launch. Escalate to product management and executive leadership immediately.

## Priority 2 — Should Have

These inputs significantly strengthen the launch plan. Their absence increases risk but doesn't block planning.

### Competitive Landscape
- [ ] Key competitors and alternatives are identified
- [ ] Competitive strengths and weaknesses are assessed
- [ ] Win/loss patterns against each competitor are understood
- [ ] Competitive strategy (fight, partner, ignore) is defined
- [ ] Competitive battle cards exist or can be created

**If missing:** Build competitive intelligence from sales team knowledge, public information, and analyst reports. Prioritize the top 2-3 competitors.

### Market Definition
- [ ] Target segments are defined and prioritized
- [ ] Segment sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM) is documented
- [ ] Problem-based segmentation is used
- [ ] Segment selection rationale is clear

**If missing:** Work with product management to confirm which segments the launch targets. Use existing customer data to validate segment sizing.

### Marketing Plan
- [ ] Fiscal period marketing plan exists
- [ ] Launch-supporting programs are budgeted
- [ ] Awareness and demand generation programs are identified
- [ ] Measurement goals are established

**If missing:** Coordinate with marketing leadership to secure budget and program support for the launch window.

## Priority 3 — Helpful

These inputs provide useful context. Their absence is manageable.

### Revenue Growth Plans
- [ ] New customer acquisition strategies are defined
- [ ] Sales capacity and territory plans account for the new product
- [ ] Pipeline targets exist

### Revenue Retention Plans
- [ ] Existing customer communication strategy exists
- [ ] Upgrade/migration path is defined (if applicable)
- [ ] Customer success team is engaged

### Distinctive Competencies
- [ ] Organization's unique abilities are articulated
- [ ] Launch messaging leverages distinctive competencies

### Sales Alignment
- [ ] Selling process is documented
- [ ] Selling process aligns with buying process
- [ ] Sales support structure is defined

## Using the Audit Results

After completing the audit:

1. **Green (complete and current):** Input is ready. Reference it in the launch plan.
2. **Yellow (exists but incomplete or outdated):** Flag for refresh. Build a task into the launch plan timeline.
3. **Red (missing):** Create a lightweight working version. Assign ownership and deadline. This becomes a readiness gap.

The audit results directly feed into the launch readiness assessment (Step 4). Missing strategic inputs are among the most common sources of launch readiness gaps.
