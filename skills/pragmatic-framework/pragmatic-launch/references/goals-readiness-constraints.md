# Goals, Readiness, and Constraints: The Three Dimensions of Launch

A successful product launch requires examining three critical dimensions. Together, they replace the mythical "launch checklist" with a planning process that adapts to the unique conditions of each launch.

## Dimension 1: Launch Goals

Launch goals are the anchor for everything that follows. Without specific goals, resources will be misaligned, budgets will swing based on opinion rather than rationale, and there's no way to measure success.

### Setting Effective Launch Goals

Goals must be **SMART:**
- **Specific** — What exactly will be achieved? (Not "be successful" but "generate 50 qualified pipeline opportunities")
- **Measurable** — How will you know? (Define the metric and the measurement method)
- **Attainable** — Is this realistic given readiness and constraints? (Grounded in market evidence, not enthusiasm)
- **Relevant** — Does this goal connect to business objectives? (Tied to revenue, market share, or strategic position)
- **Time-bound** — By when? (Define the launch window — typically 90 days post-launch)

### Goal Categories

**Pipeline Goals:**
- Number of qualified opportunities created within launch window
- Pipeline value generated
- Sales-qualified leads from launch programs
- Example: "Generate 75 SQLs worth $2M in pipeline within 90 days of launch"

**Revenue Goals:**
- Bookings closed within launch window
- Average deal size for launch product
- Revenue run-rate established by end of launch window
- Example: "Close $500K in new bookings within 90 days"

**Adoption Goals:**
- Free trial activations
- Product activations or provisioning
- Feature adoption rates (for existing product launches)
- Example: "Achieve 1,000 trial activations within 60 days"

**Awareness Goals:**
- Share of voice in target media
- Analyst inclusion in relevant reports
- Website traffic to launch-related pages
- Social media engagement and reach
- Example: "Secure coverage in 3 tier-1 industry publications within 30 days"

**Competitive Goals:**
- Competitive displacement wins
- Improvement in win rate against specific competitors
- Inclusion in competitive evaluations/shortlists
- Example: "Win 5 competitive displacement deals against [Competitor X] within 90 days"

**Channel Goals:**
- Partner activations or certifications
- Channel-sourced pipeline
- Deal registrations through partner ecosystem
- Example: "Activate 15 channel partners with certified sales reps within 60 days"

### Goal-Setting Pitfalls
- Setting goals based on what executives want to hear rather than what the market evidence supports
- Setting too many goals (focus on 2-3 primary goals)
- Setting goals without a measurement plan
- Confusing launch goals with annual business goals (launch goals are time-bound to the launch window)

## Dimension 2: Launch Readiness

Organizational readiness goes beyond the product being "done." Every functional area must be prepared to sell and support the product.

### The Launch Readiness Assessment

For each functional area, evaluate critical capabilities using this scoring framework:

**Importance (1-3):**
- 3 = Critical to launch success
- 2 = Important but not blocking
- 1 = Nice to have

**Readiness (0-3):**
- 3 = Fully ready, no action needed
- 2 = Mostly ready, minor gaps
- 1 = Significant gaps requiring attention
- 0 = Not ready at all

**Gap = Importance - Readiness** (positive gaps require action)

### Assessment Areas by Function

**Sales Team:**
- Buyer knowledge (economic buyer, user buyer, technical buyer)
- Problem understanding (problems the product addresses)
- Buyer criteria knowledge (what matters to buyers in this category)
- Buying process knowledge (how buyers evaluate and select)
- Product knowledge (capabilities, limitations, roadmap)
- Pricing knowledge (model, discounting, competitive pricing)
- Competitive knowledge (positioning, differentiation, objection handling)
- Tool readiness (demos, proposals, presentations, ROI calculators)

**Marketing:**
- Messaging and positioning assets
- Campaign content (web, email, social, advertising)
- Event and webinar plans
- PR and analyst relations preparation
- Lead generation programs
- Lead scoring and routing processes

**Customer Support:**
- Product training completed
- Knowledge base articles created
- Support escalation paths defined
- SLA adjustments if needed
- Known issues documented with workarounds

**Operations:**
- Billing and invoicing systems updated
- Provisioning and fulfillment processes ready
- Contract templates updated
- Order processing tested

**Legal:**
- Terms of service updated
- Compliance requirements met
- Partner agreements in place
- Data privacy requirements addressed

**Channel/Partners:**
- Partner enablement materials ready
- Partner certification program launched
- Partner portal updated
- Co-marketing plans confirmed
- Partner incentive structure defined

**Engineering:**
- Product documentation complete
- API documentation updated
- Integration guides published
- Known issues and workarounds documented
- Support engineering team briefed

### Using Assessment Results

1. **Gaps with importance ≥ 2 and readiness ≤ 1** — These are launch blockers. Build remediation into the launch plan with specific owners and deadlines.
2. **Gaps with importance = 3 and readiness = 0** — These may require adjusting launch goals or timeline. Escalate to leadership.
3. **Areas with readiness ≥ importance** — These are ready. No action needed.

The readiness assessment should be conducted **early enough** (6-8 weeks before launch for Tier 1) to allow time to close gaps. Conducting it the week before launch is too late.

## Dimension 3: Launch Constraints

Constraints are fixed conditions the launch plan must respect. Unlike readiness gaps (which can be closed), constraints must be planned around.

### Types of Constraints

**Time Constraints:**
- Contractual deadlines with customers or partners
- Competitive windows (launching before/after a competitor)
- Seasonal buying patterns in the target market
- Industry events with fixed dates
- Regulatory compliance deadlines

**Budget Constraints:**
- Total launch budget available
- Allocation restrictions (e.g., no budget for paid media)
- Approval thresholds and processes
- Timing of budget availability

**Resource Constraints:**
- Available headcount and their bandwidth
- Competing priorities for shared resources (design, content, web)
- Geographic or timezone coverage gaps
- Specialized skill availability (e.g., analyst relations, PR agency)

**Dependency Constraints:**
- Engineering delivery confidence (is the ship date firm?)
- Partner readiness timelines
- Third-party certifications or approvals
- Infrastructure or platform dependencies

**Market Constraints:**
- Known competitive launches in the same window
- Market events that could overshadow the launch
- Economic conditions affecting buyer behavior
- Regulatory changes in progress

### Working with Constraints

1. **Document all constraints explicitly** — Don't assume everyone knows about them.
2. **Assess constraint severity** — Which constraints are truly fixed vs. which could potentially be moved?
3. **Make tradeoffs transparent** — When constraints limit what's possible, show leadership the tradeoff explicitly (e.g., "Given the budget constraint, we can execute strategies A and B but not C").
4. **Build contingency plans** — For the most impactful constraints, have a Plan B ready.
5. **Monitor constraint changes** — Constraints can shift. A competitive launch might move. An engineering date might slip. Update the plan accordingly.
