# Sales Enablement for Launch

Sales enablement is where most product launches break down. The typical pattern: product marketing delivers a feature-focused presentation, a product demo, and a brochure. Sales smiles politely, then spends the next three months figuring out on their own how to actually sell the product.

The Pragmatic approach to launch sales enablement focuses on teaching sales to **have conversations with buyers about their problems** — not to memorize product features.

## The Core Problem

When products are "thrown over the wall" to sales without adequate enablement:
- Expect at least a 3-month delay in building meaningful pipeline
- Sales creates their own tools (without marketing input), leading to inconsistent messaging
- Multiple stops and starts as sales gradually discovers what works
- Product marketing gets pulled into firefighting — creating more slides, more collateral, more demos
- Sales loses confidence in the product and focuses on selling other products to make quota

## Enablement Principles

### 1. Train How to Sell, Not How to Use

Sales training should answer: "How do I have a conversation with [buyer persona] about [their problem] and guide them toward our solution?"

It should NOT answer: "Here are all the features of Widget 3.0 and how they work."

**Wrong approach:**
- 60-minute product demo walkthrough
- Feature-by-feature comparison chart
- Technical architecture presentation

**Right approach:**
- "When talking to a CFO who is struggling with [problem], here's how to open the conversation..."
- "The economic buyer cares about [criteria]. Here's how to connect our solution to those criteria..."
- "When the technical buyer asks about [concern], here's market evidence that addresses it..."

### 2. Align Tools to the Buying Process

Every sales tool should map to a specific stage of the buyer's journey. If you can't identify which buying stage a tool supports, it probably isn't useful.

**Sales Tool Map Structure:**

| Buying Stage | Buyer Need | Sales Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | "I have a problem" | Problem-focused content, industry research | Validate the buyer's problem and establish credibility |
| Education | "What solutions exist?" | Solution overview, buyer's guide | Help buyer understand solution categories and criteria |
| Selection | "Which solution is best?" | Competitive comparisons, ROI calculator, demo | Differentiate and prove value |
| Purchase | "How do I justify this?" | Business case template, reference customers, pricing proposal | Remove purchase barriers |
| Implementation | "How do I succeed?" | Onboarding guide, success plan, training | Reduce time-to-value and ensure adoption |

### 3. Focus on the Shortest Path

Not all prospects are equal at launch. Help sales identify the shortest path to achieving launch goals:

- **Existing customers** — Already trust you. Lower effort to sell. Consider upsell/cross-sell first.
- **Active evaluators** — Already in buying mode. Need to see why you're the right choice now.
- **Dissatisfied competitor customers** — Open to switching. Need to see differentiation and migration path.
- **New prospects** — Highest effort. Need full awareness-to-purchase journey support.

## Key Sales Enablement Deliverables

### Competitive Battle Cards
One-page reference per major competitor:
- Their positioning vs. yours
- Their strengths (be honest — sales needs to know)
- Their weaknesses and how to exploit them
- Common objections when competing against them and responses
- Landmine questions to plant that highlight competitor weaknesses
- Recent win/loss insights

### Buyer Conversation Guides (Per Persona)
For each buyer persona involved in the purchase:
- Their role in the buying decision
- Problems they care about most
- Questions to ask to uncover needs
- Key messages that resonate with this persona
- Proof points and evidence they trust
- Common objections from this persona and responses
- Trigger events that create urgency

### Objection Handling Playbook
Organized by objection category:
- **Price objections** — "It's too expensive" / "Competitor is cheaper"
- **Timing objections** — "Not now" / "We're in a budget freeze"
- **Competitive objections** — "We're already using [Competitor]"
- **Risk objections** — "You're too new" / "We need references"
- **Feature objections** — "You don't have [feature]"
- **Organizational objections** — "We need to involve more stakeholders"

For each: acknowledge the concern, reframe around the buyer's problem, provide evidence, and offer a next step.

### Demo Scripts
Problem-focused, not feature-focused:
- Open with the buyer's problem (not your product)
- Show the "day in the life" before and after
- Focus on 3-4 key capabilities that solve the stated problem
- Include natural discovery questions within the demo flow
- End with a clear next step tied to the buying process

### ROI/Business Case Tools
Help buyers justify the purchase internally:
- ROI calculator (input their numbers, see projected value)
- Business case template (they can customize and present to leadership)
- Industry benchmarks and statistics
- Total cost of ownership comparison

### Reference Customer Stories
Structured as problem-solution-results:
- What problem did the customer face?
- Why did they choose your solution?
- What measurable results did they achieve?
- Pull-quote from the customer
- Customer's industry, size, and segment (for relevance matching)

## Sales Training Format

For Tier 1 launches, sales training should include:

1. **Market context** (15 min) — Why this launch matters, what problems the market faces, competitive landscape
2. **Buyer personas** (15 min) — Who buys, what they care about, how they evaluate
3. **Positioning and messaging** (15 min) — How to talk about the product in terms of buyer problems
4. **Buying process and sales tools** (20 min) — Walk through the sales tool map, show how each tool is used at each buying stage
5. **Competitive positioning** (15 min) — How to win against specific competitors, battle card walkthrough
6. **Practice** (30 min) — Role-play buyer conversations, practice objection handling
7. **Q&A** (10 min) — Address concerns and gaps

Total: ~2 hours of focused, practical training (not a 4-hour product feature marathon).

## Measuring Sales Enablement Effectiveness

Track these indicators post-launch:
- Time from launch to first qualified opportunity
- Sales tool usage and feedback
- Win rate for launch product deals
- Average deal cycle length
- Most common sales questions (indicates enablement gaps)
- Competitive win/loss ratio
- Sales confidence survey (do they feel equipped to sell?)
