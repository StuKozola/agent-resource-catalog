# Operations Needs Assessment Guide

## Purpose

Before you can effectively support the operations group, you need to understand what operations teams exist, what systems they manage, and where they lack market and solution context. This assessment follows the Pragmatic principle of NIHITO (Nothing Important Happens In The Office) — but applied internally: you must go talk to your operations teams to understand their real needs, not assume what they require.

## Identifying Your Operations Groups

Most organizations have some combination of the following operations functions. Each has distinct information needs from product marketing.

### Marketing Operations (MOps)
**What they manage:** Marketing automation platforms (Marketo, HubSpot, Pardot, Eloqua), email infrastructure, landing pages, form strategy, lead scoring models, nurture workflows, campaign reporting, martech stack integration.

**What they need from product marketing:**
- Buyer persona definitions with specific attributes that can be captured in forms and used for segmentation
- Buying stage definitions that map to lifecycle stages in the MAP
- Segment criteria that translate to list-building rules
- Campaign themes and messaging frameworks for nurture track design
- Launch timelines and campaign requirements for operational readiness
- Content mapping by persona and buying stage for automated workflows

### Sales Operations (SOps)
**What they manage:** CRM configuration (Salesforce, HubSpot CRM, Dynamics), pipeline stages, lead routing rules, territory assignments, quoting and CPQ systems, sales reporting and dashboards, deal desk processes.

**What they need from product marketing:**
- Buying process stages that map to opportunity pipeline stages
- Competitive displacement data for routing rules and alerts
- Solution packaging and pricing structures for CPQ configuration
- Persona-based lead scoring criteria for MQL/SQL definitions
- Win/loss patterns that inform pipeline stage exit criteria
- Market segment data for territory planning and account scoring

### Revenue Operations (RevOps)
**What they manage:** Cross-functional alignment of marketing, sales, and customer success systems; unified reporting; revenue attribution; data governance; tech stack rationalization.

**What they need from product marketing:**
- Unified persona and segment definitions that work across the full customer lifecycle
- Consistent buying stage vocabulary shared by marketing and sales
- Market-informed attribution models (which touchpoints matter for which personas)
- Competitive context for churn prediction and retention modeling
- Product usage data requirements tied to user personas

### Business Operations (BizOps)
**What they manage:** Strategic planning support, operational efficiency, cross-functional process design, executive reporting, resource allocation.

**What they need from product marketing:**
- Market sizing data (TAM/SAM/SOM) for planning and forecasting
- Competitive landscape summaries for strategic planning
- Product-market fit indicators for portfolio decisions
- Customer segment performance data for resource allocation

## Conducting the Needs Assessment

### Step 1: Map the Current State
For each operations group, document:
1. **Team structure** — Who leads it? How many people? Who are the key contacts?
2. **Systems owned** — What platforms and tools do they manage?
3. **Current information sources** — Where do they get market and solution information today?
4. **Known gaps** — What information do they wish they had but don't?
5. **Pain points** — What breaks down most often due to lack of market context?

### Step 2: Interview Operations Leaders
Schedule 30-45 minute conversations with operations leaders using these questions:

**Understanding their world:**
- Walk me through how a new campaign or program gets operationalized from request to execution.
- When you're configuring a new nurture track or lead scoring model, what information do you need that you often don't have?
- How do you currently define your lifecycle stages or pipeline stages? What informed those definitions?

**Identifying gaps:**
- When was the last time you had to make a system configuration decision without enough market context? What happened?
- If you could get one piece of market intelligence on a regular basis that you don't get today, what would it be?
- How do you handle launches from an ops perspective? What do you wish product marketing told you earlier?

**Understanding preferences:**
- How do you prefer to receive information — documents, meetings, Slack updates, wiki pages?
- What format works best for system configuration — structured data tables, narrative briefs, or both?
- How far in advance do you need information before a launch or major campaign?

### Step 3: Identify Patterns and Priorities
After interviewing all operations groups, look for:
- **Common information needs** that you can address once and share broadly
- **Unique needs** that require tailored deliverables for specific ops functions
- **Urgent gaps** where the lack of information is causing immediate problems
- **Structural gaps** where no information-sharing process exists at all

### Step 4: Define Your Support Model
Based on the assessment, determine:
- What standard deliverables will you produce (operations support briefs, persona mapping docs, etc.)?
- What cadence will you maintain for updates (monthly, quarterly, per-launch)?
- Who is the primary liaison between product marketing and each ops group?
- What channels will you use for information sharing (wiki, Slack, email, meetings)?

## Common Anti-Patterns to Avoid

**The "throw it over the wall" approach:** Sharing raw positioning documents or persona decks without translating them into operations-ready formats. Ops teams need structured data they can configure into systems, not narrative strategy documents.

**The "launch surprise" approach:** Informing ops teams about a new product, segment, or campaign at the last minute. Operations readiness requires lead time for system configuration, testing, and quality assurance.

**The "one and done" approach:** Providing ops teams with persona or segment information once and never updating it. Markets evolve, personas shift, and competitive landscapes change — ops configurations should evolve with them.

**The "assume they know" approach:** Expecting operations teams to extract what they need from broadly shared materials like business plans or marketing plans. Ops teams are busy running systems; they need curated, relevant information delivered proactively.
