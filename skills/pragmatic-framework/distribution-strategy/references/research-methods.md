# Research Methods for Distribution Strategy

Understanding your market's buying preferences requires structured research. This reference covers practical methods for gathering the insights that inform distribution strategy decisions.

## Core Principle: Outside-In

The Pragmatic Framework is built on the principle that the answer to most of your questions is not inside your building. Distribution strategy research must be grounded in how the market actually buys — not in assumptions made by your product team, sales leadership, or executives.

## Primary Research Methods

### Win/Loss Interviews

Win/loss analysis is a separate Pragmatic Framework activity (in the Market category), but its findings directly feed distribution strategy. Key questions for the distribution angle:

- How did you first hear about our product (and the alternatives)?
- Walk me through your evaluation process from start to finish.
- Who was involved in the buying decision? What were their roles?
- Where did you expect to find a product like ours?
- Was the purchasing process easy or difficult? What would you change?
- Did you consider buying through a different channel? Why or why not?

**Important**: Win/loss interviews should be conducted by someone not involved in the sales effort (per Pragmatic best practices) to ensure objectivity.

### Buyer Persona Interviews

If buyer personas have been developed (another Pragmatic Framework activity), their documentation should include buying behavior insights. If not, interview target buyers to understand:

- Their typical procurement process for this product category
- Who influences and who approves purchases
- Whether they prefer self-service, sales-assisted, or consultant-guided buying
- What information sources they trust during evaluation
- Their expectations around delivery, onboarding, and post-sale support

**Tip**: Interview 10-15 buyers per segment for reliable patterns. Look for convergence in how they describe their buying process.

### Customer Advisory Boards

For existing products, a customer advisory board can provide candid feedback on distribution preferences. Ask:

- If you were buying this product again today, how would you want to purchase it?
- What's working well about how you receive and access the product?
- What's frustrating about the current purchasing or delivery experience?
- Are there channels you use for similar products that we should consider?

### Channel Partner Interviews

If you already use indirect channels (or are evaluating them), interview current and prospective partners:

- What products in your portfolio sell well, and why?
- What would make our product attractive (or unattractive) for your portfolio?
- How do your customers prefer to buy products in our category?
- What support do you need from manufacturers to be successful?
- What margin structure makes this economically viable for you?

## Secondary Research Methods

### Competitive Channel Analysis

Document how competitors distribute their products:

- Which channels do they use (direct, partners, marketplaces, retail)?
- What is their apparent channel mix emphasis?
- Are they under- or over-serving any segments through their channel choices?
- Are there channel gaps you can exploit?
- What can you learn from their channel partner programs?

Sources: competitor websites, partner directories, job postings (they reveal channel-related hiring), industry analyst reports, trade publication coverage.

### Industry and Analyst Reports

Industry analysts (Gartner, Forrester, IDC, etc.) often publish research on buying behavior and channel trends for specific markets. Look for:

- Buyer journey maps for your product category
- Channel preference surveys
- Market sizing by channel type
- Emerging channel trends (e.g., the rise of marketplace purchasing in B2B software)

### Market Data and Benchmarks

Quantitative data points that inform channel decisions:

- What percentage of purchases in your category happen online vs. through sales vs. through partners?
- What is the average deal size by channel?
- What are typical channel margins in your industry?
- What is the customer acquisition cost by channel?

Sources: industry associations, trade publications, government trade data, SaaS benchmarking reports (for software), analyst firms.

### Customer Journey Mapping

If the Buyer Experience activity has been completed, that journey map is a direct input. If not, construct a simplified buying journey:

1. **Awareness** — How do buyers first learn about solutions in this category?
2. **Research** — Where do they go to evaluate options?
3. **Evaluation** — How do they compare and shortlist?
4. **Purchase** — Through what mechanism do they actually transact?
5. **Onboarding** — How do they expect to receive and begin using the product?
6. **Expansion** — How do they buy more (upsell, cross-sell, renewals)?

Map each stage to the channels that serve it. This reveals whether a single channel covers the entire journey or whether multiple channels are needed at different stages.

## Synthesizing Findings

After gathering data from multiple sources, synthesize into a channel recommendation by answering:

1. **What are the dominant buying patterns?** — Where do most buyers in each segment actually purchase?
2. **Are there underserved preferences?** — Are buyers forced into channels they don't prefer because alternatives aren't available?
3. **What channel economics work?** — Which channels can deliver adequate margins given your cost structure?
4. **What can you execute?** — Do you have (or can you build) the capabilities to support the preferred channels?
5. **What does the competitive landscape suggest?** — Is there a channel-based differentiation opportunity?

Document the evidence base for your channel recommendations. In Pragmatic terms, this means using market facts — not opinions — to drive the strategy. As Pragmatic Marketing Rule #6 states: "Your opinion, although interesting, is irrelevant."

## Research Output Template

Structure your findings as:

```
DISTRIBUTION STRATEGY RESEARCH FINDINGS

Segment: [Target segment name]

Buying Behavior Summary:
- Discovery channels: [How buyers find products]
- Evaluation process: [How they compare options]
- Purchase preference: [How they want to buy]
- Delivery expectation: [How they want to receive/access]
- Post-sale expectation: [Support and relationship needs]

Data Sources:
- [X] win/loss interviews conducted
- [X] buyer persona interviews conducted
- [Y] analyst reports reviewed
- [Z] competitive channel analyses completed

Key Findings:
1. [Finding with supporting evidence]
2. [Finding with supporting evidence]
3. [Finding with supporting evidence]

Channel Implications:
- [Recommended channel and rationale]
- [Alternative channels considered and why they were deprioritized]
```

Repeat for each target segment if buying behaviors differ across segments.
