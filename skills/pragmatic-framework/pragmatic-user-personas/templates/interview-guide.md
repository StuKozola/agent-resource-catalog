# User Research Interview Guide

> **Purpose:** A structured interview script for conducting user persona discovery interviews. Adapt questions based on your specific research objectives and the user's context.
>
> **Duration:** 45-60 minutes
> **Format:** Semi-structured, one-on-one
> **Recording:** Always request consent before recording

## Opening Script (5 minutes)

"Thank you so much for taking the time to speak with me today. My name is [name] and I'm joined by [note-taker name] who will be helping me take notes.

We're doing research to better understand how people like you [describe the task domain, e.g., 'manage logistics operations' or 'create marketing content']. We want to understand your experience — what works well, what's frustrating, and what your day actually looks like.

There are no right or wrong answers. We're not testing you, and this isn't a sales call. We genuinely want to learn from your experience. If a question doesn't make sense, just say so, and we'll move on.

Would it be okay if we record this conversation? It helps us capture details accurately and we won't share it outside our research team."

[Wait for consent. If declined, proceed with notes only.]

"Great. Let's get started."

---

## Section 1: Context & Background (10 minutes)

*Goal: Understand the person, their role, and their daily world before diving into specifics.*

1. "Tell me about your role. What do you do day-to-day?"

2. "How long have you been in this role? How did you get into it?"

3. "Walk me through a typical day. What does your morning look like? When do things get busy?"

4. "Who do you work most closely with? How does your work connect to theirs?"

5. "What are the biggest priorities or pressures you deal with in your role right now?"

**Listen for:** Role context, team dynamics, time pressures, organizational structure, and the landscape in which product usage sits.

---

## Section 2: Goals & Motivations (10 minutes)

*Goal: Understand what the user is trying to accomplish and why it matters to them.*

6. "When you think about [relevant task area], what are you ultimately trying to achieve?"

7. "What does a really good outcome look like? How do you know when things went well?"

8. "What would change for you — in your work or your life — if you could do this perfectly every time?"

9. "How is your success in this area measured? By you? By your manager?"

10. "What motivates you to put extra effort into [task area]? What makes you cut corners?"

**Listen for:** Primary goals, success metrics (formal and informal), intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation, and the gap between current and desired state.

---

## Section 3: Current Workflow & Behaviors (10-15 minutes)

*Goal: Map the actual step-by-step process, including tools, handoffs, and decision points.*

11. "Walk me through the last time you [performed the relevant task]. Start from the very beginning — what triggered it?"

12. "What was the first thing you did? Then what?"
    - [Follow the thread: "And then?" "What happened next?" until the task is complete]

13. "What tools or systems did you use along the way? How did you move between them?"

14. "Was anyone else involved? How did you coordinate or hand off?"

15. "How long did the whole thing take? Is that typical?"

16. "Have you figured out any shortcuts or tricks that make this easier?"

17. "Has your approach changed since you first started doing this? What's different now?"

**Listen for:** Actual workflow (not idealized), tools used, workarounds, collaboration patterns, decision points, and how the process has evolved.

---

## Section 4: Pain Points & Frustrations (10 minutes)

*Goal: Uncover specific friction points, not general complaints.*

18. "What's the most frustrating part of [task area]? Tell me about a specific time."

19. "What takes longer than you think it should?"

20. "When things go wrong, what typically happens? Give me an example."

21. "What do you wish someone had told you when you first started doing this?"

22. "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about how you do this work, what would it be?"

23. "Is there anything you've given up trying to fix — you just live with it?"

**Listen for:** Specific frustrations with examples (not vague complaints), emotional responses, resignation vs. active frustration, and workarounds that mask pain.

---

## Section 5: Environment & Context (5 minutes)

*Goal: Understand the physical and digital environment in which they work.*

24. "Describe your workspace when you're doing this work. What's around you?"

25. "What's on your screen? What other applications are usually open?"

26. "How often are you interrupted while doing this? What pulls you away?"

27. "Do you do this work at a desk, on the go, or both? What device(s) do you use?"

28. "Is your internet connection reliable? Does performance or speed matter for your work?"

**Listen for:** Physical workspace details, digital tool ecosystem, interruption patterns, device usage, and environmental constraints.

---

## Section 6: Attitudes & Emotions (5 minutes)

*Goal: Understand how they feel about the domain and about using tools in this area.*

29. "How do you feel about [product category] tools in general? Are they helping or just another thing to manage?"

30. "When you encounter a new tool or feature, what's your first reaction? Excited, cautious, annoyed?"

31. "Who do you turn to when you need help with [task/product]? Why them?"

32. "What excites you about where things are heading in your field? What worries you?"

**Listen for:** Attitude toward technology, openness to change, support-seeking behavior, and emotional relationship with their work.

---

## Section 7: Future-Looking (5 minutes)

*Goal: Understand aspirations and what would make them switch approaches or tools.*

33. "If you imagine your work a year or two from now, what's different? What do you hope has changed?"

34. "What would make you switch to a completely different tool or approach?"

35. "What advice would you give to someone just starting in your role?"

**Listen for:** Future aspirations, switching triggers (reveals unmet needs), and the advice question often surfaces what the user wishes they had known.

---

## Wrap-Up (5 minutes)

36. "Is there anything important about your experience that we haven't covered? Anything else you'd want us to know?"

37. "Is there anyone else you'd recommend we talk to — someone who does similar work but might have a different perspective?"

"Thank you so much for your time. This has been incredibly helpful. [Explain next steps if appropriate — e.g., 'We may reach out for a brief follow-up if we have clarifying questions.']"
