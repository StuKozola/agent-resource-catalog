## 1. From Research to Personas

Persona creation is the synthesis step that transforms raw research data into actionable user archetypes. The process is both analytical (identifying patterns in data) and creative (bringing those patterns to life as a believable character).

### The Two-Stage Approach

Pragmatic Institute recommends a two-stage persona development process:

**Stage 1: Provisional Personas** — Created early, based on available knowledge and initial research. These are hypotheses about who your users are, explicitly marked as unvalidated. They guide further research and early design decisions.

**Stage 2: Refined Personas** — Created after substantial user research. These are validated archetypes supported by data. They become the authoritative reference for product decisions.

This staged approach avoids two failure modes: (1) waiting so long for "perfect" research that teams make decisions without any persona guidance, and (2) treating assumption-based personas as fact.

## 2. Provisional Personas

### When to Create Provisional Personas

- At the start of a new product initiative before user research is complete
- When entering a new market segment where you have limited direct user contact
- When you need to align the team on initial assumptions to plan research
- As collaborative artifacts between product and design teams

### How to Build a Provisional Persona

1. **Gather the team:** Product manager, designer, and 1-2 stakeholders with user exposure (support, sales, services)
2. **Brain-dump known information:** What do we think we know about our users? Sources?
3. **Identify distinct user types:** Based on available data, how many meaningfully different user types exist?
4. **Draft provisional personas:** Use the `templates/provisional-persona.md` template
5. **Mark confidence levels:** For each persona attribute, note whether it's based on data, stakeholder knowledge, or assumption
6. **Define validation plan:** What specific research will confirm or challenge each assumption?

### Provisional Persona Essentials

A provisional persona should include at minimum:
- A name and brief description
- Hypothesized goals (what they're trying to accomplish)
- Hypothesized behaviors (how they currently approach the task)
- Hypothesized pain points (where they struggle)
- Key assumptions to validate (explicitly stated)
- Confidence level indicators (data-backed, stakeholder-informed, or assumption)

## 3. Refined Personas

### When to Transition from Provisional to Refined

- After completing 8+ user interviews per persona type
- When behavioral clusters are stable across multiple data sources
- When the team can predict new interviewees' responses
- When empathy maps for each persona type are well-populated

### Building a Refined Persona

1. **Review all research data** — Interview transcripts, contextual inquiry notes, empathy maps, analytics
2. **Confirm behavioral clusters** — Validate that the groups identified during analysis are distinct and meaningful
3. **Select the archetype** — Choose the most representative example from each cluster (not an average, but a vivid archetype)
4. **Draft the persona narrative** — Write the persona as a character, not a spreadsheet row
5. **Populate all persona dimensions** — Use the `templates/refined-persona.md` template
6. **Include supporting evidence** — Attach representative quotes, photos of environments (with consent), and data points
7. **Peer review** — Have the research team and 2-3 stakeholders review for accuracy and resonance

## 4. Persona Anatomy — What to Include

### Core Elements (Required)

**Identity:**
- Name (memorable, realistic, not cute or punny)
- Photo or illustration (realistic; avoid stock photos that feel generic)
- One-line description ("Sarah is a regional operations manager who coordinates logistics across three warehouses")

**Goals:**
- Primary goal: What they are ultimately trying to accomplish
- Secondary goals: Supporting objectives
- Life/career goals: Broader aspirations that provide context
- Product-specific goals: What they want from this specific product

**Behaviors:**
- Current workflow: Step-by-step how they accomplish relevant tasks today
- Tool ecosystem: What other tools, systems, and resources they use
- Information sources: Where they go to learn, get help, or stay current
- Collaboration patterns: Who they interact with and how

**Attitudes:**
- Toward the product category (enthusiastic, pragmatic, skeptical, resistant)
- Toward technology in general
- Toward change and learning new tools
- Toward their own expertise level

**Pain Points:**
- Task-level frustrations (specific workflow breakdowns)
- Emotional frustrations (feelings of confusion, anxiety, being undervalued)
- Environmental constraints (time pressure, resource limits, organizational politics)

**Context/Environment:**
- Physical workspace description
- Digital environment (platforms, devices, connectivity)
- Organizational context (team size, reporting structure, autonomy level)
- Time and attention constraints

### Supporting Elements (Recommended)

**Skills and Knowledge:**
- Domain expertise level
- Technical proficiency
- Learning style and preferences
- How skills evolve over time with the product

**Key Quotes:**
- 2-3 verbatim quotes from research that capture the persona's voice
- These quotes should be memorable and emotionally resonant

**Day-in-the-Life Narrative:**
- A brief story describing a typical day, focusing on where the product fits
- Highlights triggers, workflows, collaboration points, and pain moments

**Empathy Map Summary:**
- Condensed version of the Says/Thinks/Does/Feels analysis

### Elements to Exclude

- Irrelevant demographics (age and gender unless they directly impact product usage)
- Technology brand preferences (unless the product has platform dependencies)
- Hobbies and personal interests (unless directly relevant to product context)
- Made-up details that sound good but don't come from research

## 5. Classifying and Prioritizing Personas

### Classification

After creating refined personas, classify each one:

**Primary Persona:**
- The user whose needs drive the core product design
- If you can only optimize for one persona, this is the one
- There should be exactly one primary persona per product or major product area
- Criteria: largest validated user group AND highest strategic alignment with Market Definition

**Secondary Persona(s):**
- Users whose needs are accommodated when doing so doesn't compromise the primary persona's experience
- Typically 1-3 secondary personas
- Their needs may drive optional features, configurations, or alternative workflows

**Negative Persona(s):**
- Users you explicitly will NOT design for
- Including a negative persona gives the team permission to say "no" to feature requests that serve only this user type
- Extremely powerful for preventing scope creep and maintaining focus

### Prioritization Framework

Evaluate each persona against these criteria to determine design priority:

| Criterion | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Strategic Alignment** | How well does this persona align with Market Definition and target segments? | High |
| **Problem Urgency** | How urgent are this persona's validated market problems? | High |
| **User Volume** | How many users does this persona represent? | Medium |
| **Revenue Impact** | Does serving this persona drive revenue (directly or through retention)? | Medium |
| **Feasibility** | Can we realistically serve this persona with current or planned capabilities? | Medium |
| **Competitive Differentiation** | Does serving this persona differentiate us from alternatives? | Low-Medium |

## 6. Validation Techniques

### Internal Validation

**The Recognition Test:** Present the persona to sales, support, and services teams without revealing it's a persona. Ask "Do you know anyone like this?" If they immediately name specific customers, the persona resonates with real patterns.

**The Decision Test:** Use the persona to make a real product decision (feature prioritization, design choice). If the team can reach consensus more quickly by referencing the persona, it's working.

**The Contradiction Test:** Deliberately present a feature idea that conflicts with the persona's needs. If the team catches the conflict, the persona has been internalized.

### External Validation

**Survey Validation:** Create a brief survey based on persona attributes and distribute to a larger user sample. Check if the behavioral clusters hold at scale.

**New Interview Validation:** Interview 3-5 additional users per persona type. If they fit the persona without forcing, the persona is validated.

**Analytics Validation:** Check if behavioral segments in usage data align with persona definitions. Look for the behavioral patterns you described in actual usage metrics.

## 7. The Negative Persona

The negative persona is one of the most powerful and underutilized tools in the Pragmatic persona toolkit.

### Why Negative Personas Matter

- They give explicit permission to say "no" to certain feature requests
- They prevent the product from trying to be everything to everyone
- They protect the primary persona's experience from being diluted
- They help teams resolve feature debates by checking "which persona does this serve?"

### How to Identify Negative Personas

Negative personas typically emerge from:
- Users whose goals fundamentally conflict with the primary persona's goals
- Users whose technical requirements would force architectural compromises
- Users who are costly to serve relative to their revenue contribution
- Users who represent a market segment you've strategically decided not to pursue (per Market Definition)

### Negative Persona Document

A negative persona needs less detail than a primary/secondary persona. Include:
- Name and brief description
- Why they are a negative persona (strategic rationale)
- What requests or features they typically generate
- How to recognize requests that serve this persona
- The consequence of designing for this persona (what the primary persona would lose)
