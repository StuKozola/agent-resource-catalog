## Interview Flow

### Opening (2-3 minutes)

**Script (adapt to your style):**

"Thank you for taking the time to speak with me today. I'm [name], and I work on the product team at [company]. We're doing some research to better understand the challenges that [persona type] like you face in [domain area]. I'm here to listen and learn — there are no right or wrong answers, and I may not be able to answer product questions today, but your candid perspective is incredibly valuable to us.

Would you mind if I record this conversation? It helps me focus on listening rather than scribbling notes. The recording is just for my internal reference."

[Get verbal consent for recording]

"Great. Let's start with some context about you and your role."

---

### Section 1: Context & Role (5 minutes)

These questions establish who you're talking to and ground the conversation in their daily reality.

- "Can you tell me a bit about your role and what you're responsible for?"
- "Walk me through a typical week — what takes up most of your time?"
- "How long have you been in this role / industry?"

**Listen for:** Responsibilities that relate to your research objective. Note the language they use to describe their work.

---

### Section 2: Problems & Pain Points (15-20 minutes)

This is the core of the interview. Stay in this section as long as the conversation is productive.

**Opening probe:**
- "What are the biggest challenges you face when it comes to [domain relevant to your research objective]?"

**Deepening probes (use as follow-ups, not as a checklist):**
- "Tell me more about that. What happens when [problem they mentioned] occurs?"
- "How often does that come up?"
- "What's the impact when that happens? On you personally? On your team? On the business?"
- "How do you currently handle that? What's your workaround?"
- "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about [domain], what would it be?"
- "What would it mean for you if that problem were solved?"
- "Is this getting better or worse over time? Why?"

**Draw-out questions (use when they touch on something interesting):**
- "How long has this been happening?"
- "What did you do after that?"
- "Can you give me a specific example?"
- "You mentioned [X] — can you tell me more about that?"
- "You don't sound too convinced. Was there something else?"
- "Who else is affected by this?"

**Listen for:**
- Problems stated in the customer's own words (write these down verbatim)
- Emotional intensity — where do they get frustrated, animated, or emphatic?
- Workarounds — complexity of workarounds indicates severity of pain
- Impact — time, money, risk, frustration
- Feature requests — dig beneath them to find the underlying problem

---

### Section 3: Buying & Evaluation (5-10 minutes)

*Adjust based on interview type. Most relevant for recent evaluators.*

**For recent evaluators (wins and losses):**
- "What was the catalyst that made you start looking for a solution?"
- "Once you decided to look, what did you do first? Second? Third?"
- "Who else was involved in the decision? What were their priorities?"
- "What criteria mattered most when comparing options?"
- "What almost made you decide differently?"

**For current customers:**
- "If you were looking for this type of product again today, what would you do?"
- "What terms would you search for?"
- "Whose opinion would you seek out?"

**For non-customers:**
- "Are you aware of products that address [domain]? Which ones?"
- "What would need to be true for you to invest in a solution for [problem]?"
- "What would the buying process look like at your organization?"

**Listen for:**
- The buying journey (steps, stakeholders, timeline)
- Decision criteria and their relative weight
- Information sources they trust
- Budget authority and approval processes

---

### Section 4: Competition & Alternatives (3-5 minutes)

- "Who do you consider the alternatives for [domain]? Not just direct competitors — anything you might use instead."
- "What could we learn from them?"
- "What do they do well? Where do they fall short?"

**Listen for:**
- Competitors you may not have considered (including non-software alternatives like spreadsheets, consultants, or manual processes)
- Competitive strengths and weaknesses in the customer's language
- Whether they see the competitive landscape differently than you do

---

### Section 5: Trends & Future (2-3 minutes)

- "What trends do you see in your industry right now that affect [domain]?"
- "What do you think will be different about your job in two years?"
- "Is there anything we didn't talk about that you think I should know?"

---

### Closing (2 minutes)

"Thank you so much for your time and your candor. This is incredibly valuable for us. A couple of closing questions:"

- "Would it be okay if I reached out again in the future with specific follow-up questions?"
- "Is there anyone else you'd recommend I talk to who faces similar challenges?"

"Thank you again. I really appreciate your time."
