# Upstream Inputs and Readiness for Channel Support

Channel Support (Enablement) depends on the quality and currency of inputs from other focus areas. This reference covers what you need before building channel training or ongoing channel support, how to assess readiness, and what to do when inputs are incomplete. It merges the former Channels "upstream inputs" and Channel Training "prerequisites check" references.

## Required Inputs

### From Positioning (`pragmatic-positioning`, Planning)
- **Positioning documents by segment and persona** — partners must communicate your value consistently. Without positioning, partners improvise messaging.
- **Key differentiators** — crisp, memorable differences from the alternatives.
- **Short positioning statement** — the concise message that anchors all communications.

**If missing:** extract at minimum a problem statement, target persona and primary differentiator. Flag Positioning as a priority. This is the #1 prerequisite for channel training: training without positioning produces partners who make up their own messaging.

### From Buyer Personas (`pragmatic-buyer-personas`, Planning)
- **Buyer persona profiles** — who sales channels sell to: role, problems, decision criteria, objections, language.
- **Persona-specific messaging** — how to frame the conversation for economic, technical and functional buyers.

**If missing:** identify at minimum the primary buyer role, their top 2–3 problems and typical evaluation criteria; validate later through market interviews.

### From User Personas (`pragmatic-user-personas`, Planning) — for delivery and usage channels
- **User profiles and workflows** — who implements, administers and uses the product, and what they need to get done. Service providers, implementers and support partners need this more than buyer profiles.

### From Competitive Landscape (`pragmatic-competitive-landscape`, Market)
- **Competitive analysis** — direct competitors, alternative approaches and doing nothing.
- **Competitive battle cards** — quick-reference comparisons (built in Assets).

**If missing:** identify the top 3 alternatives partners meet most often, with basic strengths, weaknesses and talking points.

### From Assets (`pragmatic-assets`, Enablement)
- **Existing tools** — decks, demos, ROI calculators, case studies, messaging guides built for direct teams.
- **Sales process documentation** — how the direct team sells, which informs partner processes.

**If missing:** inventory what exists; channel versions can start from direct materials. Training can reference assets in development; flag gaps and prioritize asset creation alongside training.

### From Distribution Strategy (`pragmatic-distribution-strategy`, Focus)
- **Channel strategy** — which sales, delivery and usage channels are in use and why.
- **Partner tier structure** — what each tier receives.
- **Channel economics** — margins, incentives, partner value proposition.

**If missing:** document at least the current channel types and the rationale for each.

## Recommended Inputs

### From Organizational Alignment (`pragmatic-organizational-alignment`, Go-to-Market)
- **Selling process mapped to the buying process**, and shared definitions of audience, value and responsibilities. If missing, design training around the buying process directly.

### From Market Problems (`pragmatic-market-problems`, Market)
- **Validated market problems** and **problem prevalence** by segment, so partners can qualify opportunities.

### From Win/Loss Analysis (`pragmatic-win-loss-analysis`, Market)
- **Channel-specific win/loss patterns** and **buying process insights** for partner-assisted purchases. If missing, use anecdotal stories from the field and advocate for a formal program.

### From Buyer Journey (`pragmatic-buyer-journey`, Planning)
- **Buyer journey map** showing where partners play a role, and **friction points** in channel-assisted purchases. If missing, use the sales team's view as a proxy and validate with win/loss data.

### From Adoption (`pragmatic-adoption`, Enablement) — for delivery and usage channels
- **Definition of meaningful use**, onboarding plans and training materials partners deliver to customers.

### From Marketing Plan (`pragmatic-marketing-plan`, Go-to-Market)
- **Upcoming campaigns and programs** partners should know about or join, and **lead routing** to partners.

## Decision Framework

**Green light — proceed.** Positioning exists for primary personas; personas are documented (even if preliminary); the buying process is understood at a basic level; some assets exist or are in development; the competitive landscape is reasonably understood.

**Yellow light — begin with caveats.** Positioning is incomplete or outdated. Proceed, but flag that training and support content will need updating, use the design work to surface gaps, and plan checkpoints to refresh as inputs mature.

**Red light — address inputs first.** Two or more Critical inputs are missing, or there is no shared understanding of personas or the buying process. Training and support built on these gaps will be inconsistent and undifferentiated. Invest in Positioning and Buyer Personas first; meanwhile, gather the field's current understanding of problems, buyers and competitors.

## Using Channel Work to Surface Upstream Gaps

Designing channel training and support exposes weaknesses elsewhere:

- **Positioning gaps** — "We don't have clear messaging for this persona."
- **Persona blind spots** — "We don't know what this buyer cares about."
- **Buying process ambiguity** — "We're not sure what happens between demo and decision."
- **Competitive weakness** — "We don't know how to win against this competitor."
- **Adoption gaps** — "Partners keep asking what a successful rollout looks like."

Document these and route them to the owners of the relevant focus areas.
