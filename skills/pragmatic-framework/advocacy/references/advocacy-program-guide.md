## Program Structure Overview

A customer advocacy program is a systematic approach to identifying, recruiting, and enabling satisfied customers to share their experiences with your market. Within the Pragmatic Framework, Advocacy sits in the Programs category alongside Marketing Plan, Revenue Growth, Revenue Retention, Launch, Awareness, Nurturing, and Measurement. It provides the authentic, outside-in evidence that makes every other Programs activity more credible.

### Program Components

A complete advocacy program has five operational layers:

**1. Identification Engine** — Systematic processes for finding potential advocates across your customer base. This is not ad hoc; it requires integration with Customer Success, Sales, and Support data.

**2. Recruitment Process** — A defined approach for inviting customers to participate, setting expectations, and onboarding new advocates.

**3. Story Capture Workflow** — Repeatable processes for interviewing customers, drafting advocacy assets, obtaining approvals, and publishing.

**4. Asset Deployment System** — Methods for matching advocacy assets to buyer personas, buying stages, and sales situations, then distributing them through the right channels.

**5. Relationship Management** — Ongoing care and feeding of the advocate relationship, including value exchange, recognition, and burnout prevention.

### Program Sizing

The size of your advocacy program should be proportional to your go-to-market complexity:

- **Early-stage / single product:** 5-10 active advocates, 2-4 case studies, focus on reference calls and online reviews
- **Growth-stage / multi-segment:** 20-50 active advocates, 8-12 case studies covering key verticals and personas, formal reference program
- **Enterprise / multi-product:** 50-200+ active advocates, comprehensive case study library organized by product, vertical, company size, and use case, Customer Advisory Board, advocate community

## Advocacy Asset Taxonomy

### Tier 1: Low-Commitment / High-Volume

**Customer Quotes / Pull-Quotes**
- Length: 1-3 sentences
- Effort: Minimal (often captured from existing communications)
- Use: Website, social media, presentations, collateral
- Approval: Email confirmation from the customer
- Example sources: Support tickets, NPS comments, email replies, social media posts, event feedback forms

**Online Reviews**
- Platforms: G2, Gartner Peer Insights, TrustRadius, Capterra, industry-specific review sites
- Effort: 10-15 minutes of the customer's time
- Use: Third-party validation, SEO, buyer research phase
- Approach: Make it easy — provide direct links to review platforms, suggest topics to cover (not exact words), follow up promptly after positive interactions

**Social Media Endorsements**
- Format: Customer-authored posts, shares, or comments about your product
- Effort: Minimal if organic; slightly more if prompted
- Use: Awareness, brand building, community engagement
- Approach: Share customer content, tag and thank advocates, create shareable moments (milestone celebrations, awards)

### Tier 2: Medium-Commitment / Core Program

**Written Testimonials**
- Length: 100-300 words
- Format: Structured quote with context (role, company, problem solved, result achieved)
- Effort: 15-30 minute interview or written Q&A
- Use: Website, sales collateral, presentations, email campaigns
- Approval: Written sign-off on final copy

**Case Studies (Written)**
- Length: 800-1,500 words (1-2 pages)
- Structure: Problem → Solution → Results (see case-study-framework.md for detailed guidance)
- Effort: 30-60 minute interview, 2-4 weeks production cycle including drafting, review, and approval
- Use: Website, sales enablement, nurturing sequences, event handouts
- Approval: Customer review of draft, legal/PR clearance if required by customer's organization

**Video Testimonials (Short-Form)**
- Length: 60-90 seconds
- Format: Customer speaking on camera about their experience
- Effort: 1-2 hours (including setup, multiple takes, and interview)
- Use: Website, social media, sales presentations, event screens
- Production: Can range from self-recorded smartphone video to professional production

### Tier 3: High-Commitment / High-Impact

**Video Case Studies (Long-Form)**
- Length: 3-5 minutes
- Format: Produced documentary-style piece with b-roll, graphics, and narrative structure
- Effort: Half-day to full-day shoot, 4-8 weeks production
- Use: Website hero content, keynote presentations, trade show displays
- Production: Professional videography recommended

**Reference Calls**
- Format: Live phone or video conversation between prospect and advocate
- Duration: 15-30 minutes typically
- Effort: Recurring time commitment from the advocate
- Use: Decision-stage deal acceleration
- Management: Track frequency to prevent advocate fatigue (recommend max 3-4 calls per quarter per advocate)

**Speaking Engagements**
- Format: Customer presents at conferences, webinars, user groups, or analyst briefings
- Effort: Significant (preparation, travel, presentation time)
- Use: Awareness, thought leadership, event credibility
- Value exchange: Speaking opportunities are often valuable for the advocate's own professional brand

**Customer Advisory Board (CAB) Membership**
- Format: Structured group of 12-20 strategic customers who meet quarterly
- Effort: Ongoing commitment (quarterly meetings plus ad hoc input)
- Use: Strategic guidance, product direction validation, deepest-level advocacy signal
- Value exchange: Early access to roadmap, direct executive relationships, influence on product direction

## The Ideal Advocate Profile

Not every satisfied customer makes a good advocate. The Ideal Advocate Profile helps you prioritize recruitment efforts.

### Qualification Criteria

Score potential advocates across these dimensions:

**1. Satisfaction Level (Weight: High)**
- NPS score of 9-10 (Promoter)
- Recent positive CSAT interactions
- Successful implementation or renewal
- Expansion or upsell activity (buying more = endorsing with their wallet)

**2. Story Strength (Weight: High)**
- Did the customer experience a clear, compelling problem that your product solved?
- Can they articulate measurable results (revenue gained, costs reduced, time saved, risk mitigated)?
- Is their use case representative of a target market segment or buyer persona?

**3. Brand Credibility (Weight: Medium)**
- Is the customer's company recognized in their industry?
- Does their company size and type match your target market profile?
- Would their name carry weight with your buyer personas?

**4. Willingness and Availability (Weight: Medium)**
- Does the individual have the personality and communication skills for advocacy?
- Does their organization's policies permit participation (some companies restrict external endorsements)?
- Do they have time and organizational support to participate?

**5. Relationship Depth (Weight: Medium)**
- Do you have a strong relationship with the individual (through CSM, account manager, or executive sponsor)?
- Have they informally advocated already (referrals, positive social posts, conference mentions)?

### Scoring Model

Rate each criterion 1-5 and apply weights to prioritize outreach:

| Criterion | Weight | Score (1-5) | Weighted Score |
|-----------|--------|-------------|----------------|
| Satisfaction Level | 25% | | |
| Story Strength | 25% | | |
| Brand Credibility | 20% | | |
| Willingness/Availability | 15% | | |
| Relationship Depth | 15% | | |
| **Total** | **100%** | | |

Advocates scoring 4.0+ are top-tier recruitment targets. Those scoring 3.0-3.9 are solid candidates. Below 3.0, focus on strengthening the relationship before asking for advocacy.

## Recruitment Strategies

### The Personal Ask

The most effective recruitment method is a personal request from someone the customer trusts. This is typically their Customer Success Manager, Account Manager, or an executive sponsor.

**Script Framework:**
1. Open with genuine appreciation for the relationship
2. Share why their specific story matters (name the problem they solved, the results they achieved)
3. Make the ask specific (not "would you be a reference?" but "would you be willing to do a 20-minute interview so we can write up how you reduced onboarding time by 40%?")
4. Explain what's in it for them (professional exposure, early access, networking, recognition)
5. Give them an easy out (no pressure, and make it clear this doesn't affect the relationship)

### Systematic Identification Channels

**NPS Follow-Up:** After every NPS survey, route Promoters (9-10) to the advocacy team with their verbatim comments. Follow up within 48 hours while sentiment is fresh.

**Customer Success Handoffs:** Build advocacy identification into the CSM workflow. After a successful QBR, implementation milestone, or renewal, the CSM flags potential advocates.

**Support Ticket Mining:** When customers leave glowing feedback after a support interaction, route those tickets to advocacy as recruitment signals.

**Event Networking:** Conference attendees and user group participants who show enthusiasm are natural advocate candidates. Capture their interest on the spot.

**Community Activity:** Customers who are active in your community forum, Slack channel, or user group are already self-selecting as advocates. Formalize the relationship.

**Win/Loss Integration:** Win/loss analysis often identifies the most enthusiastic new customers. Customers who chose you over a strong competitor have a compelling comparative story to tell.

### Overcoming Objections

Common objections and responses:

- **"I need to check with my legal/PR team."** — Offer to provide a sample case study and a simple participation agreement they can share with their legal team. Make approval easy.
- **"I don't have time."** — Start with the smallest possible ask. A 15-minute phone interview or a 2-paragraph email testimonial. Lower the barrier.
- **"I'm not comfortable being public."** — Offer anonymized options: "A Fortune 500 financial services company" instead of a named case study. Or limit visibility (sales reference calls only, not published).
- **"What's in it for me?"** — Be transparent about the value exchange. Professional recognition, early product access, executive networking, speaking opportunities, co-marketing.
- **"My company doesn't allow endorsements."** — Respect this. Ask if there are alternative formats they can participate in (anonymous case study, internal-only reference, speaking at a closed event).

## The Advocacy Ladder

The Advocacy Ladder is a progression model that moves customers through increasing levels of engagement over time. Never start at the top.

### Level 1: Passive Advocacy (Entry Point)
- Post an online review
- Provide a short quote for internal use
- Agree to be listed as a customer (logo usage)
- Share a social media post

### Level 2: Active Participation
- Complete a written testimonial (100-300 words)
- Participate in a brief interview for a case study
- Allow company name and use case to be published
- Serve as a reference for 1-2 calls per quarter

### Level 3: Engaged Advocacy
- Participate in a full case study (written and/or video)
- Serve as a regular reference (3-4 calls per quarter)
- Present at a webinar or virtual event
- Contribute to a blog post or co-authored content piece

### Level 4: Champion
- Speak at conferences or industry events
- Participate in analyst briefings
- Serve on Customer Advisory Board
- Mentor other customers in the community
- Co-develop thought leadership content

### Advancement Triggers

Move an advocate to the next level when:
- They express willingness to do more
- Their success story strengthens (new results, expanded usage)
- The relationship deepens (executive connections, multi-year partnership)
- They informally take on champion behaviors (unsolicited referrals, community leadership)

Never advance without asking. Each level increase requires a new conversation and a new commitment.

## Program Operations

### Advocacy Request Workflow

When Sales, Marketing, or other teams need advocacy assets, use a structured request process:

1. **Request Intake:** Requester submits: what type of asset, which persona/vertical/use case, by when, and for what purpose
2. **Match:** Advocacy program owner matches the request to available advocates using the segment coverage map
3. **Outreach:** Contact the matched advocate(s) to confirm availability and willingness for this specific activity
4. **Facilitate:** Coordinate the activity (schedule the call, conduct the interview, arrange the shoot)
5. **Produce:** Create the asset (draft, edit, design)
6. **Approve:** Send to the customer for review and sign-off
7. **Deploy:** Publish and distribute through appropriate channels
8. **Track:** Log the activity in the advocacy CRM/tracker for measurement and burnout prevention

### Advocacy Database

Maintain a centralized record of all advocates and advocacy activities:

- Advocate name, title, company, and contact info
- Product(s) used and use case
- Key results and metrics they can speak to
- Approved asset types (what they've agreed to do)
- Activity history (dates and types of advocacy activities completed)
- Utilization tracking (reference calls this quarter, total lifetime)
- Satisfaction signals (NPS score, health score, last interaction date)
- Notes and relationship context

### Legal and Compliance

- **Participation agreements:** Create a simple, clear agreement that covers what the customer is agreeing to, how their name/story will be used, and their right to withdraw
- **Approval workflows:** Every published asset must be reviewed and approved by the customer before release
- **Logo usage rights:** Separate permission for using the customer's company logo on your website, in presentations, or in marketing materials
- **Regulated industries:** Healthcare, financial services, government, and other regulated industries may have specific compliance requirements around endorsements and testimonials — always check

## Measuring Advocacy Impact

### Leading Indicators (Program Health)

These tell you whether the program is operationally healthy:

| Metric | Target Range | Measurement Frequency |
|--------|-------------|----------------------|
| Active advocates | Varies by company size | Monthly |
| Advocate pipeline coverage | 3:1 (advocates to monthly requests) | Monthly |
| New advocates recruited | 3-5 per quarter minimum | Quarterly |
| Advocate retention rate | >80% year-over-year | Annually |
| Asset production rate | Per quarterly goal | Quarterly |
| Average time-to-publish | <30 days from interview to live | Per asset |
| Segment coverage gaps | Zero uncovered priority segments | Quarterly |

### Lagging Indicators (Business Impact)

These tell you whether advocacy is driving business outcomes:

| Metric | How to Measure | Target |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| Influenced pipeline | Deals where advocacy assets were shared or reference calls occurred | Track in CRM |
| Win rate with advocacy | Compare close rate on deals that used advocacy vs. those that didn't | 10-20% lift |
| Sales cycle compression | Average days-to-close with vs. without advocacy | 10-15% shorter |
| Revenue influenced | Closed-won revenue on advocacy-touched deals | Quarterly reporting |
| Third-party review score | Average rating on G2, Gartner Peer Insights, etc. | ≥4.3/5.0 |
| Review velocity | New reviews posted per quarter | Steady or increasing |
| Reference request fulfillment | % of reference requests filled within SLA | >90% |

### Earned Advocacy Score

An alternative or complement to NPS for measuring advocacy health. The Earned Advocacy Score tracks actual advocacy behaviors (referrals given, reviews posted, case studies completed, events spoken at) rather than stated intent. This grounds measurement in actions, not just survey responses.

## Advocate Relationship Management

### The Value Exchange

Advocacy must be reciprocal. Advocates give you their time, reputation, and social capital. You must give value back. The value exchange should never feel transactional — it should feel like a genuine partnership.

**Professional Value:**
- Speaking opportunities that build their personal brand
- Case study and testimonial content they can use in their own portfolio
- LinkedIn recommendations and public recognition
- Invitations to executive roundtables and networking events
- Early access to product features, roadmap previews, and beta programs

**Practical Value:**
- Dedicated support channels or escalation paths
- Training credits or certification opportunities
- Product discounts or extended terms (where appropriate and ethical)
- Branded merchandise (quality items, not cheap swag)

**Relationship Value:**
- Executive access and direct relationships with leadership
- Community leadership roles (moderator, mentor, advisory board member)
- Genuine listening — advocates often have product feedback, and showing that you act on it deepens the relationship
- Personal recognition (handwritten thank-you notes, birthday acknowledgments, milestone celebrations)

### Preventing Advocate Burnout

The fastest way to lose an advocate is to over-ask. Implement guardrails:

- **Utilization caps:** No more than 3-4 reference calls per advocate per quarter unless they explicitly request more
- **Cool-down periods:** After a high-effort activity (video shoot, conference presentation), give the advocate at least one quarter before the next big ask
- **Activity rotation:** Vary the types of requests — don't always go to the same person for the same thing
- **Check-in cadence:** The CSM or advocacy manager should have a regular (quarterly minimum) relationship check-in with each active advocate that is NOT an advocacy ask — it's a genuine "how are things going?" conversation
- **Graceful exits:** If an advocate's situation changes (new job, product issues, reduced enthusiasm), make it easy for them to step back without awkwardness

### When Advocates Have Problems

If an advocate reports a product issue, service failure, or dissatisfaction, treat it as the highest priority. An advocate who experiences a problem and sees it resolved quickly becomes an even stronger advocate. An advocate whose problem is ignored becomes your most vocal detractor. Escalate immediately and follow up personally.
