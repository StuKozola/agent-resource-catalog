# Channel Training — Prerequisites Check

## Purpose

Channel Training builds on several upstream Pragmatic Framework activities. This reference helps you assess whether you have sufficient inputs to begin designing a Channel Training program — and what to do if some prerequisites are incomplete.

## Prerequisites Assessment Matrix

| Prerequisite Activity | Minimum Required Input | Ideal State | If Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positioning** | At least one positioning document per primary buyer persona, with problem statements and value messaging | Complete positioning documents for all target personas with validated messaging | Channel Training without positioning produces reps who make up their own messaging. STOP and complete positioning first. This is the #1 prerequisite. |
| **Buyer Personas** | Documented persona profiles for the 2-3 primary buyers involved in purchasing decisions | Full persona documents with problems, motivations, objections, and language patterns | Use preliminary persona sketches from sales team input. Flag for validation through market interviews. |
| **Buyer Experience** | Basic understanding of the buying stages and key decision points | Complete buyer journey map with information needs, barriers, and tools mapped to each stage | Use sales team's understanding of the buying process as a starting proxy. Plan to validate with win/loss data. |
| **Sales Alignment** | Selling process stages defined and mapped to the buying process | Full sales-to-buying process alignment with content and tool mapping at each stage | Design training around the buying process directly. Advocate for formal Sales Alignment work in parallel. |
| **Sales Tools** | At least basic tools (battle cards, one-pagers, demo scripts) available for key selling conversations | Complete sales tool suite mapped to selling process stages | Training can reference tools in development. Flag gaps and prioritize tool creation alongside training. |
| **Competitive Landscape** | Knowledge of top 3-5 alternatives and basic differentiation | Complete competitive analysis with positioning-based win strategies | Use sales team's competitive knowledge. Plan formal competitive analysis to strengthen training content. |
| **Win/Loss Analysis** | Some understanding of why recent deals were won or lost | Systematic win/loss program with pattern analysis feeding into training content | Use anecdotal win/loss stories from the sales team. Advocate for formal win/loss program. |

## Decision Framework

### Green Light — Ready to Build Channel Training
All of these are true:
- Positioning documents exist for primary buyer personas
- Buyer personas are documented (even if preliminary)
- The buying process is understood at a basic level
- Some sales tools exist or are in development
- Competitive landscape is reasonably understood

### Yellow Light — Can Begin with Caveats
Some positioning exists but it's incomplete or outdated. You can begin Channel Training design but must:
- Flag that training content will need updating as upstream activities mature
- Use the training design process itself to surface gaps in positioning, personas, and competitive knowledge
- Plan explicit checkpoints to refresh training as prerequisites are completed

### Red Light — Address Prerequisites First
If there is no positioning documented, or there is no shared understanding of buyer personas or buying process:
- Channel Training without these foundations will teach reps to sell in ways that are inconsistent, undifferentiated, and disconnected from the market
- Invest in Positioning and Buyer Persona work first
- In the interim, gather the sales team's current understanding of problems, buyers, and competitive landscape to begin informal training content development

## Using the Training Design Process to Surface Gaps

One benefit of the Channel Training design process is that it reveals weaknesses in upstream activities. As you build training curriculum, you'll naturally uncover:

- **Positioning gaps** — "We don't have clear messaging for this persona" surfaces when you try to write a training module for selling to that persona.
- **Persona blind spots** — "We don't know what this buyer cares about" surfaces when you try to create persona-specific training content.
- **Buying process ambiguity** — "We're not sure what happens between demo and decision" surfaces when you try to map sales tools to buying stages.
- **Competitive weakness** — "We don't know how to win against this competitor" surfaces when you try to write objection-handling scenarios.

Document these gaps and feed them back to the product marketing team as inputs for improving the upstream Pragmatic activities.
