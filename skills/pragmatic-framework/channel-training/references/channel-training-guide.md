## Audit and Gap Analysis

### Current State Assessment

Before designing any Channel Training program, conduct a thorough audit using these dimensions:

**Training Inventory Checklist:**
- List every existing training program, module, or session delivered to the channel in the past 12 months
- Categorize each as "product/feature training" vs. "selling training" vs. "process/logistics training"
- Note the delivery format (live, virtual, self-paced, documentation)
- Record the frequency and last delivery date
- Identify the owner (product marketing, sales ops, L&D, partner management)

**The Feature-vs-Selling Test:**
Review each training module and ask these diagnostic questions:
- Does this module teach reps what the product DOES, or what problems the product SOLVES?
- Does it reference buyer personas by name, or just describe "the customer" generically?
- Does it walk through the buying process, or the product's feature set?
- Does it include competitive scenarios, or just product specs?
- Does it provide practice selling conversations, or just information?

If the answers lean toward features, specs, and product capabilities, the training is product training — not Channel Training as Pragmatic defines it.

**Channel Feedback Collection:**
Gather input directly from channel reps using these methods:
- **Structured surveys** — Ask reps to rate their confidence in articulating market problems, handling top objections, navigating the buying process, and differentiating against competitors.
- **Ride-along observations** — Sit in on actual sales calls and note whether reps lead with problems or features.
- **Win/loss correlation** — Cross-reference training completion data with win/loss outcomes. Are trained reps winning more?
- **Content usage analysis** — Which training materials and sales tools do reps actually use? Which are ignored?
- **Partner advisory input** — Engage top-performing partners in a focused discussion about what training they wish they had.

**Gap Summary Framework:**
Organize findings into a gap matrix:

| Dimension | Current State | Desired State | Gap Severity (H/M/L) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem articulation | Reps describe features | Reps articulate persona problems | |
| Buyer persona knowledge | Generic "customer" references | Named personas with specific needs | |
| Buying process navigation | Reps follow their own pitch flow | Reps map to buyer's decision stages | |
| Competitive differentiation | Feature comparison sheets | Problem-based competitive positioning | |
| Objection handling | Ad hoc, rep-by-rep | Consistent, trained responses | |
| New product selling readiness | Training delayed or missing at launch | Training delivered before/at launch | |

## Objectives and Segmentation

### Setting Channel Training Objectives

Effective Channel Training objectives are behavioral and measurable. Avoid vague goals like "improve partner knowledge" in favor of specific selling behaviors:

**Behavioral Objectives (what reps should DO after training):**
- Articulate the top 3 market problems for each target buyer persona in the buyer's own language
- Conduct a discovery conversation using problem-based questions (not feature-based qualification)
- Navigate a buyer through their evaluation process using appropriate sales tools at each stage
- Handle the top 5 competitive objections with prepared, positioning-aligned responses
- Identify when a deal involves multiple buyer personas and adjust the conversation accordingly
- Recognize buying signals and advance deals through the pipeline appropriately

**Business Outcome Objectives (what results training should drive):**
- Increase partner-sourced pipeline by X% within 6 months
- Reduce time-to-first-deal for newly onboarded partners from X weeks to Y weeks
- Improve channel win rate by X percentage points within two quarters
- Increase average deal size for channel-sourced opportunities by X%
- Achieve X% partner certification completion within 90 days of onboarding

### Audience Segmentation Models

Not all channel reps need the same training. Segment your audience along these dimensions:

**By Channel Type:**
- **Direct sales reps** — Full access to internal resources, typically the most deeply trained
- **Resellers/VARs** — Sell multiple vendors' products; need concise, differentiated selling points
- **Distributors** — May train downstream resellers; need train-the-trainer capability
- **Strategic/SI partners** — Sell solutions, not products; need training on how your product fits within larger engagements
- **MSPs** — Ongoing service relationships; need training on renewal conversations and expansion selling
- **OEM partners** — Embed your product; need training on the embedded value proposition
- **Referral partners** — Lightest touch; need enough knowledge to identify and qualify opportunities

**By Experience Level:**
- **New partner onboarding** — Comprehensive foundation: market problems, personas, buying process, positioning, competitive landscape, and tools
- **Experienced partner updates** — Incremental: new products, messaging changes, competitive shifts, and fresh win/loss insights
- **Advanced/certification track** — Deep: complex selling scenarios, multi-persona deals, enterprise selling motion, and strategic account planning

**By Role:**
- **Quota-carrying sales reps** — Focus on selling conversations, objection handling, and deal advancement
- **Pre-sales/technical consultants** — Focus on connecting technical capabilities to business problems during evaluation stages
- **Partner sales managers** — Focus on pipeline management, coaching their reps, and identifying training needs
- **Marketing contacts at partners** — Focus on positioning, messaging, and demand generation alignment

## Curriculum Design

### The Pragmatic Channel Training Curriculum Framework

Organize curriculum into modules that mirror the selling motion, not the product architecture. Each module should follow this structure:

**Module Structure Template:**

```
Module Title: [Linked to a selling activity, not a product area]
Target Audience: [Segment from above]
Duration: [Time estimate]
Prerequisites: [Prior modules required]

Learning Objectives:
- [Behavioral objective 1]
- [Behavioral objective 2]

Content Outline:
1. Market Problem Context
   - What problem does this address?
   - Which persona experiences it?
   - Why is it urgent/pervasive?

2. Positioning and Messaging
   - How we describe our solution to this problem
   - Key phrases and language to use
   - Language to avoid (internal jargon, feature-speak)

3. Buying Process Mapping
   - Where in the buying journey does this come up?
   - What does the buyer need at this stage?
   - What sales tool supports this conversation?

4. Competitive Context
   - How do alternatives address this problem?
   - Our differentiated approach
   - Objections and responses

5. Practice Exercise
   - Role-play scenario
   - Discovery question practice
   - Objection handling drill

6. Quick-Reference Job Aid
   - One-page summary for field use
```

### Recommended Curriculum Modules

**Foundation Modules (required for all channel reps):**

1. **Our Market and Its Problems** — The problems we solve, for whom, and why they matter. NOT product features or company history.
2. **Know Your Buyers** — Buyer persona profiles, what each cares about, their role in the decision, and the language they use.
3. **The Buyer's Journey** — How buyers evaluate, compare, and select solutions. The steps, the barriers, and how to support each stage.
4. **Our Positioning** — What we say, to whom, and why. The positioning document translated into selling conversations.
5. **Competitive Landscape** — Alternatives, differentiation, and competitive selling strategies. When to fight, when to redirect.
6. **Using Your Sales Tools** — Which tool to use at which stage of the selling process, and how to deploy them effectively.

**Advanced Modules (for experienced reps and certification tracks):**

7. **Multi-Persona Selling** — Navigating deals with multiple buyer personas, each with different problems and priorities.
8. **Enterprise Selling Motion** — Longer sales cycles, procurement involvement, and complex evaluation processes.
9. **Expansion and Cross-Sell** — Identifying opportunities within existing accounts using problem-based discovery.
10. **New Product Launch Selling** — Updated positioning, new personas addressed, new competitive dynamics.

### Content Creation Guidelines

**Do:**
- Use real (anonymized) customer stories from win/loss analysis
- Include actual buyer quotes that illustrate problems and language
- Create scenario-based exercises drawn from recent deals
- Build quick-reference cards reps can use during live selling conversations
- Record short video examples of effective selling conversations

**Don't:**
- Lead with product architecture diagrams or technical specifications
- Create modules organized by product feature areas
- Use internal jargon or engineering terminology in selling scenarios
- Rely on slides-only formats without interactive practice components
- Build training content without input from field-facing teams

## Delivery and Reinforcement

### Delivery Method Selection Matrix

| Method | Best For | Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live instructor-led (in-person) | New partner onboarding, key partner events, annual kickoffs | High engagement, relationship building, real-time practice | Expensive, limited scale, scheduling challenges |
| Live instructor-led (virtual) | Distributed channels, quarterly updates, competitive refreshers | Good scale, interactive, recordable | Zoom fatigue, harder to practice role-play |
| Self-paced e-learning | Foundation modules, product updates, certification prerequisites | Maximum scale, on-demand access, consistent delivery | Low completion without incentives, no live practice |
| Microlearning (2-5 min) | Reinforcement, competitive updates, new messaging rollouts | Easy to consume, mobile-friendly, high completion | Cannot teach complex selling skills alone |
| Role-play/simulation | Objection handling, discovery skills, competitive scenarios | Builds muscle memory, reveals skill gaps | Requires facilitation, doesn't scale easily |
| Field ride-alongs | Top partner development, coaching, real-world validation | Most impactful learning, builds relationships | Very limited scale, time-intensive |
| Certification programs | Structured progression, partner tiering, quality assurance | Motivates learning, validates competency | Requires maintenance, can feel bureaucratic |

### Launch and Rollout Checklist

- [ ] Pilot program tested with 3-5 trusted partners and feedback incorporated
- [ ] Training content reviewed by product marketing for positioning accuracy
- [ ] Sales tools referenced in training are published and accessible
- [ ] Training platform/LMS configured with all modules and assessments
- [ ] Partner communications sent announcing the program with clear value proposition
- [ ] Incentives defined and communicated (certification benefits, SPIFF eligibility, tier advancement)
- [ ] Channel managers briefed on the program so they can coach and reinforce
- [ ] Feedback mechanism in place (post-training surveys, ongoing feedback channels)
- [ ] Calendar established for quarterly updates and refresher sessions
- [ ] Reporting dashboards configured to track completion and correlation with sales outcomes

### Reinforcement Cadence

Training decays without reinforcement. Establish a rhythm:

**Weekly:** Share a competitive insight, customer story, or selling tip via email or messaging channel. Keep it under 2 minutes to consume.

**Monthly:** Conduct a 30-minute virtual session covering recent wins (what worked), losses (what to learn), and competitive changes. Include one practice scenario.

**Quarterly:** Deliver a more comprehensive update covering new products/features translated into selling language, updated competitive positioning, and fresh buyer persona insights from market interviews.

**At Each Launch:** Full Channel Training update aligned with launch messaging, including updated positioning, new selling scenarios, refreshed competitive analysis, and new or updated sales tools.

**Annually:** Comprehensive Channel Training refresh incorporating full year of win/loss learnings, updated buyer persona research, and evolved competitive landscape. Often delivered at partner kickoff events.

## Measurement Framework

### Channel Training Metrics Dashboard

**Tier 1 — Training Activity Metrics:**
These tell you whether the program is being consumed, but not whether it's working.
- Training module completion rates (by segment, by module)
- Certification achievement rates and time-to-certification
- Live session attendance and engagement scores
- Content access and download rates

**Tier 2 — Behavior Change Metrics:**
These tell you whether training is changing how reps sell.
- Discovery question quality scores (from call reviews or ride-alongs)
- Positioning language adoption (are reps using the trained messaging?)
- Sales tool utilization rates (are reps using the right tool at the right stage?)
- Objection handling consistency (are reps using trained responses?)
- Rep confidence self-assessment scores (pre- and post-training)

**Tier 3 — Business Outcome Metrics:**
These tell you whether training is driving revenue results.
- Win rate (overall and by channel segment) — pre-training vs. post-training
- Time-to-first-deal for newly onboarded partners
- Average deal size for channel-sourced opportunities
- Sales cycle length for channel-sourced deals
- Pipeline coverage ratio by channel
- Competitive win rate (percentage of competitive deals won)
- Partner-sourced revenue and revenue growth rate
- Net new logos sourced by channel

**Correlation Analysis:**
The most valuable measurement connects training activity to business outcomes:
- Do certified partners have higher win rates than non-certified?
- Do partners who complete advanced modules close larger deals?
- Is time-to-first-deal shorter for partners who completed onboarding training within 30 days?
- Does training completion correlate with partner retention and loyalty?

## Channel Type Considerations

### Adapting Channel Training by Partner Type

**Direct Sales (Internal Channel):**
- Deepest training investment — full curriculum access
- Tightest alignment with product marketing messaging
- Regular ride-alongs and coaching from product marketing
- First to receive launch training; often pilot group for new content

**Resellers and VARs:**
- Sell multiple vendors; your training competes for their attention
- Make training concise, high-value, and easy to consume
- Emphasize what makes your product DIFFERENT (competitive positioning)
- Provide co-branded materials they can use immediately
- Incentivize completion with partner tier benefits

**Distributors:**
- May train downstream resellers on your behalf
- Invest in train-the-trainer programs with approved curriculum
- Provide modular, reusable training assets they can customize
- Ensure messaging consistency through certification requirements

**Systems Integrators / Strategic Partners:**
- Sell solutions, not individual products
- Train on how your product fits within broader solutions
- Provide use-case-based training rather than product-centric training
- Develop joint selling scenarios and reference architectures

**MSPs / Managed Service Providers:**
- Ongoing service relationship with customers
- Train on expansion and renewal conversations
- Emphasize problem-based discovery for upsell and cross-sell
- Provide customer health indicators and expansion triggers

## Training Content Library Structure

### Recommended Asset Organization

Organize training content by selling activity, not by product feature:

```
Channel Training Library/
├── Foundation/
│   ├── Market Problems Overview
│   ├── Buyer Persona Profiles
│   ├── Buying Process Map
│   ├── Positioning Essentials
│   ├── Competitive Landscape Overview
│   └── Sales Tools Orientation
├── Selling Skills/
│   ├── Discovery Question Framework
│   ├── Objection Handling Playbook
│   ├── Competitive Battle Cards (selling-focused)
│   ├── Value Articulation Practice
│   └── Multi-Persona Navigation Guide
├── Product Launches/
│   ├── [Product/Release] Selling Guide
│   ├── [Product/Release] New Positioning
│   ├── [Product/Release] Competitive Updates
│   └── [Product/Release] Updated Sales Tools
├── Reinforcement/
│   ├── Weekly Selling Tips Archive
│   ├── Win Story Library
│   ├── Loss Lesson Library
│   └── Competitive Update Log
├── Certification/
│   ├── Level 1 — Foundation Assessment
│   ├── Level 2 — Advanced Selling Assessment
│   └── Level 3 — Expert Certification
└── Quick Reference/
    ├── Persona Cheat Sheets
    ├── Objection Response Cards
    ├── Competitive Quick Comparisons
    └── Buying Stage / Tool Matrix
```

### Content Ownership

| Content Type | Primary Owner | Contributors |
|---|---|---|
| Market problems and persona content | Product Marketing | Product Management (market research) |
| Positioning and messaging modules | Product Marketing | Marketing Communications |
| Competitive training content | Product Marketing | Sales, Competitive Intelligence |
| Sales tool training | Product Marketing | Sales Enablement/Ops |
| Selling skills (discovery, objections) | Product Marketing + Sales Enablement | Top-performing reps |
| Certification assessments | Product Marketing | Partner Management |
| Training logistics and LMS | Sales Enablement / Partner Ops | Product Marketing (content) |
