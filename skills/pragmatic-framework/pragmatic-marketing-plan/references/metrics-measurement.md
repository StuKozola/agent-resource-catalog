# Metrics and Measurement — Quantifying Marketing Plan Effectiveness

A Pragmatic marketing plan requires clear measurements and goals for every program. This reference covers how to select metrics that matter, avoid vanity metrics, and create a measurement framework that demonstrates marketing's contribution to business outcomes.

## The Metrics Hierarchy

Organize metrics into three tiers that connect activity to business outcomes:

### Tier 1: Business Outcomes (Lagging Indicators)
These are the ultimate measures of success — the metrics executives care about.

- **Revenue from marketing-sourced pipeline** — Revenue from opportunities where marketing generated the initial lead or contact
- **Revenue from marketing-influenced pipeline** — Revenue from opportunities where marketing touched the buyer at any point in the journey
- **Customer acquisition cost (CAC)** — Total marketing and sales cost to acquire a new customer
- **Marketing ROI** — Revenue attributed to marketing divided by marketing investment
- **Pipeline contribution** — Percentage of total pipeline generated by marketing
- **Win rate on marketing-sourced leads** — Conversion rate of marketing-generated opportunities
- **Net revenue retention (NRR)** — Revenue retained and expanded from existing customers

### Tier 2: Pipeline and Conversion Metrics (Leading Indicators)
These predict future business outcomes and help diagnose where the funnel is healthy or broken.

- **Marketing qualified leads (MQLs)** — Leads that meet defined criteria for sales handoff
- **Sales qualified leads (SQLs)** — MQLs accepted by sales as genuine opportunities
- **Opportunities created** — New sales opportunities in the pipeline
- **Pipeline value** — Total dollar value of active opportunities
- **Conversion rates by stage** — Lead → MQL → SQL → Opportunity → Close
- **Sales cycle length** — Average time from first touch to closed deal
- **Average deal size** — Particularly for marketing-sourced vs. other sources

### Tier 3: Activity and Engagement Metrics (Operational Indicators)
These measure program execution and buyer engagement.

- **Website traffic** — Segmented by source, persona, and content type
- **Content engagement** — Downloads, time on page, scroll depth, shares
- **Email performance** — Open rates, click-through rates, unsubscribe rates
- **Event metrics** — Registrations, attendance, engagement scores
- **Social metrics** — Reach, engagement, share of voice
- **Ad performance** — Impressions, clicks, cost per click, cost per lead
- **Brand awareness** — Aided and unaided awareness in target segments

## Setting Goals Using the Funnel Model

Work backwards from revenue targets to set goals at each funnel stage. This creates a quantitative foundation for the marketing plan.

### The Reverse-Funnel Calculation

1. **Start with the revenue target** — e.g., $10M in new business revenue
2. **Divide by average deal size** — e.g., $100K ACV → need 100 new customers
3. **Apply win rate** — e.g., 25% win rate → need 400 opportunities
4. **Apply SQL-to-opportunity rate** — e.g., 50% → need 800 SQLs
5. **Apply MQL-to-SQL rate** — e.g., 40% → need 2,000 MQLs
6. **Apply lead-to-MQL rate** — e.g., 20% → need 10,000 leads
7. **Determine marketing's share** — e.g., marketing generates 50% of pipeline → 5,000 leads needed from marketing

This gives you a quantitative target for each funnel stage that can be allocated across programs.

### Goal-Setting by Program

For each program in the plan, define:

**Leading metrics** (what you expect to see during execution):
- Reach and impressions
- Engagement (clicks, downloads, registrations)
- Lead generation
- MQL generation

**Lagging metrics** (what you expect to see after execution):
- Pipeline contribution
- Opportunities influenced
- Revenue attributed
- Win rate impact

**Operational metrics** (how efficiently the program runs):
- Cost per lead (CPL)
- Cost per MQL
- Cost per opportunity
- Program ROI

## Attribution Models

Marketing attribution — connecting revenue to specific marketing programs — is complex. Choose a model that matches your organization's sophistication and data infrastructure.

### First-Touch Attribution
Credits the first marketing interaction that brought a buyer into the funnel. Simple but undervalues downstream programs.

**Best for:** Understanding which programs are most effective at creating new pipeline.

### Last-Touch Attribution
Credits the last marketing interaction before a buyer became an opportunity. Undervalues awareness and education programs.

**Best for:** Understanding which programs most directly drive conversion.

### Multi-Touch Attribution
Distributes credit across all marketing interactions in the buyer's journey. More accurate but requires sophisticated tracking.

**Models include:**
- **Linear** — Equal credit to all touchpoints
- **Time-decay** — More credit to recent touchpoints
- **U-shaped** — More credit to first and last touch, less to middle
- **W-shaped** — More credit to first touch, lead creation, and opportunity creation

**Best for:** Organizations with mature marketing technology stacks and CRM integration.

### Self-Reported Attribution
Asks buyers "How did you hear about us?" during the conversion process. Captures dark social and word-of-mouth that digital tracking misses.

**Best for:** Supplementing digital attribution; capturing offline and peer-influence channels.

## Building the Measurement Dashboard

The marketing plan should specify a measurement cadence and dashboard structure:

### Weekly Review
- Program execution status (on track, at risk, behind)
- Key activity metrics (leads generated, content published, campaigns launched)
- Anomaly detection (unexpected spikes or drops)

### Monthly Review
- Funnel metrics and conversion rates
- Pipeline contribution and velocity
- Program performance by leading indicators
- Budget tracking (spend vs. plan)

### Quarterly Review
- Revenue attribution and marketing ROI
- Program-level performance assessment (continue, optimize, or sunset)
- Funnel bottleneck analysis
- Plan adjustments based on performance data and market changes

### Annual Review
- Full-year marketing ROI and business impact
- Year-over-year trend analysis
- Input to next fiscal period's marketing plan

## Common Measurement Mistakes

**Measuring activity instead of outcomes** — Counting blog posts published, emails sent, and events hosted tells you about effort, not impact. Always connect activities to pipeline and revenue metrics.

**Vanity metrics** — High website traffic, large social followings, and impressive email list sizes feel good but may not correlate with business outcomes. Always ask: "Does this metric predict revenue?"

**Inconsistent definitions** — If marketing and sales define MQL differently, the entire funnel model breaks down. Agree on lead scoring criteria, handoff processes, and stage definitions before launching programs.

**No baseline** — Goals without baselines are guesses. Establish current-state metrics before setting targets. If baselines don't exist, the first quarter's goal is to establish them.

**Ignoring attribution complexity** — Claiming 100% credit for a deal that marketing touched once in a 12-month sales cycle destroys credibility. Use appropriate attribution models and be transparent about methodology.

**Measuring too late** — Waiting until the end of the fiscal period to measure means you can't course-correct. Build in leading indicators that predict outcomes early enough to adjust programs.
