# Field Tools — Command of the Message, Step 4

A Value Framework only matters if reps can use it with a buyer. This step converts the framework into short, practical tools that make reps audible-ready: able to ask the right questions, recognize what the buyer cares about, and respond with the relevant part of the message.

**Prerequisite:** a Value Framework (`cotm-value-framework`). If the user has none, build at least one persona section first or work from their positioning and clearly label the tools as provisional.

**Output:** for each persona, a discovery question set, trap-setting questions, the mantra, a talk track, objection responses and a call plan template.

## The tools

### 1. Discovery questions

Map each question to a framework element so every answer moves the rep toward a relevant message. Write open questions in the buyer's language, in roughly this order:

- **Before scenario:** "How are you handling [process] today?"
- **Negative consequences:** "What happens when [issue] occurs? What has it cost you?"
- **Positive business outcomes:** "If this were solved, what would be different six months from now?"
- **Metrics:** "How would you measure that? What does it look like today?"
- **Required capabilities:** "What would a solution need to do for that to happen?"
- **Decision context:** who else is affected, how decisions get made, what has been tried before.

Aim for 3–5 questions per element per persona, plus follow-ups that deepen ("Tell me more…", "What's the impact of that on…?").

### 2. Trap-setting questions

These lead the buyer to recognize the importance of a capability you deliver uniquely or better, so it becomes part of their decision criteria. Build them from capabilities marked *unique* or *better* in the framework.

**Pattern:** ask about the risk or cost the capability addresses, without naming your product or any competitor.

- Capability: automatic risk capture from activity data.
- Trap-setting question: "How confident are you in a forecast that depends on reps keeping the CRM up to date? What happens when they don't?"

Rules: never disparage competitors; never ask questions whose only purpose is a gotcha; ensure the topic genuinely matters to the buyer's outcome.

### 3. The mantra

A short, memorable statement (two or three sentences, about 30 seconds spoken) that answers the three essential questions for a persona. Formula:

> We help [persona] who struggle with [before scenario] to [positive business outcome], measured by [metric]. Unlike [alternative approach], we [differentiator]. [Proof point in one clause].

Write a company-level mantra plus persona variants. Test it aloud; cut anything a rep wouldn't naturally say.

### 4. Persona talk tracks

A one-page conversation guide per persona: the top two or three before scenarios they are likely to have, matching PBOs and metrics, the most relevant differentiator, a proof point, and suggested transitions ("Other CROs we work with have found…"). Talk tracks are a menu, not a script.

### 5. Objection responses

For common objections (price, timing, status quo, "we can build it", competitor X), write responses that go back to the framework: restate the consequence of not acting, the outcome and metric, and proof. Use `templates/objection-response.md`.

### 6. Call plans

A short pre-call plan: what we know about the before state, hypotheses about outcomes and metrics, discovery questions to ask, trap-setting questions to use, the proof point to have ready, and the desired next step. Use `templates/call-plan.md`.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the persona(s) and the Value Framework source.
2. Draft discovery and trap-setting questions per persona with `templates/discovery-question-bank.md`.
3. Draft the mantra and persona variants; test them for speakability.
4. Build talk tracks and objection responses.
5. Validate with two or three top reps: would they use these words? Revise.
6. Package as a one-page field guide per persona. Offer to produce assets (deck, pocket card) via `pragmatic-assets` if wanted.

## Quality checks

- Every question traces to a framework element.
- Trap-setting questions are buyer-relevant and competitor-neutral.
- The mantra is under about 75 words and contains a problem, an outcome, a differentiator and proof.
- Nothing reads as a script; reps can pick and choose.

## Using AI in this step

- Generate first-draft question banks and mantra variants from the framework.
- Role-play as a buyer persona so reps can rehearse.
- Review call transcripts to see which questions are actually being asked.

Validate everything with real reps and buyers; AI-generated questions often sound polished but unnatural in a live call.
