# Marketing Plan — Pragmatic Framework (2027)

## Where this fits in the 2027 Pragmatic Framework

- **Category:** Go-to-Market — *How will the market find, choose and stay with the product?*
- **Focus-area question:** *How will we reach the market this year?*
- **Position:** Above the bar (market-focused)
- **Other focus areas in this category:** Growth, Retention, Launch, Organizational Alignment, Awareness, Engagement, Customer Satisfaction
- **Pragmatic Institute course:** Market

## What This Skill Does

A marketing plan describes how you'll introduce the product to potential customers within a set period. It covers the tactics you'll use (content, social media, PR, events, sales, advertising) and is usually built for a year, then revisited regularly as results come in. The 2027 guide expects eight elements: **executive summary, market analysis, goals, target audience, differentiation, strategies, budget and success measures.**

The output connects upstream market knowledge (Positioning, Buyer Personas, Buyer Journey, Competitive Landscape) to the Go-to-Market programs that execute it (Growth, Retention, Launch, Awareness, Engagement, Customer Satisfaction). It replaces inside-out guesswork with an outside-in plan.

## Context Within the Pragmatic Framework

The Marketing Plan is the umbrella for the Go-to-Market category. Each sibling focus area covers one execution area; the plan ties them into one time-bound strategy with shared goals and measures.

### Key Inputs

- **Positioning** (Planning) — Every program traces back to positioning that describes the product by the market problems it solves. If positioning is missing, create a lightweight statement first (`pragmatic-positioning`).
- **Buyer Personas** (Planning) — Programs target defined buyers, sponsors, approvers and influencers (`pragmatic-buyer-personas`).
- **Buyer Journey** (Planning) — Tells you *where* in the path from need to decision to deploy each program (`pragmatic-buyer-journey`).
- **Competitive Landscape** (Market) — Shapes differentiation, including against substitutes and doing nothing (`pragmatic-competitive-landscape`).
- **Market Definition** (Focus) — Determines which segments get investment (`pragmatic-market-definition`).
- **Business Plan** (Business) — Constrains budget and sets the revenue or adoption expectations the plan must support (`pragmatic-business-plan`).
- **Product Roadmap** (Focus) — Release timing drives launch and campaign sequencing (`pragmatic-product-roadmap`).

### What the Marketing Plan Feeds

- **Growth** (Go-to-Market) — New revenue and new-user programs (`pragmatic-growth`)
- **Retention** (Go-to-Market) — Renewal, expansion and continued-use programs (`pragmatic-retention`)
- **Launch** (Go-to-Market) — Strategic framing for launches (`pragmatic-launch`)
- **Awareness** and **Engagement** (Go-to-Market) — Top-of-funnel and interest-to-trial programs (`pragmatic-awareness`, `pragmatic-engagement`)
- **Customer Satisfaction** (Go-to-Market) — Customer marketing and advocate programs (`pragmatic-customer-satisfaction`)
- **Organizational Alignment** (Go-to-Market) — Sales, success and support share the plan's picture of audience and value (`pragmatic-organizational-alignment`)
- **Measurement** (Business) — The plan's goals become tracked indicators (`pragmatic-measurement`)
- **Content** (Enablement) — Content needs mapped to personas and journey stages (`pragmatic-content`)

## Workflow

A Pragmatic marketing plan is market-driven, not activity-driven. The question is never "what programs should we run?" — it's "what do our buyers need to hear, see and experience to recognize their problem, evaluate solutions and choose us?"

### Step 1: Audit Available Inputs

Read `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-marketing-plan/references/input-audit.md` for the checklist and how to work with incomplete inputs.

Ask the user:
- Do you have positioning by segment/persona?
- Do you have documented buyer personas?
- Do you have a buyer journey map?
- Do you have competitive landscape analysis?
- What's the plan period (usually annual) and review cadence?
- What's your approximate marketing budget or resource level?

If critical inputs are missing (especially positioning and buyer personas), help the user create lightweight versions before proceeding. A marketing plan built without positioning is just a list of tactics.

### Step 2: Market Analysis and Goals

Read `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-marketing-plan/references/strategic-foundation.md`.

Work with the user to articulate:
1. **Market analysis** — Trends, competitive moves, buyer-behavior shifts, each tied to evidence.
2. **Goals** — Revenue, growth or adoption targets the plan must support, split between Growth (new) and Retention (existing).
3. **Target audience** — Priority segments and buyer personas.
4. **Differentiation** — Core positioning pillars: why us rather than the alternatives, including doing nothing.
5. **Key challenges** — Competitive, internal or market obstacles the plan must address.

### Step 3: Map Strategies and Programs to the Buyer Journey

Read `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-marketing-plan/references/program-mapping.md` for the method. For each target persona, plan programs by stage:

1. **Awareness** — How buyers recognize the problem and learn that solutions (and you) exist: thought leadership, content, PR, analyst relations, events, SEO/SEM, social.
2. **Education and evaluation** — How buyers learn enough to evaluate and try: webinars, whitepapers, case studies, comparison guides, demos, trials, engagement sequences.
3. **Selection** — How buyers decide: ROI calculators, proof-of-concept support, references, competitive displacement.
4. **Loyalty and expansion** — How customers stay and grow: onboarding support, user groups, upsell/cross-sell, renewal marketing, advocate programs.

### Step 4: Build the Program Portfolio

For each program, define: name and description; target persona(s) and segment(s); journey stage; positioning message it reinforces; channels and tactics; timeline and milestones; budget; success measures (leading and lagging); owner and dependencies.

Read `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-marketing-plan/references/metrics-measurement.md` for choosing metrics. Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-marketing-plan/templates/go-to-market-program-planning.md` to plan programs focus area by focus area (Growth, Retention, Launch, Organizational Alignment, Awareness, Engagement, Customer Satisfaction) with a shared success-measures section.

### Step 5: Assemble the Marketing Plan Document

Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-marketing-plan/templates/marketing-plan-document.md` for the full plan. Its first eight sections follow the guide's elements in order. Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-marketing-plan/templates/program-brief.md` for individual program briefs.

### Step 6: Review, Align and Revisit

The plan is a cross-functional alignment tool, not a marketing-department document. Review with:
- **Product management** — Positioning, roadmap alignment, launch timing
- **Sales leadership** — Programs support how buyers buy and how sales sells
- **Executive leadership** — Budget, goals, strategic fit
- **Customer success and support** — Retention and expansion programs reflect customer reality

Set a review cadence (at least quarterly) and the criteria for shifting budget between programs.

## Internal Products

For internal platforms and tools, "the market" is the internal audience. Goals become adoption across teams rather than revenue, budget is effort and enablement time, and channels are internal (team meetings, intranet, champions, training). The eight plan elements still apply.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Activity-driven instead of market-driven** — Tactics without a link to positioning, personas or the buyer journey. Every program should answer: "Which persona, at what stage, with what message?"
2. **No differentiation** — If the plan can't say why a buyer should choose you over the alternatives, programs will produce scattered messaging.
3. **Inside-out thinking** — The plan reflects what the company wants to say rather than what buyers need to hear.
4. **Missing measures** — Every program needs leading indicators (reach, engagement, pipeline contribution) and lagging indicators (revenue, win rate, retention).
5. **Ignoring existing customers** — The Framework separates Growth (new) and Retention (existing) so neither gets dropped.
6. **Disconnected from sales and success** — Programs that don't match how sales sells and how customers are served create friction. Coordinate through Organizational Alignment.
7. **Static plan** — Written once and shelved. Build in a regular review and clear criteria for adjusting programs.
8. **Budget without strategy** — Allocating by channel ("40% digital") instead of by persona, segment and journey stage leads to sprawl.

## Using AI in this focus area

- Draft market-analysis summaries from research you've gathered, and turn a finished plan into an executive summary or calendar view.
- Generate program ideas per persona and journey stage, then filter them against positioning and budget.
- Model budget scenarios and funnel math (pipeline needed, conversion rates) quickly.
- Rules: check AI output against real market evidence — talk to the market, NIHITO still applies; keep sensitive customer, deal and company data (budgets, targets) out of tools not approved for it; the team, not the tool, owns the plan. AI tends to produce generic, activity-driven plans, so insist on persona, stage and message for every program.
