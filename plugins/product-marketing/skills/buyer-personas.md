# Buyer Personas — Pragmatic Marketing Framework

## What this skill does

This skill guides the creation of **Buyer Persona** documents as defined by the Pragmatic Framework. In the Framework, the Buyer Personas box sits in the **Planning** category and is defined as:

> **Define the archetypical buyers involved in the purchasing of your products and services.**

Buyer personas are distinct from user personas. A *user persona* profiles someone who uses the product day-to-day. A *buyer persona* profiles someone who influences, evaluates, or decides on the *purchase* — they may never touch the product themselves.

The skill produces persona documents that the rest of the organization can internalize and act on — from Positioning to Sales Alignment to Content creation.

## When to use this skill

- User asks to create or draft buyer personas
- User wants to document buying committee roles
- User references Pragmatic Framework / Pragmatic Marketing personas
- User needs archetypical buyer profiles for product marketing
- User asks for economic buyer, technical buyer, or functional buyer profiles
- User wants to align sales and marketing around buyer understanding
- User is working on Positioning, Buyer Experience, or Message Matrix activities that require persona inputs

## How to use this skill

### Step 1 — Gather context from the user

Before writing anything, collect the inputs you need. Ask the user for the following (adapt based on what they've already provided):

1. **Product / solution description** — What does the product do? What market problems does it solve?
2. **Market segment(s)** — Which segments are these personas for? (Personas often vary by segment.)
3. **B2B or B2C context** — Enterprise sales with buying committees, or consumer purchase?
4. **Known buyer roles** — Does the user already know who is involved in the purchase decision? (e.g., VP of IT, Procurement, end-user manager)
5. **Available research** — Has the user done win/loss interviews, market interviews, or customer surveys? Do they have data to draw from, or do they need a template to fill in later?
6. **Output format** — Do they want a Word document (.docx), a markdown artifact, a presentation, or just structured text in chat?

If the user provides a lot of context upfront, skip questions they've already answered. If they provide minimal context, walk them through it conversationally — don't dump all six questions at once.

### Step 2 — Identify the buyer types

For B2B products, a typical purchase involves multiple buying influences. Use the Pragmatic Framework's three-category model as the starting point:

| Buyer Type | Focus | What They Care About |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic Buyer** | Budget and ROI | Total cost of ownership, payback period, risk, strategic alignment |
| **Technical Buyer** | Feasibility and integration | Interoperability, security, implementation effort, admin burden |
| **User Buyer (Functional)** | Day-to-day use | Features, workflow fit, ease of use, how it solves their daily problems |

Depending on the product and market, there may also be:
- **Champion / Lead Evaluator** — Drives the evaluation process internally
- **Procurement / Gatekeeper** — Enforces compliance, legal, and vendor policies
- **Executive Sponsor** — Signs off on strategic investments

For B2C products, buyer and user may overlap more, but the distinction still matters (e.g., parents buying for children, IT managers buying for teams).

The user may not need all of these. Help them identify which 2–5 buyer roles are most relevant to their specific sale. One vendor in Pragmatic's experience identified 4 buyer personas for a generic segment but ultimately found 26 once market segments were accounted for — so segment matters. Start with the primary segment and expand from there.

### Step 3 — Build each persona document

For each identified buyer type, create a persona profile. Read `references/persona-template.md` for the full template structure and field-by-field guidance.

The persona should feel like a real, specific person — not a demographic summary. The Pragmatic Framework emphasizes that you know you have a valid persona when you can imagine working alongside them or encountering them at a user conference.

**Key principles from the Pragmatic Framework:**

- **Archetypes, not stereotypes.** A persona represents the ideal pattern of a buyer segment, not a caricature. Avoid manufactured bias through stock photos or overly narrow demographics.
- **Research-based, not fictional.** The best personas come from win/loss interviews, market interviews, and customer surveys — not from a conference room brainstorm. If the user doesn't have research yet, the persona document becomes a hypothesis to validate.
- **Buying role, not just job title.** The same job title can play different buying roles depending on the deal. Focus on the role in the purchase decision.
- **Keep it alive.** Personas must be refreshed regularly. They are living documents, not one-time deliverables. Companies exceeding revenue goals are far more likely to have refreshed personas in the last six months.
- **Limit the count.** 3–5 buyer personas typically cover 90%+ of sales. More leads to confusion and scattered effort. Zero is also a problem.

### Step 4 — Connect personas to downstream activities

The Pragmatic Framework treats Buyer Personas as an input to several other activities. After creating the personas, remind the user how they connect:

- **Positioning** — "You need a positioning document for each buyer persona" (Pragmatic Rule #16). Each persona hears a different message.
- **Buyer Experience** — Map the buying journey for each persona. The economic buyer has a different journey than the technical evaluator.
- **Message Matrix** — Craft messages that speak to each persona's role, using their vernacular and addressing their specific concerns.
- **Sales Alignment** — Help sales understand which persona they're talking to and what that persona needs to hear at each stage.
- **Content** — Create content assets (white papers, ROI calculators, technical specs) that serve specific personas at specific journey stages.
- **Sales Tools** — Build tools that help reps engage each persona type effectively.

### Step 5 — Deliver the output

Based on the user's preferred format:

- **Word document (.docx)** — Use the `docx` skill to create a polished, professional document. Include a cover page, table of contents, and one section per persona.
- **Markdown / artifact** — Create a clean, structured markdown document.
- **Presentation (.pptx)** — Use the `pptx` skill. One slide per persona with key details, plus a buying committee overview slide.
- **Chat output** — Present each persona in a structured format directly in conversation.

If the user doesn't specify, default to a Word document — these are meant to be shared across the organization.

## Responsibilities and skills (from the Pragmatic Framework)

The Buyer Personas box in the Framework defines these responsibilities:
- Define the archetypical buyers involved in the purchase of company products and services
- Create and share persona documents for each buyer type

Required skills:
- Understanding of win/loss and market interviews
- Data synthesis and trendspotting from interviews with all components of market
- Ability to make a persona "come alive" to all parts of the organization and to keep it fresh and relevant
