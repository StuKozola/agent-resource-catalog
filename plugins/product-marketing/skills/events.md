# Events — Pragmatic Framework Skill

## What This Skill Does

This skill helps you provide **market-driven event support** as defined by the Pragmatic Institute Framework. The Events activity lives in the **Support** category and is defined as:

> Provide needed market and solution information to support marketing events such as conferences, trade shows and webinars.

The key insight is that Events is a *support* function — the product team brings market expertise, positioning, competitive intelligence, and solution knowledge to events planned and executed by marketing, demand generation, or event teams. The goal is to ensure every event touchpoint communicates market-driven messages rather than inside-out feature pitches.

## Framework Context

Events connects to several upstream Pragmatic activities:

- **Positioning** → Provides the messaging foundation for all event content
- **Buyer Personas** → Determines which personas attend and what resonates with them
- **Buyer Experience** → Maps event touchpoints to stages in the buying journey
- **Competitive Landscape** → Informs competitive positioning at events where competitors are also present
- **Market Problems** → Ensures event content addresses validated market problems
- **Awareness** → Events are a primary awareness vehicle; this activity supports that execution
- **Marketing Plan** → Events appear as programs in the marketing plan; this activity supports their delivery
- **Launch** → Events often serve as launch vehicles; product expertise supports launch messaging
- **Advocacy** → Events leverage customer advocates for testimonials, panels, and case studies

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:

1. Preparing product/market content for an upcoming conference, trade show, webinar, or seminar
2. Creating event briefing documents or speaker preparation materials
3. Developing demo scripts, booth messaging, or presentation abstracts
4. Aligning event participation with positioning and buyer persona strategy
5. Conducting pre-event strategy sessions or post-event debriefs
6. Evaluating event portfolio and ROI from a market-driven perspective
7. Training booth staff or presenters on market problems and competitive positioning

## Workflow

Follow this six-step workflow to provide comprehensive event support:

### Step 1: Assess Event Context and Objectives

Before creating any content, understand the event and its strategic fit.

**Gather from the user:**
- Event type (conference, trade show, webinar, seminar, executive roundtable, virtual event, user conference)
- Event role (sponsor, exhibitor, speaker, panelist, attendee, host)
- Target personas expected to attend
- Primary objectives (awareness, pipeline generation, thought leadership, customer engagement, launch support, competitive positioning)
- Upstream inputs available (positioning document, buyer personas, competitive landscape, market problems)

> **Load reference file** `references/event-types.md` for detailed guidance on event formats, roles, and strategic considerations for each type.

### Step 2: Align Event Content with Positioning and Personas

Map the event strategy to existing positioning and persona work.

**Key activities:**
- Identify which buyer personas will be present and their stage in the buying journey
- Select positioning messages that align with the event audience
- Determine which market problems to emphasize based on audience composition
- Assess competitive presence and prepare differentiation talking points
- Define the "one thing" the audience should remember from your participation

> **Load reference file** `references/content-alignment.md` for the persona-message mapping methodology, competitive preparation framework, and messaging hierarchy for events.

### Step 3: Develop Event Support Materials

Create the specific deliverables needed for the event.

**Common deliverables include:**
- Event briefing document (strategy, messaging, logistics, roles)
- Speaker/presenter preparation materials
- Demo scripts tied to market problems
- Booth messaging and conversation guides
- Presentation abstracts and session descriptions
- Pre-event promotional content (emails, social, blog)
- Handout or leave-behind content aligned with positioning

> **Load reference file** `references/deliverables-guide.md` for detailed guidance on each deliverable type, including structure, best practices, and common mistakes.

### Step 4: Prepare Event Participants

Ensure everyone representing the company at the event speaks with one voice grounded in market knowledge.

**Key activities:**
- Brief booth staff on positioning, personas, and competitive talking points
- Prepare speakers with market data, customer stories, and problem framing
- Create quick-reference cards for common prospect questions
- Rehearse demos against market problems, not feature lists
- Align on lead qualification criteria and handoff processes
- Share counterintelligence guidelines (what not to disclose)

### Step 5: Support Event Execution

During the event, provide real-time market expertise.

**Key activities:**
- Serve as subject matter expert for impromptu prospect conversations
- Capture market intelligence from attendee interactions (NIHITO — Nothing Important Happens In The Office)
- Note competitive intelligence gathered at the event
- Support speakers and booth staff with on-the-spot positioning guidance
- Document customer stories and advocacy opportunities

### Step 6: Conduct Post-Event Debrief and Measurement

After the event, capture learnings and measure impact.

**Key activities:**
- Facilitate structured debrief with all event participants
- Capture market intelligence and feed it back to product team
- Assess messaging effectiveness — what resonated, what fell flat
- Review lead quality against target personas
- Measure event ROI using the metrics framework
- Document recommendations for future events

> **Load reference file** `references/measurement-framework.md` for event ROI metrics, debrief methodology, and intelligence capture templates.

## Output Formats

Depending on what the user needs, produce one or more of:

1. **Event Briefing Document** — Comprehensive strategy and preparation guide for event participants → Use `templates/event-briefing.md`
2. **Speaker Preparation Kit** — Positioning-aligned materials for presenters → Use `templates/speaker-prep.md`
3. **Event Debrief Report** — Post-event analysis and recommendations
4. **Event Portfolio Assessment** — Strategic evaluation of event participation across the fiscal period
5. **Individual deliverables** — Demo scripts, booth messaging, conversation guides, etc.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Feature-dump presentations** — Leading with product features instead of market problems the audience cares about
2. **Generic booth messaging** — Using the same messages regardless of which personas attend a specific event
3. **No competitive preparation** — Ignoring that competitors are exhibiting at the same event
4. **Disconnected from positioning** — Event content that contradicts or ignores the positioning document
5. **No debrief discipline** — Failing to capture market intelligence and messaging learnings after events
6. **Treating events as one-way broadcast** — Missing the NIHITO opportunity to listen and learn from the market
7. **No lead qualification alignment** — Booth staff collecting badge scans without qualifying against buyer personas
8. **Counterintelligence failures** — Accidentally disclosing roadmap, pricing, or strategy details at public events
