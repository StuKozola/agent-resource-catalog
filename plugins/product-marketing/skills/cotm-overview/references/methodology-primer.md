## 1. Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Value Framework** | The single shared document that captures the buyer's problems, the outcomes they want, what is required to get there, how success is measured, how you are different, and your proof. Everything else is built from it. |
| **Before scenario** | The buyer's current state: the business issues they face and how things work today. |
| **Negative consequences** | What the current state costs the buyer, and what happens if nothing changes. These create urgency. |
| **Positive business outcomes (PBOs)** | The tangible business results the buyer gets once the problem is solved — the "after" scenario. |
| **Required capabilities** | What any solution must be able to do to deliver the outcomes. Written in neutral terms first, so the buyer can agree with them before you claim them. |
| **Metrics** | How the buyer will measure success — the numbers that prove an outcome was reached. |
| **Differentiators** | How your approach is unique, better and defensible, stated in terms that matter to the buyer. |
| **Proof points** | Evidence you've done it before: customer stories, quantified results, references, third-party validation. |
| **Discovery questions** | Questions mapped to the framework that uncover the buyer's before state, consequences, desired outcomes and metrics. |
| **Trap-setting questions** | Questions that lead the buyer to value a capability only you provide, raising doubts about alternatives without naming or attacking them. |
| **Mantra** | A short, memorable value statement every customer-facing person can deliver consistently. |
| **Audible-ready** | Able to adapt the message on the spot to the buyer's situation while staying within the framework. |

## 2. The three essential questions

Every customer-facing person should be able to answer these for each buyer persona and use case:

1. **What problems do we solve?** → before scenarios, negative consequences
2. **How do we solve them differently or better?** → positive business outcomes, required capabilities, differentiators
3. **Where have we done it before?** → proof points, metrics

If a rep can't answer all three in the buyer's language, the framework or the training has a gap.

## 3. How the Value Framework flows

```
Before scenario → Negative consequences → Positive business outcomes
       → Required capabilities → Metrics → Differentiators → Proof points
```

The sequence matters. Reps establish the problem and its cost before describing a better future, agree on the capabilities needed before claiming them, and agree on how success will be measured before asking the buyer to believe they'll get there.

## 4. Audible-ready: consistency with flexibility

The framework is not a script. It gives every rep the same raw material, but the order and emphasis in any conversation depend on what the buyer reveals. A finance leader may care most about one outcome and metric; an operations leader about another. Reps pick the parts of the framework that match, so the message stays consistent across the organization even though no two conversations are identical.

## 5. CotM and MEDDPICC

CotM gives reps the message; MEDDPICC gives them a way to qualify and manage the opportunity. They connect directly:

| MEDDPICC element | Connection to the Value Framework |
|---|---|
| Metrics | The framework's metrics, quantified for this buyer |
| Economic buyer | The persona whose outcomes and metrics carry the decision |
| Decision criteria | Shaped by required capabilities and trap-setting questions |
| Decision process | Mapped during discovery |
| Paper process | Mapped during discovery |
| Identify pain | Before scenario and negative consequences |
| Champion | The person who tells your story internally — they need the mantra and proof points |
| Competition | Differentiators and trap-setting questions |

See `cotm-sales-process-integration` for how to embed this in CRM and deal reviews.

## 6. Comparison with other methodologies

| Methodology | Main emphasis | How CotM relates |
|---|---|---|
| Solution Selling | Diagnose pain, then propose a solution | Similar buyer-first starting point; CotM adds one shared organizational framework and explicit differentiation |
| SPIN Selling | Question types (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff) | CotM discovery covers similar ground; SPIN-style questions can be mapped into the framework |
| Challenger | Teaching buyers a new insight and taking control | Compatible; Challenger insights can become before scenarios and trap-setting questions |
| Sandler | Seller behavior and qualification discipline | Different layer; Sandler covers how the call is conducted, CotM the content of the message |
| MEDDICC/MEDDPICC | Qualification and deal management | Complementary; usually deployed together |

## 7. Who uses the message

Not just sales. Marketing builds campaigns and content from it, product management checks roadmap themes against the outcomes it promises, customer success uses its metrics in value reviews, services scopes work against its outcomes, and executives use it with boards and investors. One framework, many audiences.
