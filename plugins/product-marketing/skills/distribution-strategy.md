# Distribution Strategy — Pragmatic Marketing Framework

## Context Within the Pragmatic Framework

Distribution Strategy is one of the 37 activities in the Pragmatic Framework (formerly known as the Pragmatic Marketing Framework), developed by Pragmatic Institute. It sits in the **Focus** category — the stage where teams filter opportunities and concentrate on the highest-potential market segments.

The official Pragmatic Institute definition:

> **Distribution Strategy**: Determine which channels best align with your markets' buying preferences.

This activity connects directly to several other Framework boxes:
- **Market Definition** (Focus) — You must know your target segments before choosing channels
- **Win/Loss Analysis** (Market) — Reveals how buyers actually purchase and why they chose competitors
- **Buyer Personas** (Planning) — Defines the archetypical buyers whose preferences drive channel selection
- **Buyer Experience** (Planning) — Documents the buying process your personas use to select products
- **Pricing** (Business) — Channel economics and margin structures affect pricing viability
- **Product Portfolio** (Focus) — Different products in a portfolio may need different channels

The fundamental principle is **outside-in thinking**: start with how your market prefers to buy, then evaluate your company's ability to serve those preferences — not the other way around.

## Key Responsibilities (from Pragmatic Institute)

The person or team owning Distribution Strategy is responsible for:

1. **Determining which channels best align with the market's buying preferences** — research how target personas discover, evaluate, and purchase solutions
2. **Understanding how users want to receive and use the product** — delivery and consumption preferences shape channel requirements
3. **Evaluating the value proposition for channel partners** — what incentive do distributors, resellers, or partners have to carry your product, and can you still make a profit?

## Required Skills

Effective distribution strategy work requires:
- Analytical capability (gathering data, synthesizing it, telling a story)
- Strategic thinking
- Financial savviness (margin analysis, channel economics)
- Partner or business development experience
- Specific knowledge of distribution channels relevant to your business

## How to Use This Skill

When a user asks for help with distribution strategy, follow these steps:

### Step 1: Understand the Situation

Before recommending channels, gather context. Ask about:
- **Product type**: Physical goods, software/SaaS, digital content, services, or hybrid?
- **Target market segments**: Who are the buyers? B2B, B2C, or both?
- **Current state**: Are they entering a new market, expanding an existing one, or rethinking a current strategy?
- **Buyer personas**: Who makes the purchasing decision? What roles are involved?
- **Market Definition work**: Have they already defined and prioritized segments?

If the user hasn't done Market Definition or Buyer Persona work yet, flag this — distribution strategy should be informed by those activities. Offer to help them do preliminary segmentation first, or proceed with what they know while noting the dependency.

### Step 2: Research Buyer Purchasing Behavior

Guide the user to investigate how their target market actually buys. The outside-in approach means the market's behavior dictates the strategy, not internal convenience. Key questions to explore:

- Where do buyers in this segment currently discover products like yours?
- What is their evaluation and comparison process?
- Do they prefer to purchase through a direct relationship (sales team, website) or through intermediaries (resellers, marketplaces, distributors)?
- What role does self-service play vs. high-touch sales?
- Are there geographic or regulatory factors that affect channel access?
- What post-sale support and delivery expectations exist?

For guidance on specific research techniques, read `references/research-methods.md`.

### Step 3: Evaluate Channel Options

Help the user assess available channel types against their market's buying preferences. Load `references/channel-types.md` for a detailed breakdown of direct, indirect, and hybrid channel models along with their trade-offs.

The core channel categories are:
- **Direct** — company sells straight to the customer (website, sales team, company stores)
- **Indirect** — third parties sell on the company's behalf (resellers, distributors, wholesalers, VARs, marketplaces)
- **Hybrid** — combination of direct and indirect for different segments or stages

For each candidate channel, evaluate:
1. **Buyer alignment** — Does this channel match how the target segment prefers to buy?
2. **Reach** — Can this channel access the geographic and demographic segments you're targeting?
3. **Economics** — What margins are required? Can you profit after channel costs?
4. **Control** — How much control do you retain over messaging, pricing, and customer experience?
5. **Scalability** — Can this channel grow with your business?
6. **Competitive dynamics** — What channels do competitors use? Is there differentiation opportunity?

### Step 4: Assess Distributor/Partner Value Proposition

If indirect channels are part of the strategy, the user needs to answer: Why would a partner want to carry this product?

Consider:
- Margin opportunity for the partner
- Complementary fit with the partner's existing portfolio
- Demand and brand awareness the manufacturer brings
- Training, marketing support, and enablement the manufacturer provides
- Exclusivity or preferential terms

### Step 5: Build the Distribution Strategy Document

Help the user create a formal document capturing their strategy. Use this structure:

```
DISTRIBUTION STRATEGY

1. Executive Summary
   - Target segments and their buying preferences
   - Recommended channel mix
   - Key rationale

2. Market Buying Behavior Analysis
   - How each target segment discovers, evaluates, and purchases
   - Data sources and research methods used
   - Key findings

3. Channel Strategy
   - Primary channel(s) and rationale
   - Secondary/supporting channels
   - Channel roles by segment (which channel serves which segment)

4. Channel Economics
   - Margin structure per channel
   - Volume assumptions
   - Profitability analysis

5. Partner Strategy (if applicable)
   - Ideal partner profile
   - Value proposition to partners
   - Partner enablement plan
   - Channel conflict mitigation

6. Implementation Plan
   - Phasing and priorities
   - Resource requirements
   - Key milestones

7. Success Metrics
   - Channel performance KPIs
   - Review cadence
```

### Step 6: Connect to Adjacent Framework Activities

After completing the distribution strategy, remind the user how it feeds into downstream activities:
- **Pricing** — channel margins affect pricing models
- **Marketing Plan** — channel-specific marketing tactics and co-marketing with partners
- **Launch** — channel readiness is a critical launch gate
- **Sales Alignment** — the sales process must align with the distribution model
- **Channel Training** (Enablement) — partners need training on how to sell the product

## Pragmatic Rule to Remember

Pragmatic Marketing Rule #11 states: **"Align your distribution strategy with personas and their problems."** This reinforces that distribution is not about what's easiest for the company — it's about matching how the market wants to buy with how you deliver.

## Common Pitfalls

When helping users, watch for and flag these mistakes:
- **Inside-out thinking** — choosing channels based on what the company already has rather than what buyers want
- **Ignoring channel conflict** — selling direct while also using partners without a conflict resolution plan
- **Skipping the economics** — selecting channels without understanding margin requirements
- **One-size-fits-all** — using the same channel for every segment when different segments buy differently
- **Neglecting partner enablement** — expecting channel partners to sell effectively without training, tools, and support
- **Treating distribution as a one-time decision** — the strategy needs regular review as markets and buying behaviors evolve

## Output Formats

Depending on the user's request, the output might be:
- A complete Distribution Strategy document (use the structure in Step 5)
- A channel evaluation matrix comparing options
- A partner value proposition one-pager
- A channel economics model or worksheet
- Advice and recommendations in conversation

For document creation, follow the appropriate file-creation skill (docx, pptx, xlsx) based on the user's preferred format.
