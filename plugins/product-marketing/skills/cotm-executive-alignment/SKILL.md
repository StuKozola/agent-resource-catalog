# Executive Alignment — Command of the Message, Step 2

A Command of the Message rollout succeeds or fails on leadership. If the CRO doesn't use the language, managers won't coach it; if marketing keeps its own messaging, buyers hear two stories. This step makes the message a leadership decision before it becomes a training program.

**Output:** a signed-off rollout charter, a change-management plan, and a scheduled (or completed) alignment workshop that produces the first-draft Value Framework.

## Workflow

### 1. Clarify the business case

Ask the user why the company is adopting CotM now. Tie it to a measurable problem, such as:

- Win rates falling or discounting rising against a specific competitor
- Long or stalled sales cycles
- Inconsistent messaging across reps, regions or products
- Slow ramp for new hires
- A move upmarket, a new product line, or a shift from product-led to sales-led growth
- Pressure from the board or investors for predictable revenue

Write the case in two or three sentences using the user's numbers where possible. It becomes the opening of the charter.

### 2. Identify the sponsor and the alignment group

- **Executive sponsor:** usually the CRO or CEO. They must attend the workshop, use the language in forecasts and all-hands, and hold managers accountable. A sponsor who delegates attendance is a red flag; say so.
- **Alignment group:** the leaders who own each part of the customer story. Typically sales leadership (including first-line manager representatives), marketing/product marketing, product management, sales engineering, customer success, services, and enablement. Add finance if pricing or deal desk changes are expected.
- **Field validators:** two to four top-performing reps and a sales engineer who will test the draft against real deals.

Keep the workshop group small enough to make decisions (roughly 8–15 people).

### 3. Draft the rollout charter

Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/command-of-the-message/cotm-executive-alignment/templates/rollout-charter.md`. It covers the business case, goals and target metrics, scope (teams, regions, products, segments), roles, timeline, decision rights for the Value Framework, and risks. Decision rights matter: name who owns the framework after the workshop (often product marketing with sales leadership) and how changes are approved.

### 4. Plan change management

Address the predictable sources of resistance:

- **Veteran reps** who see it as "another methodology." Involve top performers as validators and show how it codifies what they already do well.
- **Managers** who lack coaching time. Agree up front on a coaching cadence and remove other demands to make room.
- **Marketing and product** who own existing messaging. Make the Value Framework the source for positioning and content, not a competing document.
- **Fatigue from past initiatives.** Show what will be different: sponsor involvement, process integration and measurement.

Plan communications: a sponsor announcement, manager briefing before rep training, and a launch moment (often the sales kickoff).

### 5. Prepare the alignment workshop

Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/command-of-the-message/cotm-executive-alignment/templates/alignment-workshop-agenda.md`. Before the workshop:

- Gather pre-work: buyer personas, market problems, competitive analysis, win/loss findings, and three to five recent customer success stories with numbers.
- Ask each leader to bring their answer to the three essential questions (what problems we solve, how we solve them differently, where we've done it before). Differences between their answers are the agenda.
- Pull 5–10 call recordings or emails showing how the company currently describes its value.

### 6. Run the workshop and capture decisions

Facilitate toward a first-draft Value Framework (use `cotm-value-framework` for the structure). Record:

- Agreed buyer personas and use cases in scope
- Top business issues and the outcomes the company commits to
- Agreed differentiators and the proof each needs
- Open questions and owners, with dates
- Next steps for validation with customers and reps

### 7. Confirm commitment

End with each leader stating what their team will do differently and by when. Circulate the charter and workshop output within 48 hours with the sponsor's endorsement.

## Common mistakes

- Running the workshop without the sponsor in the room.
- Inviting only sales — the framework then becomes a sales document marketing ignores.
- Leaving with a vague framework and no owner.
- Announcing the methodology to reps before managers are briefed.

## Using AI in this step

- Summarize leaders' differing answers to the three essential questions into a gap list for the workshop.
- Draft the charter and communications from the user's notes.
- Analyze call transcripts to show how inconsistently value is currently described.

Leaders, not the tool, make the commitments. Keep confidential strategy and deal data in approved tools.
