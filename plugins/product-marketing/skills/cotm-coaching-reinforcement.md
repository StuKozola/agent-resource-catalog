# Coaching and Reinforcement — Command of the Message, Step 7

Most methodology rollouts fade within a quarter of training. What prevents fade is managers coaching to the framework every week, leaders using the language in every review, and the framework itself staying current. This step is permanent, not a phase.

**Output:** a manager coaching cadence, a call review scorecard, a reinforcement calendar and a framework refresh process.

## Workflow

### 1. Set the coaching cadence

Agree a minimum rhythm with sales leadership, for example:

| Activity | Frequency | Time | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call review (recorded or live) | Weekly per rep | 20–30 min | Manager and rep |
| Deal review using value questions | Weekly or biweekly | Per deal | Manager and rep |
| Team practice (role-play, mantra) | Biweekly | 30 min | Team |
| Win story share | Monthly | 15 min | Team |
| Skip-level review of coaching | Monthly | 30 min | Second-line leader and managers |

Protect the time: if managers are expected to coach, remove lower-value reporting or meetings.

### 2. Coach with a scorecard

Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/command-of-the-message/cotm-coaching-reinforcement/templates/call-review-scorecard.md` to review calls against the framework: did the rep establish the before state, quantify consequences, get the buyer to describe outcomes and metrics, use trap-setting questions, deliver relevant differentiation and proof, and secure a next step?

Focus each coaching session on one or two behaviors. Use the rep's own words from the recording. Ask before telling ("What did you learn about how they measure success?").

### 3. Coach in deal reviews

Deal reviews are the highest-leverage coaching moment because the stakes are real. Use the value-first format from `cotm-sales-process-integration` and end every review with one specific action that strengthens the buyer's value story.

### 4. Reinforce through leadership behavior

- Executives ask value questions in forecast calls and QBRs.
- Wins are announced with the before state, outcome and metric, not just the amount.
- Promotions and recognition reflect use of the methodology, not just quota attainment.

### 5. Run peer learning

Have top reps share recordings of strong discovery or trap-setting moments. Build a library of short clips by framework element. Win stories become new proof points.

### 6. Refresh the message

Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/command-of-the-message/cotm-coaching-reinforcement/templates/reinforcement-plan.md`. At least quarterly, and after launches, pricing changes or competitive shifts:

- Review win/loss findings (`pragmatic-win-loss-analysis`) and call trends for new before scenarios, objections and proof.
- Update the Value Framework (`cotm-value-framework`) through its change process.
- Update field tools, then run a short refresher and re-certify where the change is significant.

### 7. Watch for fade

Signals that adoption is slipping:

- CRM value fields empty or copy-pasted
- Deal reviews drifting back to dates and amounts first
- Calls opening with product demos
- Manager coaching sessions cancelled
- New hires not certified on schedule

When you see them, go back to the sponsor and managers first, not the reps.

## Common mistakes

- Coaching only on struggling deals or reps.
- Generic feedback ("good call") instead of framework-specific behavior.
- Updating the framework without updating training and tools.
- Managers who were never certified themselves.

## Using AI in this step

- Score call transcripts against the scorecard to help managers choose which calls to review.
- Surface trends across calls (common objections, skipped framework elements).
- Draft coaching notes for the manager to edit.

AI scoring supports manager judgment; it doesn't replace the coaching conversation. Be transparent with reps about how recordings are analyzed.
