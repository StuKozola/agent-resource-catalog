# Channel Support — Pragmatic Framework (2027)

## Where this fits in the 2027 Pragmatic Framework

- **Category:** Enablement — *Can the people around the product make it succeed?*
- **Focus-area question:** *Can our partners and channels position, sell and support it?*
- **Position:** Below the bar (product- and team-focused)
- **Other focus areas in this category:** Adoption, Content, Assets
- **Formerly:** Channels (Support category) and Channel Training (Enablement), merged

## What Channel Support Covers

Channel support equips **partners, resellers, service providers and adjacent teams** with the knowledge and materials they need to **position, sell, deliver and support** your product. It combines two kinds of work that used to be separate: **training** channels (initial and certification programs) and **ongoing support** (current market and solution information, deal help, communications and feedback).

### Three kinds of channel

Consider every channel your product moves through:

| Kind | Where | Examples | What they need beyond the basics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales channels** | Where customers buy | Direct sales, inside sales, resellers, VARs, distributors, marketplaces, referral partners | Positioning, competitive differentiation, buying-process navigation. **Teach them to sell, not to use.** |
| **Delivery channels** | How the product reaches customers | Systems integrators, implementation partners, OEMs, MSPs, app stores, internal rollout teams | Implementation and onboarding know-how, reference architectures, the adoption milestones customers should hit |
| **Usage channels** | Where customers work with the product | Support partners, managed service providers, platform/embedded partners, customer success and support teams, adjacent internal teams | User personas and workflows, troubleshooting, approved explanations, escalation paths |

**Every channel gets the same foundation:** the market, the buyer's problems and how the product solves them. Then add what that channel's job requires. The "sell, not use" principle still applies to sales channels — a sales channel that pitches features instead of problems breaks your positioning — but delivery and usage channels do need product how-to, anchored in the outcome the customer bought for.

### Boundaries
- **Distribution Strategy** (`pragmatic-distribution-strategy`, Focus) decides *which* channels to use. Channel Support makes the chosen channels effective.
- **Assets** (`pragmatic-assets`, Enablement) builds the tools; Channel Support adapts and delivers partner versions.
- **Organizational Alignment** (`pragmatic-organizational-alignment`, Go-to-Market) aligns internal teams; adjacent internal teams acting as a channel (e.g., a services or support org) are covered here when they need training and materials to deliver or support the product.

### Who does the work

Product marketing owns the message and the curriculum foundation. Partner/channel managers own relationships and programs. Product management and solutions engineering supply technical depth for delivery and usage channels. Enablement or L&D teams run logistics and learning platforms.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Map the Channel Landscape

List partners and adjacent teams by kind (sales, delivery, usage), type (reseller, SI, MSP, support partner…), tier, segments served and contribution. Note where one partner plays several roles. Ask: what's the current state (ad hoc or systematic)? Where do partners report the biggest gaps? Where do partner deals stall or partner-delivered customers struggle?

### Step 2: Assess Current Training and Information Gaps

- **Training audit** — inventory programs from the last 12 months and run the feature-vs-selling test on sales-channel modules: do they teach problems, personas, the buying process and competition, or features and specs? Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/references/channel-training-guide.md` → "Audit and Gap Analysis".
- **Information needs** — what market intelligence, solution information and opportunity support each channel kind needs. Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/references/information-needs.md`.
- **Field evidence** — partner surveys, ride-alongs, win/loss by channel, implementation and support outcomes.

### Step 3: Define Objectives and Audience Segments

Define what "ready" means per channel kind, for example:
- **Sales:** articulate the top 3 problems by persona, handle the top 5 objections, navigate the buying process.
- **Delivery:** implement to the agreed success criteria and get customers to first value on schedule.
- **Usage:** resolve common issues, explain the product consistently, escalate correctly.

Segment audiences (new vs. existing partners, technical vs. quota-carrying, partner managers) and set outcome measures, not just completion. Tie the calendar to launches. Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/references/channel-training-guide.md` → "Objectives and Segmentation".

### Step 4: Design the Curriculum and Support Model

**Curriculum** — a shared foundation (market problems, buyers, buyer journey, positioning, competitive landscape, assets) plus tracks per channel kind: advanced selling for sales channels; implementation and onboarding for delivery channels; user workflows, support and escalation for usage channels. Use role-play, labs and real win/loss and customer stories. Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/references/channel-training-guide.md` → "Curriculum Design" and "Channel Type Considerations"; use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/templates/channel-training-plan.md`.

**Ongoing support model** — how information reaches partners after training: self-service portal, cadenced communications (newsletters, update calls), on-demand help (partner desk, channel managers) and co-selling or co-delivery support. Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/references/delivery-mechanisms.md`. Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/templates/partner-communication-brief.md` for individual updates.

### Step 5: Launch, Deliver, Reinforce and Make It Repeatable

Pilot with trusted partners, then roll out. Align every launch with updated training and partner briefs. Set a reinforcement cadence (monthly tips, quarterly updates, annual refresh at partner kickoff). Incentivize through certification and tier benefits; use train-the-trainer for distributors. Turn recurring requests into reusable packages (release kits, competitive alerts, market summaries, deal-support workflows). Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/references/channel-training-guide.md` → "Delivery and Reinforcement".

### Step 6: Measure, Close the Loop and Optimize

- **Activity** — completion, certification, portal usage, time to answer partner requests
- **Capability** — partner confidence, selling behaviors observed (problems vs. features), implementation quality
- **Outcomes** — sales: partner-sourced pipeline, win rate, deal size, time to first deal; delivery: time to go-live, early adoption; usage: resolution time, customer satisfaction for partner-supported customers
- **Feedback loop** — route partner intelligence to Competitive Landscape, Win/Loss Analysis, Market Problems, Positioning, Assets, Requirements, Adoption, Customer Satisfaction and Escalations

Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/references/metrics-optimization.md`. Assemble everything in `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-channel-support/templates/channel-support-plan.md`.

## Internal products

For internal platforms, the "channels" are the teams that roll the platform out, support it and help others use it: internal IT or platform operations (delivery), help desks and team champions (usage), and the architects or leads who recommend it (sales). Give each the same problem-and-outcome foundation, then the rollout, support or advocacy material their role needs.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Feature training for sales channels** — if the agenda reads like a spec sheet, it's product training. Sales channels need problems, personas and the buying process.
2. **Ignoring delivery and usage channels** — partners who implement and support the product shape satisfaction and adoption as much as sellers shape pipeline.
3. **Treating partners like direct sales** — they sell multiple vendors and have limited mindshare; keep it concise and high-value.
4. **One-size-fits-all** — tier and tailor by channel kind, type and partner maturity.
5. **One-and-done training** — without reinforcement and ongoing information, readiness decays.
6. **Flooding the channel** — curate; less, better information wins.
7. **Reactive-only support** — waiting for partners to ask creates delays.
8. **No feedback loop** — partners hear the market daily; failing to capture it wastes NIHITO evidence.
9. **Measuring attendance, not outcomes** — certificates don't close deals or deliver go-lives.

## Key Pragmatic Principles

- **Help channels, not individual salespeople** (Pragmatic Rule #20) — build scalable programs, not one-off coaching.
- **Positioning focuses on the problems you solve** — anchor every module, for every channel, to a market problem.
- **Outside-in** — reflect how buyers buy and how customers use the product, not how your org is structured.
- **Your opinion, although interesting, is irrelevant** — teach validated problems, not internal assumptions.

## Using AI in this focus area

- Draft training modules, quizzes and role-play scenarios from approved positioning, personas and battle cards; generate channel-specific variants.
- Power a partner-facing assistant over approved portal content so partners get fast, consistent answers.
- Summarize partner feedback, deal debriefs and support tickets into themes for product and marketing.
- Rules: check AI output against real market evidence — partner interviews, field observation and win/loss still matter (NIHITO still applies). Keep sensitive customer, deal, pricing and roadmap data out of tools not approved for it, and be careful what partner-facing AI can reveal. The team, not the tool, owns the decisions. AI-generated training can drift into feature recitation; check modules against the sell-not-use test for sales channels.
