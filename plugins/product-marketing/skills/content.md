# Pragmatic Framework: Content

## Framework Context

Content is an **Enablement** activity in the Pragmatic Framework. Its official definition:

> **Develop relevant content to be used for all go-to-market channels and materials.**

Content sits at the intersection of strategy and execution. It translates upstream strategic work — positioning, personas, buyer experience — into tangible assets that move buyers through their journey and equip the sales organization to communicate value. Content is not simply "writing stuff." It is a disciplined practice of creating the right message, for the right persona, at the right stage of the buying process, in the right format, through the right channel.

### Key Responsibilities (per Pragmatic Institute)
- Provide content for buyer personas focused on a specific step of the buying process
- Curate content from product users or product teams that aligns with stages of the buying process and drives campaign objectives

### Required Skills
- Strong product writing and communications
- Agility to learn from marketing analytics and evolve content to meet product success metrics
- Understanding of the market and the issues faced

### Relationship to Other Framework Activities

Content does not exist in isolation. It depends on and feeds into many other boxes:

**Content draws FROM (upstream inputs):**
- **Positioning** — The foundational messaging pillars, value propositions, and problem statements that anchor all content
- **Buyer Personas** — The archetypical buyers whose language, concerns, and information needs shape content
- **Buyer Experience** — The documented buying process and journey stages that determine when and where content is needed
- **Use Scenarios** — Stories that put market problems in context, providing source material for content narratives
- **Competitive Landscape** — Strengths, weaknesses, and differentiation points that inform comparative content
- **Market Problems** — The urgent, pervasive problems validated through market research that content must address
- **Marketing Plan** — The strategic programs, campaigns, and goals that content must support

**Content feeds INTO (downstream consumers):**
- **Sales Alignment** — Content supports the aligned selling/buying process
- **Sales Tools** — Content is adapted into step-specific selling tools
- **Channel Training** — Content informs training on how to sell (not how to use) the product
- **Programs / Events / Operations / Channels** — All Support activities consume content

## Workflow

Follow these six steps when helping a user with Content work. Adapt based on where they are — some users need to start from scratch, others need to audit and fill gaps.

### Step 1: Validate Strategic Inputs

Before creating any content, confirm the user has (or can approximate) these upstream deliverables. Load `references/input-validation.md` for the detailed checklist.

**Must-have inputs:**
- Positioning document (or at minimum, a value proposition and key messaging pillars)
- Buyer personas (at least the primary buyer persona)
- Buyer experience / journey map (even a rough one)

**Valuable inputs:**
- Use scenarios
- Competitive landscape analysis
- Marketing plan with program priorities
- Win/loss insights

If inputs are missing, help the user create lightweight working versions. Content built without these foundations risks being inside-out (product-centric rather than market-driven).

### Step 2: Audit Existing Content

Help the user inventory what content already exists and evaluate its quality and coverage. Load `references/content-audit.md` for the audit methodology.

The audit answers three questions:
1. **What do we have?** — Inventory all existing content assets
2. **How well does it perform?** — Assess quality, relevance, accuracy, and engagement
3. **Where are the gaps?** — Map existing content against personas × journey stages to find holes

The primary output is a content matrix showing coverage across personas and buying stages, with gap identification.

### Step 3: Map Content to Buyer Journey

This is the core of the Content activity. For each persona, determine what content is needed at each stage of their buying journey. Load `references/content-mapping.md` for the detailed framework.

The content map answers: "What does [persona] need to know, believe, or feel at [stage] to move forward?"

**Journey stages** (adapt to user's specific buyer experience work):
- **Awareness** — Buyer recognizes they have a problem
- **Education** — Buyer researches the problem space and possible approaches
- **Selection** — Buyer evaluates specific solutions and vendors
- **Purchase** — Buyer makes the final decision and completes procurement
- **Loyalty** — Buyer becomes an advocate or expands usage

For each cell in the persona × stage matrix, define: the buyer's key questions, the content objective, recommended formats, distribution channels, and success metrics.

### Step 4: Develop Content Briefs

For each piece of content to be created (especially high-priority gap fills), develop a content brief that ensures alignment with strategy. Load `templates/content-brief.md` for the template.

A content brief connects each asset back to:
- The persona it serves
- The journey stage it addresses
- The positioning pillar it reinforces
- The buyer question it answers
- The action it should drive

### Step 5: Build the Content Calendar and Production Plan

Organize content creation into a prioritized, time-sequenced plan. Load `templates/content-plan.md` for the planning template.

Prioritization criteria:
1. **Gap severity** — High-traffic journey stages with no content come first
2. **Program alignment** — Content needed for upcoming campaigns and launches
3. **Reuse potential** — Assets that can be repurposed across multiple channels or personas
4. **Resource availability** — Balance ambition with realistic production capacity

### Step 6: Establish Measurement and Iteration

Define how content performance will be measured and how the content library will evolve. Load `references/content-measurement.md` for the metrics framework.

Content measurement operates at three levels:
1. **Asset-level** — How does each piece perform? (engagement, consumption, shares)
2. **Journey-level** — How well does content move buyers between stages? (conversion, progression)
3. **Business-level** — How does content contribute to pipeline and revenue? (influenced pipeline, content-attributed revenue)

## Output Formats

Depending on the user's needs, the primary deliverables include:

- **Content Audit Report** — Inventory of existing assets with quality scores and gap analysis
- **Content Map / Matrix** — Persona × journey stage grid showing content needs, existing assets, and gaps
- **Content Brief** — Individual asset specification connecting strategy to execution
- **Content Calendar / Production Plan** — Prioritized, time-sequenced creation schedule
- **Content Performance Dashboard** — Metrics framework for ongoing measurement

## Common Pitfalls

Watch for these and coach the user away from them:

1. **Inside-out content** — Writing about product features rather than buyer problems. Content should lead with the market problem and only introduce the product as the solution. Pragmatic's outside-in philosophy demands starting from the buyer's world.

2. **Persona-blind content** — Creating generic content that doesn't speak to any specific buyer. Every piece should be traceable to at least one persona and one journey stage.

3. **Stage-blind content** — Creating content without considering where the buyer is in their journey. An awareness-stage buyer needs different content than a selection-stage buyer.

4. **Content without positioning roots** — Content that drifts from established positioning pillars creates inconsistent messaging. Every asset should reinforce the core value proposition.

5. **Volume over value** — Prioritizing content quantity over quality and strategic alignment. A small library of well-targeted, high-quality assets outperforms a large library of unfocused content.

6. **Create-and-forget** — Failing to measure content performance and iterate. Content must evolve based on analytics and changing market conditions.

7. **Sales disconnect** — Creating content that marketing loves but sales never uses. Content in the Enablement category must serve the sales process, not just marketing campaigns.

8. **Channel confusion** — Distributing content through channels the persona doesn't use. Match format and channel to persona preferences discovered through buyer experience research.

## Adaptive Handling

- **If the user has strong upstream work** (positioning, personas, buyer experience): Jump to Step 2 (audit) or Step 3 (mapping) depending on whether they have existing content.
- **If the user is starting from scratch**: Begin with Step 1 and help them create lightweight versions of missing inputs before proceeding.
- **If the user wants a single content brief**: Skip directly to Step 4, but verify they can articulate the target persona and journey stage.
- **If the user wants to fix underperforming content**: Start with Step 2 (audit) with emphasis on performance assessment, then use the mapping framework to identify misalignment.
- **If the user is focused on sales enablement content**: Emphasize the Selection and Purchase stages, and pay particular attention to the Sales Alignment and Sales Tools connections.
