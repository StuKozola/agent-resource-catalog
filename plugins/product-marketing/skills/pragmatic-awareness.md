# Awareness — Pragmatic Framework (2027)

## Where this fits in the 2027 Pragmatic Framework

- **Category:** Go-to-Market — *How will the market find, choose and stay with the product?*
- **Focus-area question:** *Does the right audience know the problem and our solution?*
- **Position:** Below the bar (product- and team-focused)
- **Other focus areas in this category:** Marketing Plan, Growth, Retention, Launch, Organizational Alignment, Engagement, Customer Satisfaction
- **Formerly:** Awareness (Programs category); also absorbs the retired Events focus area

## What This Skill Does

Awareness is the first step in the buying process. People can't choose your product if they don't know it exists, so this focus area helps the right audiences recognize three things: **the problem** they have, **your company** as one that understands it, and **your solution** as a credible way to solve it. Marketing and communications teams usually lead, product marketers contribute market and buyer insight, and the whole organization shares responsibility for how the company shows up in the market.

The output is an awareness program plan that connects market knowledge (positioning, buyer personas, market problems, competitive landscape) to execution — content, thought leadership, PR and analyst relations, advertising, community and events — so your company is the first one prospects think of when they recognize a need.

## Framework Context

Awareness brings people into view of the product; Engagement (`pragmatic-engagement`, Go-to-Market) moves them from interest to evaluation, trial and early use. Both sit under the Marketing Plan.

**Key inputs:**
- **Positioning** (`pragmatic-positioning`, Planning) — messaging pillars and problem-solution framing
- **Buyer Personas** (`pragmatic-buyer-personas`, Planning) — who to reach, their roles, watering holes and information preferences
- **Buyer Journey** (`pragmatic-buyer-journey`, Planning) — triggers and early stages where awareness work has to operate
- **Market Problems** (`pragmatic-market-problems`, Market) — the validated problems awareness content should name
- **Market Definition** (`pragmatic-market-definition`, Focus) — the segments where awareness investment should concentrate
- **Competitive Landscape** (`pragmatic-competitive-landscape`, Market) — share-of-voice baseline and differentiation openings
- **Marketing Plan** (`pragmatic-marketing-plan`, Go-to-Market) — budget, goals and calendar for awareness programs

**Key downstream connections:**
- **Engagement** (`pragmatic-engagement`, Go-to-Market) — picks up people once they show active interest
- **Measurement** (`pragmatic-measurement`, Business) — sets the leading and lagging indicators for awareness work
- **Growth** (`pragmatic-growth`, Go-to-Market) — awareness feeds the pipeline growth plans depend on
- **Content** (`pragmatic-content`, Enablement) — awareness programs generate and consume content
- **Launch** (`pragmatic-launch`, Go-to-Market) — launches often use awareness programs and events as vehicles

## Roles and Responsibilities

- **Marketing and communications** usually lead: brand, PR, advertising, social, events.
- **Product marketers** supply the market problem framing, persona insight, positioning and competitive context, and often present or write thought leadership.
- **Everyone else** (executives, product, sales, customer success) shares responsibility: speaking at events, posting, talking to analysts and press, and staying on message.

Useful skills: written and verbal communication, storytelling, presenting, credibility with the market, and a strong understanding of the industry and its problems.

## Workflow

Follow these six steps to build an awareness program plan. Load reference files as indicated.

### Step 1: Audit Strategic Inputs
> Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/input-audit.md`.

Check that positioning, buyer personas, market definition, buyer journey and competitive landscape exist and are current. Identify gaps and decide whether to proceed with stated assumptions or pause for upstream work.

### Step 2: Define Awareness Objectives and Audience
> Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/objectives-audience.md`.

Decide which kind of awareness you need — of the problem, of your company, of your solution — and how you'll judge it (unaided recall, aided recognition, share of voice, category association, thought leadership authority). Set measurable objectives tied to business outcomes. Map target audiences by segment and persona, with the channels and watering holes where they learn.

### Step 3: Develop the Awareness Strategy
> Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/awareness-strategy.md`.

Choose the awareness model (problem-led, brand-led or thought-leadership-led), derive content pillars from positioning, select program types (content, PR and analyst relations, advertising, events, social, community) and set the messaging hierarchy for awareness-stage communication.

### Step 4: Design the Program Portfolio
> Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/program-portfolio.md`.

Turn the strategy into concrete programs, each with objectives, target personas, channels, content needs, timing and budget. Balance always-on programs (SEO, content hub, social presence) with campaigns (launches, events, seasonal pushes), across owned, earned and paid channels, and map each to buyer journey stages.

### Step 5: Establish Measurement
> Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/measurement.md`.

Measure at three levels: activity (what you did), engagement (how the market responded) and outcome (business impact). Include leading indicators (reach, share of voice) and lagging ones (unaided recall, pipeline contribution). Set up the loop from results back to program changes.

### Step 6: Assemble and Review
> Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/templates/awareness-program-plan.md` and `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/templates/program-brief.md`.

Compile the plan, check it against the pitfalls below, confirm alignment with the Marketing Plan and Positioning, and get stakeholder buy-in. Write a program brief for each major initiative.

## Events (formerly the Events focus area)

Events — conferences, trade shows, webinars, seminars, executive roundtables, user conferences — are one of the main ways awareness work reaches people, and they also serve Engagement (evaluators at a booth, prospects at a roadshow, customers at a user conference). The product team's job is to bring market expertise to events that marketing or event teams usually plan and run, so every touchpoint communicates market problems and positioning rather than a feature pitch.

**Event workflow:**
1. **Assess context and objectives** — event type, your role (sponsor, exhibitor, speaker, host), expected personas, and the objective (awareness, pipeline, thought leadership, customer engagement, launch support). Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/event-types.md`.
2. **Align content with positioning and personas** — pick the problems and messages that fit this audience, prepare competitive talking points, and define the one thing attendees should remember. Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/event-content-alignment.md`.
3. **Develop support materials** — event briefing, speaker prep, demo scripts tied to market problems, booth conversation guides, abstracts, leave-behinds. Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/event-deliverables-guide.md`, `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/templates/event-briefing.md` and `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/templates/speaker-prep.md`.
4. **Prepare participants** — brief booth staff and speakers, rehearse demos against problems, agree lead qualification against personas, and set rules on what not to disclose (roadmap, pricing).
5. **Support execution and listen** — act as the subject matter expert, and capture market and competitive intelligence from conversations (NIHITO: Nothing Important Happens In The Office).
6. **Debrief and measure** — run a structured debrief, feed intelligence back to the product team, review lead quality and event ROI. Load `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StuKozola/agent-resource-catalog/main/skills/pragmatic-framework/pragmatic-awareness/references/event-measurement.md`.

Hand qualified event leads to Engagement programs (`pragmatic-engagement`); route customer stories and reference candidates to Customer Satisfaction (`pragmatic-customer-satisfaction`) and case-study work to Assets (`pragmatic-assets`).

## Internal products

For internal platforms and tools, awareness means making sure the teams who have the problem know the platform exists and what it solves. Typical programs: internal launch announcements, demos at all-hands or engineering forums, internal docs portals, office hours and champions in each team. Measure recognition and inbound interest from target teams rather than share of voice.

## Output Formats

1. **Awareness Program Plan** — objectives, audience, strategy, program portfolio, measurement and calendar
2. **Program Briefs** — tactical briefs for each initiative
3. **Awareness Audit** — current awareness posture and gaps
4. **Measurement Dashboard Specification** — KPI definitions, data sources, cadence
5. **Event Briefing Document** and **Speaker Preparation Kit**
6. **Event Debrief Report** and **Event Portfolio Assessment**

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Inside-out messaging** — leading with features instead of the problems the audience cares about
2. **Company awareness without problem awareness** — people know your name but not what problem you solve
3. **Segment-blind programs** — generic campaigns instead of targeting the segments chosen in Market Definition
4. **Persona-deaf content** — content that doesn't match how specific personas consume information
5. **Activity over outcome** — reporting what you produced rather than what the market did in response
6. **Blurring awareness and engagement** — programs that try to build visibility and convert evaluators at once, and do neither well
7. **Channel-first thinking** — "we need a LinkedIn strategy" instead of "where do our personas learn about problems like ours?"
8. **Ignoring the 95-5 rule** — focusing only on the ~5% of buyers in-market now and neglecting the 95% who will buy later
9. **No competitive context** — measuring awareness without share of voice relative to competitors
10. **Events as one-way broadcast** — no event briefing, no debrief, and no market intelligence captured

## Using AI in this focus area

- Draft variations of awareness content (articles, social posts, abstracts) from approved positioning and persona documents.
- Summarize social listening, press coverage and share-of-voice data, and cluster themes in market conversations.
- Prepare event briefings and speaker prep drafts, and summarize debrief notes into intelligence reports.
- Rules: check AI output against real market evidence — AI can't tell you what your audience actually recalls, so keep surveying and talking to the market (NIHITO still applies). Keep sensitive customer, deal and company data (unreleased roadmap, pricing) out of tools not approved for it. The team, not the tool, owns the decisions. Watch for generic, look-alike AI copy that weakens distinct positioning.
